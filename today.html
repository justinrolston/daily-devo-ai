[Christ Our Advocate] My little children, I am writing these things to you so that you may not sin. But if anyone does sin, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous.
1 John 2:1 (ESV)

Our Perfect Advocate

Reflection

What does this text teach us? John writes to believers with the heart of a loving father, addressing them as “my little children.” His first desire is clear: that Christians would not sin. This isn’t mere moral advice—it’s a call to live in the light of who we are in Christ. Sin damages our fellowship with God, grieves the Holy Spirit, and harms our witness to the world.

Yet John immediately acknowledges a sobering reality: believers do sin. This isn’t pessimism but biblical realism about our ongoing struggle with indwelling sin. Even as regenerated people, we still battle the flesh, still stumble, and still need grace daily. John doesn’t minimize sin or offer cheap grace, but he also doesn’t leave us in despair.

Here’s the glorious truth: “we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous.” The word “advocate” means one who stands beside us, pleading our case. When Satan accuses us before God’s throne, pointing to our failures and shortcomings, Jesus stands as our defense attorney. But unlike earthly advocates, He doesn’t argue that we’re innocent. Instead, He points to His own perfect righteousness and finished work on the cross.

Notice that Jesus is called “the righteous.” This isn’t just describing His character—it’s highlighting why He can advocate for us. He lived the perfect life we couldn’t live and died the death we deserved to die. His righteousness becomes the ground of our acceptance with God. When the Father looks at us, He sees His Son’s perfect record credited to our account.

This advocacy isn’t a one-time event but an ongoing ministry. Right now, as you read these words, if you’re in Christ, Jesus is interceding for you. Every moment of weakness, every failure, every struggle—He knows them all and continually presents His sacrifice on your behalf.

This truth should both humble and encourage us. Humble us because we need constant advocacy—we’re not self-sufficient moral achievers. Encourage us because our standing with God doesn’t depend on our performance but on Christ’s perfect work. We can confess sin boldly, knowing we won’t be rejected. We can approach God’s throne with confidence, not because we’re worthy, but because our Advocate has made us so.

Confessional Tie-in

“Christ, by his obedience and death, did fully discharge the debt of all those that are thus justified, and did make a proper, real and full satisfaction to his Father’s justice on their behalf.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter XI, Article 3

Prayer Prompt

Lord Jesus, thank You for being my perfect Advocate, standing before the Father on my behalf. Help me rest in Your righteousness rather than my own performance, and grant me grace to pursue holiness out of gratitude for Your finished work.

Go Deeper

Consider keeping a brief journal this week of moments when you’re tempted to hide from God because of sin—then remember your Advocate and approach Him boldly instead. Meditate on what it means that Christ’s advocacy is based not on your worthiness but on His perfect righteousness.
