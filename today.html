Wait for the LORD; be strong, and let your heart take courage; wait for the LORD!
Psalm 27:14 (ESV)

Strength for the Waiting

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? David wrote these words while surrounded by enemies, longing to see God’s face, fearing abandonment even by his own parents (Psalm 27:10). Yet in the very same breath, he commands his own soul—and ours—to wait for the Lord. Notice the repetition: this verse begins and ends with the same charge. Waiting is not incidental to faith; it is central to it.

This is not passive waiting, the kind we do in a checkout line, disengaged and impatient. The Hebrew word carries the sense of hopeful expectation, like a watchman straining for the dawn. David couples this waiting with strength and courage—”be strong, and let your heart take courage.” This is active trust, a settled confidence that God will act even when His timing differs from ours.

Throughout Scripture, waiting is woven into the fabric of redemptive history. Abraham waited decades for a promised son. Israel waited centuries for a deliverer. The exiles waited for restoration. And believers today wait for the full consummation of Christ’s kingdom. We live between the “already” of Christ’s finished work and the “not yet” of His return—a season defined by faithful waiting.

Here is the comfort: our courage in waiting is not generated from within ourselves. Left to our own resources, we are prone to despair, self-reliance, or impatient grasping for control. But God strengthens the hearts of His people. The courage David calls for is itself a gift of grace, sustained by the character of the One we wait for—a covenant-keeping, faithful God who has never abandoned His own.

Perhaps you are waiting today—for healing, for reconciliation, for clarity, for relief from a long trial. The command to wait is not God’s way of withholding Himself from you; it is an invitation to know Him more deeply in the waiting itself. Your strength will not come from the resolution of your circumstances but from fixing your heart on the Lord who holds them.

Confessional Tie-in:

“God hath endued the will of man with that natural liberty, that is neither forced, nor by any absolute necessity of nature determined to good or evil… the almighty power of God [enables believers] to persevere therein to the end, and be eternally saved.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter IX & XVII (on perseverance of the saints)

Prayer Prompt:

Lord, You are faithful even when my circumstances feel uncertain and my patience grows thin. Forgive me for the times I have sought control instead of trusting Your timing. Strengthen my heart today to wait on You with hope, not dread.

Go Deeper:

Consider one area of your life where waiting feels especially hard right now. Ask yourself: Is my courage rooted in outcomes I cannot control, or in the character of God I can always trust? Spend time this week reading Psalm 27 in full, noting how David moves from fear to confident trust—and let his prayer become your own.
