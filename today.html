November 11, 2026

[Do Not Love the World] Do not love the world or the things in the world. If anyone loves the world, the love of the Father is not in him. For all that is in the world—the desires of the flesh and the desires of the eyes and pride of life—is not from the Father but is from the world.
1 John 2:15-16 (ESV)

A Love That Cannot Share the Throne

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? John writes to believers—people already in the family of God—with a startling command: do not love the world. This isn’t a call to despise creation itself, which God made good, nor to withdraw from people in need of the gospel. Rather, John addresses the world system—a way of life organized around rebellion against God, chasing temporary pleasures as if they were ultimate treasures.

John identifies three specific expressions of this worldly love: the desires of the flesh (craving physical gratification beyond God’s boundaries), the desires of the eyes (covetousness, the restless hunger for what we see and don’t have), and the pride of life (the boastful confidence in our own status, achievements, and self-sufficiency). These three temptations are ancient—they echo the garden, where Eve saw that the fruit was good for food (flesh), a delight to the eyes (eyes), and desirable for gaining wisdom (pride). They are the same temptations Satan offered Christ in the wilderness, and the same ones that confront us in a thousand daily forms.

Here is the sobering truth: love for the world and love for the Father cannot coexist in the same heart. This isn’t about perfection—John writes elsewhere that we all stumble and need continual cleansing through Christ’s blood. But it reveals where our deepest allegiance lies. Our hearts were made for worship, and they will love something ultimately. The question is never whether we’ll worship, but whom.

This is why the gospel matters so much here. We cannot manufacture love for the Father through sheer willpower or guilt-driven effort. Only as the Spirit opens our eyes to see Christ’s surpassing worth—His beauty, His sacrifice, His sufficiency—does our love for fleeting things begin to lose its grip. Sanctification isn’t white-knuckled resistance to worldliness; it’s falling more deeply in love with the One who gave Himself for us, until lesser loves grow dim by comparison.

Confessional Tie-in:

“Q. What is the chief end of man? A. Man’s chief end is to glorify God, and to enjoy him forever.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Q.1

Prayer Prompt:

Father, I confess that my heart too often chases what the world offers—comfort, recognition, approval—rather than finding its rest in You. Thank You that Christ perfected love and righteousness is credited to me, not earned by my fickle affections. Spirit, awaken fresh desire for the Father in me today, that lesser loves would lose their hold.

Go Deeper:

Take time today to honestly name one area where “the desires of the flesh,” “the desires of the eyes,” or “pride of life” has quietly captured your affections. Bring it to God not with shame alone, but with confidence in Christ’s finished work, asking the Spirit to replace that desire with deeper delight in Him. Consider meditating on Psalm 73:25—”Whom have I in heaven but you? And there is nothing on earth that I desire besides you.”
