The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom, and the knowledge of the Holy One is insight.
Proverbs 9:10 (ESV)

Where True Wisdom Begins

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Solomon, the wisest man to ever live apart from Christ, makes a startling claim: wisdom doesn’t begin with intelligence, education, or experience—it begins with the fear of the LORD. This isn’t a cowering, anxious dread, but a reverent awe that recognizes God for who He truly is: holy, sovereign, all-knowing, and worthy of our complete submission. It’s the posture of a creature before its Creator, rightly understanding the vast gap between divine majesty and human frailty.

Notice the structure of the verse: fear is the beginning, and knowledge of the Holy One is insight. These aren’t separate paths but one integrated reality. You cannot truly know God without reverencing Him, and you cannot genuinely fear Him without knowing Him more deeply. This challenges our modern assumption that wisdom is primarily about accumulating information. Solomon insists that all true understanding flows from a right relationship with God first.

This matters profoundly for daily life. We live in a culture that prizes autonomous reasoning—figuring things out ourselves, trusting our own judgment above all. But Scripture teaches that apart from a right relationship with God, our hearts default toward self-deception and folly (Jeremiah 17:9). We are not neutral, objective observers of truth; we need a transformed heart to perceive reality rightly.

This is why the gospel is essential to true wisdom. We cannot manufacture reverent fear through sheer effort—our hearts are too bent toward self-reliance. But in Christ, who is “the wisdom of God” (1 Corinthians 1:24), we receive new hearts that genuinely delight in God’s holiness rather than merely tolerating it. The fear of the Lord isn’t the price we pay for wisdom; it’s the gift we receive through union with Christ, who perfectly feared and honored His Father on our behalf.

So wisdom isn’t ultimately about clever strategies for successful living. It’s about knowing God, treasuring Him above all things, and letting that relationship reshape every decision, relationship, and ambition you have.

Confessional Tie-in:

“Man’s chief end is to glorify God, and to enjoy him forever.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Q.1

Prayer Prompt:

Holy God, I confess that I often seek wisdom in my own understanding rather than in reverent submission to You. Thank You for sending Christ, who perfectly feared and honored You, and whose righteousness is credited to me. Teach me today to treasure You above my own cleverness, and let that awe shape every choice I make.

Go Deeper:

Consider one decision you’re facing this week—financial, relational, or vocational. Before consulting your own reasoning, pause and ask: “What does reverence for God’s holiness require here?” For further meditation, read Job 28:12-28, where Job explores humanity’s inability to locate wisdom apart from God, concluding with this same truth.
