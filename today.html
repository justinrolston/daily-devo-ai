Bear one another's burdens, and so fulfill the law of Christ.
Galatians 6:2 (ESV)

Carrying What We Cannot Carry Alone

Title: Carrying What We Cannot Carry Alone

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Paul writes to churches in Galatia caught in conflict and confusion, and in the middle of his instructions about walking by the Spirit, he gives a strikingly simple command: carry each other’s burdens. The Greek word for “burden” here describes a heavy weight—something crushing, something too much for one person to bear alone. Paul isn’t talking about everyday inconveniences but the genuine weights of life: grief, chronic sin struggles, financial hardship, illness, doubt, and despair.

Notice what Paul calls this mutual burden-bearing: it fulfills the law of Christ. This is striking language. The law of Christ isn’t a new list of rules—it’s the law of love that Jesus himself embodied and taught, summarized in loving God and neighbor. Christ himself bore the ultimate burden on our behalf. He carried the full weight of sin, judgment, and death that we could never carry ourselves. Our burden-bearing for one another, then, isn’t merely nice behavior—it’s a reflection of the gospel we’ve received. We who have been carried by Christ now carry others.

This truth cuts against two modern temptations. The first is isolation—the lie that we should handle our struggles privately, that needing help is weakness. The second is independence as a virtue—the cultural myth of the self-made, self-sufficient individual. Scripture paints a different picture of the Christian life: we are members of one body, knit together, genuinely needing each other.

This is costly. Burden-bearing takes time, humility, and often discomfort. It means entering someone else’s pain, extending patience to those whose struggles are slow to heal, and being honest about our own weaknesses so others can bear ours too. It is sanctification in action—the Spirit forming Christlike love in us through the ordinary, unglamorous work of showing up for one another.

Who in your life is carrying a weight too heavy to bear alone? And where might you need to let someone help carry yours?

Confessional Tie-in:

“The saints by profession are bound to maintain an holy fellowship and communion in the worship of God, and in performing such other spiritual services as tend to their mutual edification; as also in relieving each other in outward things, according to their several abilities and necessities.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter 26.2

Prayer Prompt:

Lord, thank You for carrying the burden of my sin that I could never bear myself. Forgive me for the times I’ve isolated myself in pride or ignored the struggles of others in busyness. Give me eyes to see who needs help today, and the humility to let others help carry me.

Go Deeper:

This week, reach out to one person and ask a question deeper than “How are you?”—something like “What’s been weighing on you lately?” Then simply listen and pray with them. Consider also: is there a burden you’ve been carrying silently that it’s time to share with a trusted brother or sister in Christ?
