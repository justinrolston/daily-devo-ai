November 10, 2026

God thunders wondrously with his voice; he does great things that we cannot comprehend. For to the snow he says, ‘Fall on the earth,’ likewise to the downpour, his mighty downpour.
Job 37:5-6 (ESV)

The Voice That Commands the Storm

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Elihu, in his final speech before God addresses Job out of the whirlwind, points to something every person has witnessed but few have truly considered: the weather. Thunder rolls, snow falls, rain pours—and Elihu insists these are not impersonal forces but the direct speech and action of God. “To the snow he says, ‘Fall on the earth.’” God doesn’t merely permit weather; he commands it, and it obeys instantly and completely.

This should arrest us. We live in an age that explains thunderstorms through meteorology and feels we’ve therefore explained them away. But Scripture never pits secondary causes against God’s sovereign hand. The snow falls because God tells it to fall. Every flake, every drop, every roll of thunder is personal communication from the Creator—”great things that we cannot comprehend.” There is a holy mystery here that resists our mastery. We can predict weather patterns, but we cannot command them. Only God speaks and the snow listens.

Why does this matter for daily life? Because most of our anxiety comes from believing the world runs on autopilot—or worse, on chaos. If storms are random, then so is sickness, so is loss, so is delay. But if God thunders wondrously, if he directs even the downpour, then nothing in your life is untethered from his purposeful hand. The same voice that commands the snow governs your circumstances. This is not fatalism; it’s the foundation for trust. The God who speaks to weather patterns also speaks through his Word to shepherd his people, and his ultimate Word is Christ himself—through whom all things, including every storm you’ve weathered, were created and are sustained.

Do you feel small before forces you cannot control? You are. But you are not forgotten. The God of the blizzard and the thunderclap is the same God who sent his Son to die for sinners and who numbers the hairs on your head.

Confessional Tie-in:

“God the great Creator of all things doth uphold, direct, dispose, and govern all creatures, actions, and things, from the greatest even to the least, by his most wise and holy providence.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter V.1

Prayer Prompt:

Father, you spoke the world into being and you still speak to it—command the snow, send the rain, roll the thunder. Forgive me for the moments I’ve treated your providence as distant or mechanical rather than personal and purposeful. Thank you that the voice which governs galaxies is the same voice that calls me your child; help me rest in your sovereign care today.

Go Deeper:

The next time you encounter weather—whether sunshine or storm—pause and remember it is not random but spoken into being by God. Read Psalm 29 alongside this passage and notice how both texts link God’s voice to his power over creation. Ask yourself: where in my life have I been functioning as though God is absent from the “ordinary” workings of the world?
