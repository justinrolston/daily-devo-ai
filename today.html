All Scripture is breathed out by God and profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for training in righteousness, that the man of God may be complete, equipped for every good work.
2 Timothy 3:16-17 (ESV)

Breathed Out, Written Down, Lived Out

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Paul writes these words to Timothy, a young pastor facing false teachers and a culture hostile to the gospel. His charge is striking: Scripture is not merely inspired in the sense of being inspiring—it is theopneustos, literally “God-breathed.” Every word of the Bible originates from the very breath of God himself. This isn’t a book about God; it is God speaking.

Notice the four purposes Paul lists: teaching, reproof, correction, and training in righteousness. Scripture tells us what is true, exposes what is false, redirects us when we’ve gone astray, and disciplines us toward godliness. This is not a haphazard list—it describes the whole process of spiritual growth. We need truth to build on, conviction to tear down our false confidence, correction to set us back on the path, and ongoing training to keep walking in it.

Why does this matter so much? Because we are, by nature, people who drift. Our hearts are prone to wander, our minds easily deceived, our consciences dulled by sin. Left to ourselves, we would construct a god in our own image and a righteousness of our own making. But God, in his mercy, has not left us to our own devices. He has spoken clearly, sufficiently, and authoritatively so that his people would be “complete, equipped for every good work.”

This completeness isn’t found in supplementing Scripture with the latest spiritual trend or human philosophy. It’s found in returning, again and again, to the Word that breathes with the very life of God. The Spirit who inspired these words is the same Spirit who illumines them in our hearts today, applying Christ’s finished work to shape us into his likeness.

Are you equipped for the good works God has prepared for you? That equipping doesn’t come through willpower or self-help. It comes through sitting under the Word—reading it, hearing it preached, letting it reprove and correct you, trusting it to train you in righteousness you could never produce on your own.

Confessional Tie-in:

“The whole counsel of God concerning all things necessary for his own glory, man’s salvation, faith and life, is either expressly set down in Scripture, or by good and necessary consequence may be deduced from Scripture.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, I.VI

Prayer Prompt:

Father, thank you for breathing out your Word so that we might know you truly and live rightly before you. Forgive us for the times we’ve neglected Scripture or sought wisdom elsewhere. Equip us today through your Word for the good works you’ve prepared for us to walk in.

Go Deeper:

Set aside time this week to read a passage of Scripture slowly, asking: What does this teach me? Where does it reprove me? How does it correct my thinking? How might it train me toward righteousness? Consider memorizing this passage as a reminder of why you open your Bible each day.
