I delight to do your will, O my God; your law is within my heart.”
Psalm 40:8 (ESV)

When Obedience Becomes Delight

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? David declares something remarkable: obedience to God is not merely his duty but his delight. This psalm was likely written after deliverance from great distress, and in response, David doesn’t just offer sacrifices—he offers himself. He says God’s law isn’t an external burden chained to his wrists; it’s written within him, internalized, part of his very identity.

This is extraordinary language for someone living under the Old Covenant, awaiting the fuller work of the Spirit. Yet it points forward to something greater. Hebrews 10 quotes this very psalm and applies it to Christ himself, who came saying, “Behold, I have come to do your will, O God.” Jesus is the true and better David—the One whose entire life was perfect delight in the Father’s will, even unto death on a cross. He fulfilled the law perfectly, not grudgingly, but joyfully, for the joy set before him.

Here’s the beautiful exchange: because Christ delighted perfectly in God’s will on our behalf, and because his righteousness is credited to us by faith, we are now free to grow in genuine delight ourselves—not to earn favor, but because we already possess it. The Spirit takes God’s law and writes it on hearts of stone, turning them to flesh (Ezekiel 36:26). This is the New Covenant promise realized in every believer.

Be honest with yourself: does obedience often feel like drudgery? That’s not uncommon—it reflects our ongoing battle with sin, not a reason for despair. Sanctification is slow, Spirit-wrought work. The question isn’t whether you perfectly delight in God’s will today, but whether you’re being transformed, little by little, to love what God loves. Where the law once felt like an enemy demanding what we couldn’t give, in Christ it becomes a trusted friend showing us how to walk in freedom.

Confessional Tie-in:

“What is sanctification? Sanctification is a work of God’s free grace, whereby we are renewed in the whole man after the image of God, and are enabled more and more to die unto sin, and live unto righteousness.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Q. 35

Prayer Prompt:

Father, thank you for Christ, who delighted perfectly in your will when I could not. Forgive me for the moments when obedience feels burdensome rather than beautiful, and by your Spirit, write your law deeper into my heart today. Grant me grace to love what you love, not from fear, but from gratitude.

Go Deeper:

Read Hebrews 10:5-10 this week and notice how the writer applies this psalm directly to Christ’s incarnation and sacrifice. Ask yourself: What specific command of God currently feels like duty rather than delight? Bring that honestly to God, asking him to transform your heart, not just your behavior.
