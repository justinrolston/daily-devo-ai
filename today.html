And if it is evil in your eyes to serve the LORD, choose this day whom you will serve, whether the gods your fathers served in the region beyond the River, or the gods of the Amorites in whose land you dwell. But as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD.”
Joshua 24:15 (ESV)

A House Undivided: Choosing Whom We Will Serve

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Joshua stands before Israel at the end of his life, having led them through the conquest of the promised land. He gathers the tribes at Shechem and reminds them of God’s faithfulness—from Abraham’s calling to their deliverance from Egypt to this very moment of inheritance. Then he issues a challenge: choose this day whom you will serve. The options were not abstract. Israel lived among real idols—gods their ancestors worshiped in Mesopotamia, gods of the Amorites surrounding them in Canaan. Joshua forces a decision, then models it: “As for me and my house, we will serve the LORD.”

This passage exposes a truth woven throughout Scripture: neutrality toward God does not exist. Every heart serves something. The Israelites’ temptation wasn’t a sudden rejection of God for atheism—it was syncretism, blending worship of the LORD with lingering loyalty to other gods. We face the same subtlety today. Our idols rarely announce themselves. They wear the names of career, comfort, approval, or control, quietly competing for the allegiance that belongs to Christ alone.

Joshua’s declaration also reveals something crucial about faith: it is both deeply personal and inescapably communal. He doesn’t merely resolve for himself—he commits his household. Serving the Lord is never private property; it shapes marriages, parenting, and the rhythms of home. This echoes throughout redemptive history, pointing forward to Christ, who calls not just individuals but builds a household of faith, His church, bound together in covenant loyalty to Him.

We cannot manufacture this resolve in our own strength. Our hearts are prone to wander, drawn toward lesser gods even after tasting grace. Yet the gospel assures us that Christ has already chosen and secured those who are His. Our daily choosing is not what saves us—it’s the fruit of being already saved, the Spirit enabling obedience that flows from a heart made new. Today’s question remains ours: not merely “do I believe in God,” but “whom am I actually serving with my time, affections, and decisions?”

Confessional Tie-in: “Man’s chief end is to glorify God, and to enjoy him forever.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Q.1

Prayer Prompt: Lord, You alone are worthy of my worship, yet I confess my heart still chases lesser loves—comfort, approval, control. Thank You for Your relentless faithfulness even when mine wavers; today, renew my household and my heart to serve You alone, through the strength of Christ.

Go Deeper: Take time today to identify one “quiet idol” competing for your devotion—something good that has become ultimate. Ask how this might be shaping your home, not just your private devotion, and consider reading Matthew 6:24 alongside this passage to reflect on undivided loyalty to Christ.
