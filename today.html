...for “All flesh is like grass and all its glory like the flower of grass. The grass withers, and the flower falls, but the word of the Lord remains forever.” And this word is the good news that was preached to you.
1 Peter 1:24-25 (ESV)

What Outlasts the Grass

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Peter, writing to scattered believers facing real suffering, quotes Isaiah 40 to make a stark contrast: everything human is temporary, but God’s word is eternal. The imagery would have resonated deeply in an agrarian culture—grass springs up green and full of promise, then the hot wind blows and it withers within days. Peter applies this not just to human life, but to human “glory”—our achievements, reputations, beauty, and strength. All of it has an expiration date.

This isn’t meant to depress us but to reorient us. We live in a culture obsessed with building lasting legacies, yet Scripture reminds us that even the most impressive human accomplishments are grass. Careers end. Bodies age. Admiration fades. Empires crumble. If we anchor our hope in anything flesh-based, we’re building on sand that’s already eroding.

But here’s the good news Peter immediately pivots to: the word of the Lord remains forever—and this enduring word is the gospel itself, the message preached to us. Our salvation doesn’t rest on human achievement or even our own spiritual performance, both of which are as fleeting as grass. It rests on a word that cannot wither, spoken by a God who cannot fail, accomplished by a Christ who cannot be undone.

This should reshape how we spend our days. Are we investing primarily in things that wither, or in the eternal word that gives life? Sanctification means increasingly loosening our grip on fading glory and tightening our grip on the unshakeable promises of God. When we face our own mortality, or watch loved ones fade, or feel the sting of our own limitations, we’re not meant to despair—we’re meant to remember that something greater holds us. The gospel we received is not fragile. It doesn’t depend on our strength to sustain it. It depended on Christ’s finished work, and it will outlast every grave.

Confessional Tie-in:

“The Holy Scripture is the most sure and infallible rule of all saving knowledge, faith, and obedience… The authority of the Holy Scripture… dependeth not upon the testimony of any man or Church, but wholly upon God.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter 1

Prayer Prompt:

Lord, forgive me for the times I’ve placed my hope in things that wither—my accomplishments, my reputation, my own strength. Thank you that your word stands forever and that my salvation rests on Christ’s finished work, not my fading glory. Help me today to treasure what is eternal above what is temporary.

Go Deeper:

Take an honest inventory: what “grass” are you currently depending on for security or identity—career success, physical appearance, others’ approval? Spend a few minutes in Isaiah 40:6-8 or Psalm 103:15-18, and ask God to help you hold these things loosely while holding His promises tightly.
