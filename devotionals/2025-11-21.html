November 21, 2025

[Thanksgiving] I give thanks to my God always for you because of the grace of God that was given you in Christ Jesus, that in every way you were enriched in him in all speech and all knowledge—
1 Corinthians 1:4-5 (ESV)

Enriched by Grace

Reflection

What does this text teach us? Paul begins his letter to the Corinthians not with criticism, but with thanksgiving. Despite their many problems—divisions, immorality, and doctrinal confusion—he first acknowledges God’s grace at work among them. This reveals something profound about how God sees His people and how we should view fellow believers.

Paul gives thanks “because of the grace of God that was given you in Christ Jesus.” Notice that grace isn’t something we achieve or maintain—it was “given” to them. This divine grace isn’t merely God’s kindness in general, but His specific, transformative work through Christ. It’s grace that accomplishes something definite in believers’ lives.

The result of this grace is that they “were enriched in him in all speech and all knowledge.” The word “enriched” suggests abundance—God doesn’t give His people just enough to get by, but lavishes spiritual wealth upon them. This enrichment happens “in him,” meaning our union with Christ is the sphere where all spiritual blessing flows.

But what does it mean to be enriched in “speech and knowledge”? This isn’t worldly eloquence or academic learning. Rather, it’s the Spirit-given ability to understand and communicate gospel truth. Even the troubled Corinthians, who struggled with so many issues, had received genuine spiritual gifts for building up the church.

Here’s the remarkable truth: God’s assessment of His people often differs dramatically from our own. We see our failures, weaknesses, and inconsistencies. God sees what His grace has accomplished and continues to accomplish. This doesn’t minimize sin or promote complacency, but it reminds us that our identity rests on God’s work in Christ, not our performance.

This perspective should transform how we view ourselves and other believers. When we’re discouraged by our slow growth, we can remember that God has enriched us with spiritual understanding and the ability to speak His truth. When we’re frustrated with fellow Christians, we can look for evidences of God’s grace rather than focusing solely on their shortcomings.

Paul’s thanksgiving also reminds us that every spiritual gift—whether knowledge, speech, or any other ability—comes from God’s grace alone. This should produce both humility and gratitude, recognizing that anything good in us flows from His unmerited favor.

Confessional Tie-in

“All those that are justified God vouchsafeth, in and for his only Son Jesus Christ, to make partakers of the grace of adoption: by which they are taken into the number, and enjoy the liberties and privileges of the children of God.”
— Westminster Confession of Faith 12.1

Prayer Prompt

Gracious Father, thank You for the spiritual riches You’ve given me in Christ—understanding of Your truth and ability to speak of Your goodness. Help me see myself and others through the lens of Your grace rather than focusing only on failures and shortcomings.

Go Deeper

Take inventory of the spiritual gifts and understanding God has given you through His grace. How can you use your “speech and knowledge” to encourage fellow believers this week? Consider writing a note of thanksgiving to God for specific ways He has enriched someone you know spiritually.
