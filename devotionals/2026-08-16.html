August 16, 2026

Since we have these promises, beloved, let us cleanse ourselves from every defilement of body and spirit, bringing holiness to completion in the fear of God.
2 Corinthians 7:1 (ESV)

Promises That Purify

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Paul writes these words as a hinge between two great truths. In the preceding verses, he has just reminded the Corinthians of staggering promises: God dwells among his people, receives them as sons and daughters, and calls them his own. Now Paul draws the conclusion—”since we have these promises”—holiness isn’t a bargaining chip to earn God’s favor. It’s a response to favor already secured.

Notice the order carefully. Paul doesn’t say, “Cleanse yourselves so that you might have these promises.” The promises come first. Grace precedes obedience. This is the rhythm of the entire Christian life: God acts, and we respond. We don’t clean ourselves up to become God’s children; we pursue holiness because we already are.

Yet Paul doesn’t let this grace become passive. “Let us cleanse ourselves” is an active, deliberate call. Sanctification is not something that merely happens to us while we watch—it’s something the Spirit works in us and through us as we actively put off defilement of “body and spirit.” Paul has in mind the whole person: our outward conduct and our inward affections, our actions and our attitudes. Sin corrupts both, and grace addresses both.

This is a lifelong pursuit—”bringing holiness to completion.” We are not yet finished. The Christian life is not a single decisive moment but an ongoing, Spirit-empowered process of growth, pursued “in the fear of God”—not a cringing terror, but a reverent awe that takes God’s holiness seriously and takes sin seriously too.

For the believer weary of the fight against sin, this text offers both comfort and challenge. Comfort: your standing before God rests on his promises, not your performance. Challenge: those very promises should provoke earnest effort, not complacency. Where in your life have you grown lax, assuming grace excuses indifference toward sin? Where do you need to actively pursue cleansing—not to earn love, but because you are already loved?

Confessional Tie-in:

“Sanctification is the work of God’s free grace, whereby we are renewed in the whole man after the image of God, and are enabled more and more to die unto sin, and live unto righteousness.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Q. 35

Prayer Prompt:

Father, thank you for the promises that make me your own before I ever cleaned myself up. Forgive me for the ways I have grown careless with sin, presuming upon your grace rather than pursuing holiness in reverent gratitude. By your Spirit, cleanse my body and spirit today, and grant me a holy fear that treasures you above every defilement.

Go Deeper:

Take a few minutes to name one area of “body” defilement (actions, habits) and one area of “spirit” defilement (attitudes, hidden desires) that need cleansing. Bring both honestly to God in confession, then ask: What promise from Scripture can fuel my pursuit of holiness today, rather than fear of punishment? Consider reading 2 Corinthians 6:16–18 for the full context of the promises Paul references.
