...so Christ, having been offered once to bear the sins of many, will appear a second time, not to deal with sin but to save those who are eagerly waiting for him.
Hebrews 9:28 (ESV)

The Finished Work and Future Hope

Reflection

What does this text teach us? Hebrews 9:28 presents us with both the accomplished reality of Christ’s sacrifice and the certain promise of His return. The verse speaks of two appearances of Christ: His first coming “to bear the sins of many,” and His second coming “to save those who are eagerly waiting for him.”

The phrase “having been offered once” carries tremendous weight. Unlike the Old Testament priests who repeatedly offered sacrifices that could never truly remove sin, Christ’s sacrifice was complete, perfect, and final. When He bore our sins on the cross, He didn’t merely cover them temporarily—He removed them entirely. This wasn’t a partial payment requiring installments, but the full satisfaction of God’s justice against sin.

Notice that Christ bore the sins of “many”—not all people without exception, but many from every tribe and tongue who would believe. This points us to the particular nature of His redemption. He didn’t make salvation possible for everyone; He actually saved His people by taking their specific sins upon Himself.

But this passage doesn’t end with the cross. Christ “will appear a second time,” and this future appearance will have a different purpose. He won’t come to deal with sin again—that work is finished. Instead, He comes “to save those who are eagerly waiting for him.” This salvation refers to the final consummation of our redemption: glorified bodies, sinless hearts, and eternal fellowship with God.

The phrase “eagerly waiting” describes the proper Christian posture. We live between the “already” and the “not yet”—already justified, not yet glorified. This waiting isn’t passive resignation but active anticipation. Like a bride preparing for her wedding day, we live with hope that shapes our daily choices.

This truth transforms how we face trials. When suffering seems overwhelming, we remember that Christ already bore our ultimate suffering. When sin patterns discourage us, we recall that our future glorification is as certain as our past justification. When the world appears to be spinning out of control, we know that Christ will return to establish perfect justice and peace.

The Christian life is lived in the confidence of what Christ has already accomplished and the hope of what He will yet complete. Both truths anchor our souls and fuel our perseverance.

Confessional Tie-in

“By his obedience and death, he did fully discharge the debt of all those that are thus justified, and did make a proper, real and full satisfaction to his Father’s justice on their behalf.” — Westminster Confession of Faith 11.3

Prayer Prompt

Lord Jesus, thank You for bearing my sins completely and forever on the cross. Help me to live today in the confidence of Your finished work while eagerly anticipating Your return to complete my salvation.

Go Deeper

Consider how the certainty of Christ’s return should affect your priorities this week. Spend time meditating on what it means that His sacrifice was offered “once”—how does this truth speak to any areas where you’re trying to earn God’s favor through your own efforts?
