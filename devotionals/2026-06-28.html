The Lord is not slow to fulfill his promise as some count slowness, but is patient toward you, not wishing that any should perish, but that all should reach repentance.
2 Peter 3:9 (ESV)

Waiting on a Patient God

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Peter writes to believers wrestling with a hard question: why hasn’t Christ returned yet? Scoffers were mocking the delay, suggesting God’s promises had failed (2 Peter 3:3-4). Peter’s answer reframes their entire perspective on time itself. What looks like slowness from our vantage point is actually patience from God’s. The delay isn’t divine forgetfulness—it’s divine mercy in action.

This verse reveals something profound about God’s character. He operates outside our timelines, and His seeming delays are often expressions of His saving purposes. The word “patient” here echoes throughout Scripture—God revealed Himself to Moses as “slow to anger, abounding in steadfast love” (Exodus 34:6). This isn’t a new trait; it’s who God has always been.

Notice the phrase “not wishing that any should perish, but that all should reach repentance.” This speaks to God’s revealed will—His invitation extended genuinely to all who hear the gospel call. Even as we affirm God’s sovereign election of His people, we must not miss the tenderness here. God takes no pleasure in judgment (Ezekiel 33:11). Every day that passes before Christ’s return is a day filled with purpose—the ingathering of those He has called, the patient work of drawing sinners to repentance.

For us, this truth cuts two ways. First, it comforts those who wonder why God hasn’t yet answered prayers, healed situations, or brought justice. His timing isn’t slowness—it’s wisdom and mercy we can’t fully see. Second, it should stir urgency in us. If God is patient toward the lost, giving space for repentance, shouldn’t we reflect that same patience toward others? Shouldn’t we use this time to share the gospel rather than grow comfortable in delay?

The Christian life often means living in tension—trusting God’s timing while actively working within it. We are not called to passive waiting but active faithfulness, using each day of God’s patience as an opportunity for our own growth and for reaching others with the good news of Christ.

Confessional Tie-in:

“God, from all eternity, did, by the most wise and holy counsel of His own will, freely and unchangeably ordain whatsoever comes to pass… Although God knows whatsoever may or can come to pass upon all supposed conditions, yet hath He not decreed anything because He foresaw it as future.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter III.1-2

Prayer Prompt:

Lord, thank You for Your patience toward me when I was still far from You. Forgive me for the times I’ve grown impatient with Your timing or complacent about sharing Your patience with others. Help me trust Your perfect wisdom while remaining faithful to the work You’ve called me to today.

Go Deeper:

Consider one relationship where you’ve been waiting for someone to come to faith or grow spiritually. How might reflecting on God’s patience change how you pray for and interact with them this week? Spend time reading Exodus 34:6-7 and Romans 2:4, noting how God’s patience is always connected to His purpose of leading people toward repentance, not merely postponing judgment.
