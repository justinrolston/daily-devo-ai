For everyone who does wicked things hates the light and does not come to the light, lest his works should be exposed. But whoever does what is true comes to the light, so that it may be clearly seen that his works have been carried out in God.”
John 3:20-21 (ESV)

Walking Into the Light

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Jesus is explaining why people respond so differently to Him. Some shrink back from the light; others walk toward it. The difference isn’t intelligence or opportunity—it’s the condition of the heart. Those who love their sin avoid the light because exposure feels like death to them. But those who have been made alive by God’s Spirit run toward the light, even though it reveals their flaws, because they now love truth more than the darkness that once hid them.

This passage comes right after John 3:16-19, in the heart of Jesus’ conversation about new birth. Nicodemus came to Jesus “by night”—a small detail loaded with meaning. He was a respected teacher, yet something in him still hesitated at the threshold between darkness and light. This tension lives in every human heart before grace intervenes.

Notice that coming to the light isn’t about achieving moral perfection first. It’s about honesty. The one who “does what is true” isn’t sinless—he’s transparent. He stops hiding. He stops managing his image before God and others. This is the posture of genuine faith: not “look how good I am,” but “here I am, fully known, and still loved.”

For believers, this truth cuts two ways. First, it exposes any remaining tendency to hide parts of our lives from God—unconfessed sin, secret compromises, areas we’d rather keep in shadow. Second, it comforts us: if we find ourselves wanting to walk into the light, even imperfectly, even trembling, that desire itself is evidence of the Spirit’s work in us. Our works are not the basis of our acceptance with God, but flowing from union with Christ, they become visible testimonies that God is at work in us.

Where in your life are you still avoiding the light—still managing appearances, still hiding? Whatever it is, take courage. Christ does not expose you to condemn you, but to heal you. Come to the light.

Confessional Tie-in:

“What is sanctification? Sanctification is the work of God’s free grace, whereby we are renewed in the whole man after the image of God, and are enabled more and more to die unto sin, and live unto righteousness.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Q. 35

Prayer Prompt:

Father, I confess the parts of my heart I still keep hidden, even from You. Thank You that Christ’s light exposes not to destroy me, but to heal and free me. Give me courage today to walk fully into that light, trusting Your grace over my shame.

Go Deeper:

Take a quiet moment to ask God to show you one area of hidden compromise—something you’ve kept in shadow. Bring it into the light through honest confession, whether in prayer, to a trusted believer, or both. Reflect on 1 John 1:7—how does walking in the light lead to deeper fellowship, not less?
