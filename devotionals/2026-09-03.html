Thus says the LORD, your Redeemer, the Holy One of Israel: “I am the LORD your God, who teaches you to profit, who leads you in the way you should go.
Isaiah 48:17 (ESV)

The God Who Teaches and Leads

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Isaiah delivers these words to a people in exile, weary and disillusioned, having suffered the consequences of persistent unfaithfulness. Yet God does not abandon them to despair. He identifies himself in three profound ways: Redeemer, the Holy One of Israel, and their covenant God. Then comes the tender promise—he is the LORD who teaches them to profit and leads them in the way they should go.

Notice the order here. Before any instruction is given, God first reminds Israel who he is to them. Redemption precedes direction. This is the pattern of grace throughout Scripture: God does not teach us to earn his favor but teaches us because we already belong to him. Our obedience flows from our identity as the redeemed, not the reverse.

This truth speaks directly into our daily struggles. So often we feel we must find our own way—through career decisions, relationships, suffering, or simple daily faithfulness—as though wisdom were something we must manufacture on our own. But the God who redeemed us through Christ’s blood is the same God who patiently instructs us. He is not a distant lawgiver but an active teacher, walking alongside his people, shaping our understanding through his Word, his Spirit, and the ordinary means of grace found in worship and fellowship.

Israel’s history reveals what happens when they refused this teaching—wandering, exile, sorrow. Our own lives echo this pattern when we insist on charting our own course rather than submitting to God’s Word. Yet even here, the text comforts us: God’s teaching is not harsh coercion but loving leadership toward what is genuinely good for us (“to profit”). He knows the way because he is the Way, ultimately revealed in Jesus Christ, our great Prophet who perfectly teaches us the Father’s will, our Priest who secured our redemption, and our King who leads us home.

Are you allowing God to teach you today, or are you insisting on your own understanding?

Confessional Tie-in:

“The Spirit of God maketh the reading, but especially the preaching of the Word, an effectual means of enlightening, convincing, and humbling sinners; of driving them out of themselves, and drawing them unto Christ…” — Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 155

Prayer Prompt:

Lord, thank you for being both my Redeemer and my Teacher, patient with my slow and stumbling heart. Forgive me for the times I’ve chosen my own way over your leading. Teach me today through your Word, and give me a willing spirit to follow where you lead.

Go Deeper:

Spend a few minutes today in a passage of Scripture, asking God specifically to teach you something about his character or his ways. Consider: In what area of your life are you currently resisting God’s direction, preferring your own wisdom? Reflect on how Christ, as your Redeemer, gives you confidence to trust his leading even when the path is unclear.
