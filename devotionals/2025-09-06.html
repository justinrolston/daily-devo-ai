The Divine Home: God’s Dwelling with Those Who Love Him

John 14:23 (ESV) “Jesus answered him, ‘If anyone loves me, he will keep my word, and my Father will love him, and we will come to him and make our home with him.’”

Reflection

What profound truth does Jesus reveal in this simple statement? He unveils one of the most remarkable promises in all of Scripture: the triune God desires to dwell intimately with His people. This isn’t merely about divine presence in general—it’s about God making His home with us.

Notice the beautiful progression Jesus describes. Love for Christ naturally flows into obedience to His word. This isn’t legalistic rule-keeping that earns God’s favor, but the inevitable fruit of genuine affection. When we truly love someone, we delight in what matters to them. The believer’s obedience springs from a transformed heart that finds joy in God’s ways, not from a desperate attempt to win His approval.

The Father’s love mentioned here isn’t conditional love that begins only when we obey. Rather, Jesus reveals how the Father’s unchanging love expresses itself toward those who demonstrate authentic faith through loving obedience. God’s love for His children is already established in Christ; our obedience simply opens our eyes to experience more fully what was always true.

But the climax of this promise takes our breath away: “we will come to him and make our home with him.” The word “home” suggests permanence, intimacy, and belonging. This isn’t a brief visit from a distant deity, but the establishment of an ongoing, personal residence. Both the Father and the Son take up dwelling within the believer’s life.

Consider what this means for your daily struggles. When anxiety overwhelms, remember that the Prince of Peace has made His home within you. When loneliness creeps in, recall that you carry the presence of the triune God wherever you go. When facing difficult decisions, know that divine wisdom dwells within your heart through the indwelling Christ.

This promise also transforms how we view spiritual growth. Sanctification isn’t about trying harder to be good enough for God to notice us. It’s about recognizing that God has already moved in and is actively transforming His dwelling place—our hearts and lives—from the inside out. We don’t obey to earn His presence; we obey because His presence is already transforming us.

Confessional Tie-in

“They who are effectually called do in this life partake of justification, adoption, and sanctification, and the several benefits which in this life do either accompany or flow from them.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter 11.1

Prayer Prompt

Father, thank You for making Your home within me through Christ. Help me recognize Your presence in both ordinary moments and extraordinary challenges. May my love for Jesus overflow into joyful obedience that reflects Your character to the world around me.

Go Deeper

Throughout today, pause periodically to remember that God dwells within you—you are His temple and His home. Consider how this truth should influence your thoughts, words, and actions. Ask yourself: “How does knowing that God has made His home in me change how I approach this situation?”
