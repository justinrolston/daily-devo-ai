Great peace have those who love your law; nothing can make them stumble.
Psalm 119:165 (ESV)

The Unshakeable Peace of God’s Law

Reflection

What does this text teach us? Psalm 119:165 presents us with a remarkable promise: “Great peace have those who love your law; nothing can make them stumble.” The psalmist isn’t speaking of mere human affection for divine rules, but of a deep, abiding love for God’s Word that transforms the very foundation of our lives.

The “great peace” described here is the Hebrew word shalom – not merely the absence of conflict, but complete wholeness, harmony, and well-being. This peace comes to “those who love your law.” The word “love” here implies more than casual appreciation; it’s the kind of devoted affection that delights in God’s character revealed through His commands. When we truly love God’s law, we’re loving God Himself, for His Word perfectly reflects His holy nature.

But notice the promise that follows: “nothing can make them stumble.” This isn’t a guarantee against all hardship or difficulty. Rather, it speaks to an inner stability that cannot be shaken by external circumstances. Those who have anchored their hearts in God’s Word possess a peace so profound that even life’s storms cannot cause them to lose their footing spiritually.

This peace flows from understanding that God’s law isn’t a burden but a gift. His commands reveal the path of blessing, showing us how life works best according to His design. When we love His law, we’re not grudgingly submitting to arbitrary rules but joyfully embracing the wisdom of our Creator.

The connection between loving God’s law and experiencing unshakeable peace reveals a profound truth: true stability comes not from controlling our circumstances but from aligning our hearts with God’s unchanging character. When we delight in His Word, we find ourselves grounded in something far more solid than shifting circumstances – we’re anchored in the eternal purposes of our sovereign God.

This doesn’t mean we won’t face trials or even fall into sin. Rather, it means that our fundamental identity and hope remain secure. We may stumble in our actions, but we won’t stumble in our standing before God because His Word has taught us where our true security lies – in Christ alone.

Confessional Tie-in

“The whole counsel of God concerning all things necessary for His own glory, man’s salvation, faith and life, is either expressly set down in Scripture, or by good and necessary consequence may be deduced from Scripture.” — Westminster Confession of Faith 1.6

Prayer Prompt

Father, create in me a heart that truly loves Your Word, not as mere duty but as delight. Grant me that great peace that comes from resting in Your unchanging truth, and help me stand firm when circumstances would shake my confidence.

Go Deeper

Consider how your relationship with Scripture reflects your love for God Himself. This week, approach Bible reading not as obligation but as opportunity to know God better. What areas of your life lack peace because you’ve been trusting in circumstances rather than anchoring yourself in God’s unchanging Word?
