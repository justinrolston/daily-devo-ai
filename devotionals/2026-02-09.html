[Thanksgiving] We ought always to give thanks to God for you, brothers, as is right, because your faith is growing abundantly, and the love of every one of you for one another is increasing.
2 Thessalonians 1:3 (ESV)

Growing Together in Faith and Love

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Paul begins his second letter to the Thessalonians not with correction or rebuke, but with heartfelt thanksgiving. Notice the apostle’s careful words: “We ought always to give thanks to God for you.” This isn’t mere politeness—Paul declares it their duty, their obligation, to thank God for what He is accomplishing in this young church.

The reasons for Paul’s gratitude reveal what healthy Christian growth looks like. First, their “faith is growing abundantly.” The Greek word suggests something that grows beyond measure, like a plant flourishing in rich soil. This wasn’t static belief, but dynamic trust in Christ that was expanding and deepening. Faith, as Scripture teaches us elsewhere, is both a gift from God and something that grows through hearing His Word and experiencing His faithfulness.

Second, Paul celebrates how “the love of every one of you for one another is increasing.” Notice this isn’t selective love for those who are easy to love, but mutual affection that encompasses the entire community. This love wasn’t manufactured through human effort but flowed from their growing faith. When our trust in Christ deepens, love for His people naturally follows.

Paul’s thanksgiving reveals a profound truth: spiritual growth is God’s work that deserves God’s glory. The apostle doesn’t congratulate the Thessalonians on their impressive spiritual disciplines or commendable moral efforts. Instead, he thanks God for what God is doing in them. This reminds us that every evidence of grace in our lives—whether growing faith, increasing love, or persevering hope—is ultimately God’s gracious work within us.

This passage also shows us that spiritual growth is observable and measurable. Paul could see their expanding faith and increasing love. True Christianity isn’t merely private feelings or personal experiences, but transformation that manifests itself in our relationships with God and others. When Christ’s Spirit works within us, it shows.

How encouraging this should be for us! If you’re struggling to see growth in your own life, remember that God who began a good work in you will bring it to completion. And if you’re experiencing seasons of growth in faith and love, join Paul in directing your gratitude toward the One who deserves all credit—our faithful God who never stops working in His people.

Confessional Tie-in: “This sanctification is throughout in the whole man, yet imperfect in this life: there abideth still some remnants of corruption in every part, whence ariseth a continual and irreconcilable war; the flesh lusting against the spirit, and the spirit against the flesh.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter XIII, Article II

Prayer Prompt: Father, thank You for Your faithful work in my life and in Your church. Help me to see and celebrate the ways You are growing faith and love in those around me, and grant me patience with the gradual nature of sanctification in my own heart.

Go Deeper: Look for evidence of God’s grace in fellow believers this week—growing faith, increasing love, persevering hope—and thank God specifically for what you observe. Consider also: What growth in faith and love might others observe in your life that deserves thanksgiving to God?
