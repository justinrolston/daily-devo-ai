Great peace have those who love your law; nothing can make them stumble.
Psalm 119:165 (ESV)

Great Peace, Unshakable Foundation

Title: Great Peace, Unshakable Foundation

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? The psalmist makes a striking claim: those who love God’s law possess “great peace” and cannot be made to stumble. This isn’t referring to people who merely tolerate Scripture or study it academically, but those who genuinely delight in it—who find in God’s Word their treasure, their guide, their joy. Psalm 119, the longest chapter in Scripture, is a sustained meditation on this love for God’s revealed will, and verse 165 stands as one of its climactic promises.

Notice the psalmist doesn’t promise an absence of trouble. Life for the law-lover isn’t trouble-free—the rest of the psalm makes that abundantly clear, filled as it is with persecution, affliction, and tears. Rather, the peace described here is deeper than circumstance. It’s the settled stability of a soul anchored to unchanging truth in a world of shifting sand. When we love God’s Word, we’re loving God himself, submitting our hearts to his wisdom rather than our own fluctuating feelings or the world’s competing voices.

This connects to the broader biblical theme of Christ as the Word made flesh. We don’t love an abstract legal code; we love the living God who has spoken clearly to us, ultimately and fully in his Son. Our peace isn’t found in perfect law-keeping—we fail constantly—but in the righteousness of Christ credited to us, which frees us to love God’s law not as a means of earning favor, but as a grateful response to grace already given.

Practically, this means the antidote to anxiety, the stabilizer against life’s inevitable stumbling blocks, is a heart increasingly saturated in Scripture. Not sporadic Bible reading done out of duty, but genuine delight cultivated over time by the Spirit. When trials come—and they will—those rooted in God’s Word have something solid beneath their feet. They may bend, but they will not be shattered, because their peace was never built on circumstances in the first place.

Confessional Tie-in: “Man’s chief end is to glorify God, and to enjoy him forever.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Q&A 1

Prayer Prompt: Lord, thank you for giving us your Word as a lamp to our feet and light to our path. Forgive me for the times I’ve sought peace in lesser things while neglecting Scripture’s steady counsel. Cultivate in me a genuine love for your Word, and let that love anchor my heart when circumstances shake everything else.

Go Deeper: This week, choose one portion of Scripture to read slowly each morning, asking not “what do I need to do?” but “what does this reveal about God’s character?” Consider: where in your life have you sought peace from sources other than God’s Word, and what would change if you brought that same situation under Scripture’s light?
