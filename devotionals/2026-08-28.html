Jesus answered them, “This is the work of God, that you believe in him whom he has sent.”
John 6:29 (ESV)

The Only Work That Matters

Title: The Only Work That Matters

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? The crowd following Jesus had just witnessed the feeding of the five thousand, and now they press him with a question: “What must we do, to be doing the works of God?” (John 6:28). It’s a natural question for the human heart—we instinctively think of salvation as a ladder of accomplishments, a checklist to satisfy a demanding deity. Jesus’ answer overturns this entirely. There are not many works to perform, but one: to believe in the one whom God has sent.

This is a stunning claim. Jesus places faith itself in the category of “work”—not because belief earns anything, but because it is the sole response God requires and the only means by which his saving work reaches us. Even our believing is not a work we conjure from spiritual strength we already possess. Scripture elsewhere tells us that faith itself is a gift (Ephesians 2:8-9), granted by the Spirit to hearts otherwise dead in sin. We do not produce faith to impress God; we receive faith because God, in mercy, opens blind eyes and softens hard hearts.

This truth ought to dismantle any lingering hope we place in our performance. How often do we quietly measure our standing before God by our Bible reading streaks, our moral improvements, our service records? These things matter as fruits of a living faith, but they are never the root. The root is simply this: do you trust him? Have you rested your whole weight on Christ, the one sent by the Father to save sinners?

For the weary believer who feels they never do enough, this is good news—your acceptance was never based on enough. For the comfortable believer who trusts subtly in their own righteousness, this is a needed correction—no amount of religious activity substitutes for genuine trust in Christ alone. Today, return again to the foundation: not what you do for God, but what Christ has already done, and receive it by faith.

Confessional Tie-in:

“Faith in Jesus Christ is a saving grace, whereby we receive and rest upon him alone for salvation, as he is offered to us in the gospel.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Q. 86

Prayer Prompt:

Father, I confess how easily I drift toward trusting my own efforts rather than resting in Christ alone. Thank you for sending your Son and for granting me faith to believe in him. Strengthen my trust today, and let it bear fruit in a life that reflects your grace.

Go Deeper:

Consider where you’ve been unconsciously trying to earn favor with God through performance rather than resting in Christ’s finished work. Read Ephesians 2:1-10 and notice how faith itself flows from grace. Ask yourself: is my confidence today anchored in what I have done, or in whom I have believed?
