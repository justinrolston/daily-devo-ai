June 29, 2026

The LORD will fulfill his purpose for me; your steadfast love, O LORD, endures forever. Do not forsake the work of your hands.
Psalm 138:8 (ESV)

He Will Finish What He Started

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? David, near the close of a psalm celebrating God’s greatness and nearness to the lowly, arrives at a settled confidence: “The LORD will fulfill his purpose for me.” This is not vague optimism about life working out. It is the declaration of a man who knows his life belongs to a covenant-keeping God with a definite plan. The Hebrew word behind “fulfill” carries the sense of completing or bringing to an end—nothing left undone.

Notice what grounds this confidence: not David’s performance, but God’s steadfast love (hesed), the covenant loyalty that “endures forever.” David isn’t confident because he’s finished the race well. He’s confident because God’s love doesn’t run out before the finish line does.

This connects to the larger story Scripture tells. From Genesis to Revelation, God is a promise-keeper who completes what He begins. Paul echoes this exact confidence in Philippians 1:6: “he who began a good work in you will bring it to completion.” Your salvation was never a project you initiated and God assists—it is His purpose, His work, from election to glorification. If it depended on your consistency, the outcome would be uncertain. Because it depends on His unchanging love, it is certain.

Yet notice David still prays: “Do not forsake the work of your hands.” Confidence in God’s purpose doesn’t eliminate prayer—it fuels it. This is the paradox of grace: we rest in God’s sovereign faithfulness and we cry out for it, because we are “the work of his hands,” fragile clay dependent moment by moment on the Potter.

For the believer wrestling with doubt, unfinished sanctification, or a sense of spiritual failure, this verse speaks directly: your standing before God does not rest on your progress but on His promise. For the comfortable believer, it summons humility—you are not self-made; you are handiwork, still being shaped.

Confessional Tie-in:

“They whom God hath accepted in his Beloved, effectually called, and sanctified by his Spirit… can neither totally nor finally fall away from the state of grace, but shall certainly persevere therein to the end, and be eternally saved… This perseverance of the saints depends not upon their own free will, but upon the immutability of the decree of election, flowing from the free and unchangeable love of God.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter XVII

Prayer Prompt:

Lord, I praise You as the God who finishes what He begins. I confess how quickly I forget this and try to secure my own standing through anxious effort. Thank You for Your steadfast love that does not depend on my consistency—please continue the work You have started in me, and do not forsake me now.

Go Deeper:

Read Philippians 1:3–6 and Psalm 138 in full, noticing how confidence in God’s sovereignty produced worship, not passivity, in both David and Paul. This week, when you feel like a spiritual failure or an unfinished project, pray David’s exact words back to God as your own petition.
