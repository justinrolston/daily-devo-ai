1 Corinthians 13: 6-7 (ESV)

Love That Rejoices in Truth and Endures All Things

Reflection

What does this text teach us about the nature of true love? Paul continues his magnificent description of love in 1 Corinthians 13 by revealing two fundamental characteristics that mark authentic Christian love: it aligns itself with truth and demonstrates remarkable endurance.

First, love “does not rejoice at wrongdoing, but rejoices with the truth.” True love cannot celebrate sin or find pleasure in others’ failures. Instead, it finds its joy in alignment with God’s truth. This isn’t merely moral preference—it reflects the character of God himself, who is both loving and holy. When we truly love someone, we cannot celebrate what destroys them, even if confronting wrongdoing proves difficult or costly.

Notice Paul says love “rejoices with the truth,” not merely tolerates it. Biblical love actively delights in God’s truth because it understands that truth leads to flourishing while deception leads to destruction. This means Christian love will sometimes appear unloving to a watching world when it refuses to affirm what God calls sin, but genuine love seeks another’s ultimate good, not their temporary comfort.

Second, Paul describes love’s extraordinary endurance through four sweeping statements: love “bears all things, believes all things, hopes all things, endures all things.” This doesn’t mean love is naive or gullible—the same apostle elsewhere warns about discernment. Rather, love operates with a fundamental orientation of trust, hope, and perseverance even in difficult circumstances.

Love “bears” burdens without breaking, “believes” the best about others when possible, “hopes” for redemption and restoration, and “endures” through trials that would cause lesser affections to fail. This kind of love reflects God’s own patient endurance with his people throughout Scripture.

Why can Christians love this way? Because we have first been loved by God with exactly this kind of love. Christ rejoiced in truth even when it led to the cross, and he bore, believed, hoped, and endured all things for our salvation. His perfect love now dwells in us through the Holy Spirit, enabling us to love others as we have been loved.

This transformative love cannot be manufactured through human effort alone—it flows from hearts that have been captured by the gospel and are being renewed by God’s Spirit day by day.

Confessional Tie-in

“We love him, because he first loved us. And this commandment have we from him, That he who loveth God love his brother also.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter XXVI, Of the Communion of Saints

Prayer Prompt

Father, thank you for loving me with truth and endurance when I deserved neither. Help me to love others with the same grace you have shown me—rejoicing in truth even when it’s difficult, and bearing with others as you have borne with me.

Go Deeper

Consider one relationship where you’ve been tempted to rejoice in wrongdoing rather than truth, or where your love has grown weary. How might the gospel enable you to love with both truth and endurance in that situation? Reflect on specific ways God has demonstrated this kind of love toward you.
