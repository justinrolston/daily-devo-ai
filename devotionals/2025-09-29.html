For everyone who does wicked things hates the light and does not come to the light, lest his works should be exposed. But whoever does what is true comes to the light, so that it may be clearly seen that his works have been carried out in God.”
John 3:20-21 (ESV)

Walking in the Light of Truth

Reflection

What does this text teach us? Jesus reveals a fundamental truth about human nature and our relationship with God’s light. Everyone falls into one of two categories: those who hate the light because it exposes their wicked deeds, and those who come to the light because their works are carried out in God.

The “light” Jesus speaks of is not merely illumination, but divine truth itself—God’s holiness, His standards, and ultimately Christ Himself as “the light of the world.” This light has a penetrating quality that reveals everything as it truly is. For those living in deliberate sin, this exposure feels threatening. They instinctively avoid environments, relationships, and truths that might reveal their spiritual condition. Like Adam and Eve hiding among the trees, they seek darkness to conceal their shame.

But notice the contrast: “whoever does what is true comes to the light.” This doesn’t mean the person is perfect or sinless. Rather, it describes someone who lives with integrity—whose life aligns with God’s truth. They welcome scrutiny because they have nothing to hide from God. Their works are “carried out in God,” meaning they live coram Deo, before the face of God, with His glory as their motivation.

This passage challenges us to examine our own hearts. Do we find ourselves avoiding certain biblical truths, godly friendships, or spiritual disciplines because they make us uncomfortable? Are there areas of our lives we hope remain hidden from God’s searching light? Or do we, by God’s grace, welcome His illumination, knowing that exposure leads to cleansing and growth?

The beauty of the gospel is that Christ’s light both exposes and heals. When we come to Him, confessing our sin rather than hiding it, we find forgiveness and transformation. The same light that terrifies the guilty becomes the lamp that guides our steps. We learn to live transparently before God, not because we’re perfect, but because we’re covered by Christ’s righteousness. This frees us to walk in integrity, knowing our acceptance isn’t based on our performance but on His perfect work.

Living in the light means embracing truth-telling in all areas of life—with God, with ourselves, and with others. It means choosing authenticity over image management, confession over concealment.

Confessional Tie-in

“The light of nature shows that there is a God, who has lordship and sovereignty over all; is good, and does good unto all; and is therefore to be feared, loved, praised, called upon, trusted in, and served with all the heart, and with all the soul, and with all the might.” — Westminster Confession of Faith 21.1

Prayer Prompt

Father, Your light reveals both our sin and Your grace. Help us to live transparently before You, welcoming Your truth even when it exposes areas that need transformation. Give us courage to walk in integrity, knowing that in Christ we have nothing to hide and everything to gain by living in Your light.

Go Deeper

Consider one area of your life where you might be avoiding God’s light. What would it look like to invite Him into that space today? Reflect on Psalm 139:23-24 and ask God to search your heart, trusting that His exposure always leads to healing for those who belong to Him.
