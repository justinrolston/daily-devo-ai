Therefore God has highly exalted him and bestowed on him the name that is above every name, so that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, in heaven and on earth and under the earth, and every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father.
Philippians 2:9-11 (ESV)

Every Knee Will Bow

Title: The Name Above Every Name

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? These verses form the triumphant conclusion to one of the most beautiful passages in Scripture—a hymn tracing Christ’s descent from glory into humiliation, and now his ascent back into glory. Paul has just described Jesus emptying himself, taking the form of a servant, and humbling himself to death on a cross. “Therefore,” Paul writes—because of this humility, this obedience, this sacrifice—God has highly exalted him.

Notice the pattern: the way down became the way up. The cross led to the crown. This is not incidental to the gospel; it is the gospel. Jesus did not grasp for glory but received it from the Father as the reward of his obedience on our behalf.

The exaltation described here is cosmic in scope. Every knee—in heaven, on earth, under the earth—will bow. Every tongue will confess. This includes willing worshippers who bow in joyful surrender now, and it includes those who will one day bow in forced acknowledgment of what they refused to believe. There is no neutral ground before this King.

For the believer, this truth reorders everything. If Jesus reigns with this kind of authority, then no circumstance, no anxiety, no earthly power holds ultimate sway over your life. The same Christ who humbled himself for you now rules over you and for you. Your daily struggles—with sin, with suffering, with uncertainty—unfold under the authority of the exalted Lord who intercedes for his people.

This also confronts our comfort. Do we live as though Jesus is merely a helpful addition to our lives, or as the Lord before whom we already bow? Genuine faith doesn’t wait for the final day of universal confession—it bends the knee now, in worship, obedience, and trust, tasting today what all creation will one day acknowledge.

Confessional Tie-in:

“Q. 27: What do you understand by the providence of God? A. The almighty and ever present power of God by which he upholds, as with his hand, heaven and earth and all creatures, and so rules them that… all things, in fact, come to us not by chance but by his fatherly hand.” — Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 27

Prayer Prompt:

Lord Jesus, I bow before you today, not out of fear alone, but out of love for who you are and gratitude for what you’ve done. Forgive me for the moments I’ve lived as though you were not truly Lord over my circumstances. Thank you for humbling yourself for my salvation—reign now in my heart as you already reign over all creation.

Go Deeper:

This week, identify one area of life where you’ve been slow to acknowledge Christ’s lordship—perhaps finances, relationships, or unspoken fears. Bring that specific area before him in prayer, confessing his authority over it. For further reflection, read Isaiah 45:22-23, the Old Testament passage Paul echoes here, and consider how this promise was already woven into God’s redemptive plan long before Bethlehem.
