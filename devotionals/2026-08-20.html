I have set the LORD always before me; because he is at my right hand, I shall not be shaken.
Psalm 16:8 (ESV)

Unshaken: The Steady Gaze of Faith

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? David writes from experience, not theory. As king, he faced betrayal, war, and the constant threat of losing his throne—and his life. Yet he declares a settled confidence: “I shall not be shaken.” This isn’t natural optimism or denial of danger. It flows from a deliberate choice: “I have set the LORD always before me.”

The phrase suggests intentional focus, like fixing your eyes on a distant point to keep your balance. David disciplined his mind and heart to keep God’s presence continually in view—not as a vague spiritual feeling, but as concrete reality. Because God is “at my right hand,” the place of strength and support in ancient warfare and daily life, David finds unshakeable ground beneath his feet.

This psalm points beyond David himself. Peter, preaching at Pentecost, quotes this very passage and applies it to Christ (Acts 2:25-28). Jesus lived this truth perfectly, setting the Father always before him, walking in unbroken communion even toward the cross. He was not abandoned to decay, but raised—securing for us the stability David could only anticipate.

This matters for you today. Your circumstances may genuinely be shaking—finances, relationships, health, uncertainty about the future. Scripture doesn’t promise the absence of tremors. It promises a foundation that doesn’t move. Because Christ has already faced ultimate instability (death itself) and emerged victorious, you who are united to him by faith stand on that same unshakeable ground.

But notice: this stability isn’t passive. David set the Lord before him. This requires practice—Scripture reading, prayer, corporate worship, remembering God’s past faithfulness. Sanctification means training our wandering hearts to fix their gaze where security actually lies. When anxiety rises, ask: what have I set before me today? My problems, my performance, my fears—or the Lord who is already at my right hand?

Confessional Tie-in:

“What is thy only comfort in life and death? That I, with body and soul, both in life and death, am not my own, but belong unto my faithful Saviour Jesus Christ, who… so preserves me that without the will of my heavenly Father, not a hair can fall from my head.” — Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 1

Prayer Prompt: Lord, I confess how easily my gaze drifts toward my troubles rather than toward you. Thank you that Christ secured an unshakeable inheritance for me through his death and resurrection. Help me today to deliberately set you before me, trusting that you are near, even when I cannot feel it.

Go Deeper: Before checking your phone tomorrow morning, spend sixty seconds naming one truth about God’s character and presence—let that be the first thing “set before” your mind. Reflect this week: what practices help you remember God’s nearness during unstable seasons, and which habits tend to displace him from your focus?
