Heaven and earth will pass away, but my words will not pass away.
Matthew 24:35 (ESV)

The Word That Outlasts the World

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Jesus speaks these words in the middle of a sobering discourse about the end of the age—wars, famines, false prophets, and cosmic upheaval. The disciples had asked about the signs of His coming and the close of the age, and Jesus paints a picture of a world in convulsion. Yet in the midst of describing creation’s unraveling, He makes an astonishing claim: heaven and earth—the very fabric of the universe—will pass away, but His words will remain forever.

This is no small statement. The mountains that seem eternal, the stars that have burned for millennia, the earth beneath our feet—all of it is temporary. But Jesus places His own words in a category above and beyond creation itself. He is claiming what only God can claim: that His speech shares in the eternal nature of God (compare Isaiah 40:8). This is Christ speaking not merely as a wise teacher, but as the eternal Word made flesh, the One through whom all things were made and by whom all things hold together.

For us, this truth is both anchor and compass. We live in a world of shifting sand—relationships change, health fails, institutions crumble, even our own bodies are wasting away. Everything we can touch and see is passing. But Scripture, the very words of Christ recorded and preserved by His Spirit, stands as bedrock beneath our feet. When Jesus promises forgiveness to those who trust Him, that promise cannot fail. When He assures us of His return, of resurrection, of a kingdom that cannot be shaken, we can build our whole lives on it.

This should reshape our priorities. Are we investing our hearts in what is passing away, or in the Word that remains? Are we anxious over the temporary while neglecting the eternal? The trustworthiness of Christ’s words calls us to read them, treasure them, obey them, and rest in them—not as one voice among many, but as the unshakable foundation for both this life and the one to come.

Confessional Tie-in:

“The heavenly Father has revealed His will unto us at sundry times and in divers manners… and finally…has fully revealed Himself to us in His Son… whose voice we must hear.” — Belgic Confession, Article 2 (paraphrased summary of God’s self-revelation culminating in Christ)

Prayer Prompt:

Lord Jesus, You are the Word that never fails, even when everything around me feels uncertain and temporary. Forgive me for the times I’ve trusted shifting circumstances more than Your unchanging promises. Thank You that Your words are my firm foundation—teach me to build my life, my hopes, and my daily decisions on them alone.

Go Deeper:

Take five minutes today to identify one promise of Christ you need to stand on right now—perhaps His promise of peace, forgiveness, or His presence with you always. Write it down and place it somewhere visible this week. Ask yourself: What am I anchoring my identity and security in that will not last—and how can I shift my trust more fully onto the enduring Word of Christ?
