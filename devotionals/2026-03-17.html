[The LORD Is My Shepherd] A Psalm of David. The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want. He makes me lie down in green pastures. He leads me beside still waters. He restores my soul. He leads me in paths of righteousness for his name's sake.
Psalm 23:1-3 (ESV)

The Shepherd’s Complete Care

Reflection:

What does this beloved text teach us? Psalm 23 reveals the intimate relationship between God and His people through the tender metaphor of a shepherd and his sheep. When David declares “The LORD is my shepherd,” he uses the covenant name of God—Yahweh—emphasizing that the almighty Creator of the universe personally tends to His flock with devoted care.

“I shall not want” doesn’t promise material abundance, but rather the assurance that our Shepherd provides everything necessary for our spiritual well-being. This contentment flows not from circumstances, but from confidence in our Shepherd’s character and provision. In a world obsessed with accumulation and achievement, believers find rest in God’s sufficiency.

The imagery deepens as David describes God’s active care: making us lie down in green pastures and leading us beside still waters. Sheep are naturally anxious creatures who struggle to rest when threatened or hungry. Similarly, our restless hearts find peace only when our Shepherd creates the conditions for spiritual nourishment and rest. He doesn’t merely point us toward good things—He leads us there and ensures we receive what we need.

“He restores my soul” speaks to our Shepherd’s ongoing work of renewal. The Hebrew word suggests bringing back something that has wandered or been damaged. How often do our souls become weary, wounded, or wayward? Yet our faithful Shepherd continuously restores us through His Word, Spirit, and grace. This restoration isn’t a one-time event but a daily mercy.

Finally, God “leads me in paths of righteousness for his name’s sake.” Our Shepherd guides us not randomly, but purposefully toward holiness. The motivation isn’t primarily our benefit—though we certainly benefit—but God’s glory. When His sheep walk in righteousness, His character as the perfect Shepherd is displayed to the world.

This psalm reminds us that we are not independent beings navigating life alone, but sheep utterly dependent on our Shepherd’s wisdom, protection, and provision. In Christ, we have the Good Shepherd who laid down His life for the sheep, ensuring that nothing can separate us from this tender, powerful care.

Confessional Tie-in: “What is your only comfort in life and in death? That I am not my own, but belong—body and soul, in life and in death—to my faithful Savior, Jesus Christ.”
— Heidelberg Catechism, Question 1

Prayer Prompt: Good Shepherd, thank You for Your faithful care over my restless heart. Help me trust Your provision when I’m tempted toward anxiety or discontent, and guide me in paths that bring glory to Your name today.

Go Deeper: Consider areas of your life where you’re striving in your own strength rather than resting in the Shepherd’s care. What would it look like to practically trust God’s sufficiency in one specific concern you’re carrying today?
