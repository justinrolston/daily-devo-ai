Have I not commanded you? Be strong and courageous. Do not be frightened, and do not be dismayed, for the LORD your God is with you wherever you go.”
Joshua 1:9 (ESV)

Commanded Courage in God’s Presence

Reflection

What does this text teach us? God’s words to Joshua reveal a profound truth about Christian courage—it flows not from our own strength or circumstances, but from God’s unchanging presence and faithful promises. Notice that God begins with a question: “Have I not commanded you?” This isn’t doubt; it’s emphasis. The Lord had already given Joshua this charge multiple times, underscoring that divine commands come with divine enablement.

The courage God commands isn’t mere human bravery or positive thinking. The Hebrew words here speak of strength that comes from being firmly established, like a tree with deep roots. This is courage rooted in the character of God Himself. When the Lord says “Be strong and courageous,” He’s calling Joshua to live according to spiritual reality rather than visible circumstances.

The prohibition is equally important: “Do not be frightened, and do not be dismayed.” Fear and discouragement are natural human responses to overwhelming challenges, but they need not define us. God doesn’t condemn Joshua for feeling these emotions; rather, He provides the antidote—His abiding presence.

The foundation of this courage is theological: “for the LORD your God is with you wherever you go.” This isn’t merely God’s proximity, but His covenant faithfulness. The same God who delivered Israel from Egypt, parted the Red Sea, and sustained them in the wilderness promises never to leave or forsake His people. His presence guarantees His power will be available.

For believers today, this promise finds its ultimate fulfillment in Christ. Through His Spirit, God’s presence is not just with us but within us. When facing job uncertainty, family struggles, health challenges, or spiritual battles, we need not muster courage from within ourselves. Instead, we can rest in the reality that our covenant God has bound Himself to us in Christ.

This doesn’t mean we’ll never feel afraid or discouraged. Rather, it means these feelings need not control our decisions or define our identity. We can acknowledge our fears while choosing to act according to God’s promises rather than our emotions. True biblical courage is not the absence of fear, but faithfulness in spite of fear—grounded in the unshakeable reality of God’s presence with His people.

Confessional Tie-in

“The almighty power, unsearchable wisdom, and infinite goodness of God so far manifest themselves in his providence, that it extendeth itself even to the first fall, and all other sins of angels and men; and that not by a bare permission, but such as hath joined with it a most wise and powerful bounding, and otherwise ordering, and governing of them, in a manifold dispensation, to his own holy ends.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter 5, Article 4

Prayer Prompt

Father, thank You for Your promise to never leave or forsake Your children. Help me to find my courage not in my own strength, but in the reality of Your presence with me. When fear and discouragement tempt me to shrink back, remind me that You have commanded me to be strong and courageous because You are always with me.

Go Deeper

Consider one specific area where you’re currently struggling with fear or discouragement. How might God’s promised presence change your perspective and response? Memorize this verse, meditating particularly on the phrase “the LORD your God is with you wherever you go” as you face today’s challenges.
