And it is my prayer that your love may abound more and more, with knowledge and all discernment, so that you may approve what is excellent, and so be pure and blameless for the day of Christ...
Philippians 1:9-10 (ESV)

Love That Discerns

Title: Love That Discerns

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Paul is praying for the Philippian church, and his request is striking: he doesn’t merely ask that their love would grow, but that it would grow with knowledge and all discernment. In our culture, love is often portrayed as purely emotional—a feeling that resists definition or evaluation. But biblical love is never sentimental blindness. It is a love that sees clearly, thinks carefully, and chooses wisely.

Notice the purpose Paul gives for this discerning love: “so that you may approve what is excellent.” The Greek word here carries the idea of testing something to determine its genuine value, like a merchant examining a coin for authenticity. Mature love doesn’t just accept whatever seems good; it tests things against the truth of God’s Word and chooses what truly matters, letting go of lesser things—even good things—for the sake of the best things.

This has everything to do with sanctification. Left to ourselves, our hearts are easily deceived, prone to call evil good and good evil. We need more than sincerity; we need a love that has been shaped and sharpened by the knowledge of Christ and his Word. This is the Spirit’s ongoing work in us—not a one-time event, but a maturing process where our affections are trained to align with God’s own priorities.

And notice the goal: purity and blamelessness “for the day of Christ.” Paul isn’t describing a self-improvement project. He’s describing a life oriented toward eternity, lived in light of the certainty that Christ will return and we will stand before him. This should free us from both complacency and anxious perfectionism. We pursue holiness not to earn favor, but because we already belong to the One who is coming again, and we long to be found faithful, resting entirely in his righteousness credited to us.

Real love, then, is not soft-headed. It’s wide awake—alert to what honors God and what quietly erodes faith. This is a love worth praying for, both for ourselves and for those we love.

Confessional Tie-in:

“The Spirit of Christ subduing and enabling their will to do that which is spiritually good… yet so as they that do good and eschew evil, not from a legal spirit, but from a right spirit.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter 16 (adapted reference to sanctified obedience)

Prayer Prompt:

Father, thank you for loving me first, before I knew how to love you rightly. Forgive me for the times I’ve confused sentiment for true love, or let my affections drift toward what is comfortable rather than what is excellent. Grow in me a love that is anchored in truth, sharpened by your Word, and aimed toward the day I see Christ face to face.

Go Deeper:

This week, ask the Spirit to show you one relationship or decision where your love needs more discernment—not less feeling, but clearer thinking. Read Philippians 1:1-11 in full to see how Paul’s prayer flows from his own deep affection for the church, and consider: what would it look like for your love to “approve what is excellent” in a specific choice you’re facing today?
