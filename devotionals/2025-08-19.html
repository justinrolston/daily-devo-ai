Whoever has the Son has life; whoever does not have the Son of God does not have life.
1 John 5:12 (ESV)

The Difference Maker: Life Through the Son

Reflection

What does this text teach us? John presents us with one of Scripture’s most profound yet simple declarations: spiritual life is found exclusively in Jesus Christ. The apostle doesn’t offer a complex formula or multiple pathways—he draws a clear line. Those who have the Son possess life; those who don’t have the Son lack life entirely.

The word “life” here isn’t merely biological existence, but zoe—the very life of God Himself. This is eternal life that begins the moment we are united to Christ, not just life that extends forever. It’s qualitatively different from natural life, marked by communion with God, freedom from sin’s dominion, and the indwelling presence of the Holy Spirit.

Notice John’s emphasis on “having” the Son. This isn’t about intellectual acknowledgment of Jesus or cultural Christianity. To have the Son means to be united to Him by faith, to belong to Him as completely as a branch belongs to the vine. It’s a relationship of intimate possession—Christ possesses us, and we possess Him.

The stark contrast John presents confronts our culture’s inclusive sensibilities, yet it reflects the reality of spiritual death. Apart from Christ, people may appear alive—successful, moral, even religious—but they lack the essential element that constitutes true life before God. They remain dead in trespasses and sins, unable to please God or inherit eternal life.

For believers, this truth brings profound assurance. Our spiritual life doesn’t fluctuate based on our performance, feelings, or circumstances. Because we have Christ, we have life—settled, secure, and permanent. When doubts arise about our standing before God, we need not look inward to our inconsistent hearts but upward to our consistent Savior.

This reality should also fuel our evangelistic urgency. If people without Christ truly lack spiritual life, then sharing the gospel isn’t merely offering improvement but resurrection. We’re not peddling self-help but proclaiming the only source of authentic spiritual life.

The exclusivity of Christ shouldn’t make us prideful but grateful. We possess life not because we’re superior but because God graciously opened our spiritually dead hearts to receive His Son.

Confessional Tie-in

“All those whom God hath predestinated unto life, and those only, He is pleased, in His appointed and accepted time, effectually to call, by His Word and Spirit, out of that state of sin and death, in which they are by nature to grace and salvation, by Jesus Christ.” — Westminster Confession of Faith 10.1

Prayer Prompt

Father, thank You that in Your Son we have found true life—not just existence, but Your own divine life flowing through us. Help us rest securely in this truth when doubts arise, and grant us compassionate urgency to share this life-giving message with those who remain spiritually dead without Christ.

Go Deeper

Consider how this truth affects your daily confidence before God and your concern for unbelieving friends and family. Spend time today thanking God specifically for the spiritual life you possess in Christ, then ask Him to show you opportunities to share this life with others who desperately need it.
