November 24, 2025

[Alive in Christ] Therefore, as you received Christ Jesus the Lord, so walk in him, rooted and built up in him and established in the faith, just as you were taught, abounding in thanksgiving.
Colossians 2:6-7 (ESV)

Walking in the Life You’ve Received

Reflection

What does this text teach us? Paul begins with “therefore,” pointing us back to the magnificent truths he’s just proclaimed about Christ’s supremacy and our completeness in Him. Having received Christ Jesus as Lord—not merely as Savior, but as the sovereign ruler of our lives—we are now called to “walk in him.” This walking isn’t a desperate attempt to earn God’s favor, but rather the natural expression of the new life we’ve already received.

The imagery Paul uses is beautifully layered. We are “rooted” like a tree drawing nourishment from rich soil, “built up” like a house constructed on a solid foundation, and “established” like something made firm and immovable. All three metaphors point to the same glorious reality: our spiritual vitality and stability come not from our own efforts, but from our union with Christ.

Notice the progression here. Our walk flows from our position. Because we have received Christ, we walk in Him. Because we are rooted in Him, we grow upward. Because we are built on His foundation, we become increasingly stable. This is the beauty of gospel-centered living—our doing flows from our being, our activity from our identity.

Paul adds that we should be “abounding in thanksgiving.” This isn’t merely polite gratitude, but an overflowing response to grace. When we truly grasp what we’ve received in Christ—forgiveness, righteousness, adoption, eternal security—thanksgiving becomes as natural as breathing. It’s both the fuel and the fruit of faithful Christian living.

The phrase “just as you were taught” reminds us that this walk isn’t based on our feelings or cultural trends, but on objective truth passed down through faithful teaching. The gospel we received is the same gospel that sustains us daily. We don’t graduate from the basic truths of Christianity; we go deeper into them.

This passage confronts our tendency toward spiritual restlessness. We often look for the next experience, method, or insight to revolutionize our faith. But Paul says the secret to spiritual vitality is found in the Christ we’ve already received. The same Jesus who saved us is sufficient to sustain, establish, and satisfy us each day. Our growth comes not from adding to Christ, but from drawing more deeply from the inexhaustible well of His grace.

Confessional Tie-in

“They who are effectually called do in this life partake of justification, adoption, and sanctification, and the several benefits which in this life do either accompany or flow from them.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter 11, Article 1

Prayer Prompt

Father, thank You for the incredible gift of Christ Jesus my Lord. Help me to walk today rooted in His love, built up by His truth, and established in the faith I’ve received. Fill my heart with overflowing thanksgiving for Your grace.

Go Deeper

Consider this week: How does remembering what you’ve already received in Christ change your approach to spiritual growth? Instead of striving to achieve God’s favor, spend time meditating on the riches that are already yours in union with Him.
