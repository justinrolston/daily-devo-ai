September 07, 2026

Him we proclaim, warning everyone and teaching everyone with all wisdom, that we may present everyone mature in Christ.
Colossians 1:28 (ESV)

Proclaimed, Warned, Taught, Presented

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Paul writes from prison, describing the singular focus of his ministry: Christ. Not a program, not a technique, but a person—”Him we proclaim.” Everything Paul does flows from this center. He warns everyone, teaches everyone, and does so with wisdom, all aimed at one goal: presenting each person mature, complete, whole in Christ.

Notice the word “everyone” appearing three times. Paul wasn’t selective in his ministry, reserving deep truth for an elite few. The gospel is for all, and spiritual maturity is the aim for all who believe—not just pastors, missionaries, or those who seem naturally devout. If you are in Christ, this verse describes God’s intention for you.

Also notice that warning and teaching go together. Growth in Christ isn’t only about comfort and encouragement, though it includes both. Sometimes maturity requires the loving warning of a friend, a text of Scripture that convicts, or a season of discipline that reshapes us. Paul doesn’t separate truth from love; he weds them together “with all wisdom.”

This maturity Paul describes isn’t something we manufacture through willpower or moral effort. It is presented—the language of a gift given, an offering made complete. Our sanctification, like our justification, has its source outside ourselves, in the finished work of Christ and the ongoing work of His Spirit. Yet this doesn’t make us passive. Paul labored, “struggling with all his energy” (v. 29), for this very purpose. God’s sovereign work and our faithful effort are not enemies; they are partners in the mystery of grace.

Where are you in this process today? Perhaps you feel stuck, wondering if real change is possible. Or perhaps you’re weary from wrestling with sin. Take heart: the same Christ who justified you is at work perfecting you. You are not presenting yourself—you are being presented, held by nail-scarred hands that will not let you go.

Confessional Tie-in:

“Sanctification is a work of God’s free grace, whereby the whole man is renewed after the image of God, and are enabled more and more to die unto sin, and live unto righteousness.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Q. 35

Prayer Prompt:

Father, thank You for not leaving me as I am, but conforming me daily to the image of Your Son. Forgive me for resisting the warnings and teaching You send my way through Your Word and Your people. Continue Your patient work in me, and grant me the perseverance to labor toward maturity, trusting that You who began this good work will complete it.

Go Deeper:

This week, ask a trusted friend or mentor to speak an honest word into your life—a warning, an encouragement, or both. Reflect on Philippians 1:6 alongside this passage: how does knowing God will “complete” His work change how you respond to unfinished growth in your own life?
