[Take Up Your Cross and Follow Jesus] And he said to all, “If anyone would come after me, let him deny himself and take up his cross daily and follow me. For whoever would save his life will lose it, but whoever loses his life for my sake will save it.
Luke 9:23-24 (ESV)

The Daily Cross: Following Jesus in Self-Denial

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Jesus makes an extraordinary claim here—that anyone who wishes to follow Him must engage in three specific actions: deny themselves, take up their cross daily, and follow Him. This isn’t merely advice for the spiritually mature; it’s the basic requirement for discipleship itself.

The imagery of cross-bearing would have been viscerally familiar to Jesus’ first-century audience. They regularly witnessed condemned criminals carrying their execution devices through the streets. When Jesus speaks of taking up our cross “daily,” He’s calling us to a daily dying—a continuous surrender of our self-centered desires, ambitions, and will to His lordship.

This self-denial isn’t merely giving up chocolate for Lent or avoiding certain sins. It’s the fundamental reorientation of our entire existence around Christ rather than ourselves. We naturally live as if we are the center of our universe, making decisions based on what serves our comfort, reputation, or pleasure. Gospel discipleship reverses this entirely.

The paradox Jesus presents cuts to the heart of human nature: those who seek to preserve their life—living for themselves, their own glory, their own plans—will ultimately lose what they’re trying so desperately to protect. But those who “lose” their life for Christ’s sake discover true life. This isn’t merely about martyrdom, though it may include that. It’s about the daily choice to live for Christ’s kingdom rather than our own.

This call reveals both our desperate need for grace and God’s transforming power. We cannot simply decide to stop being self-centered any more than we can choose to stop breathing. Yet the same Christ who calls us to take up our cross is the One who first carried His own cross for our salvation. He doesn’t ask us to earn His love through self-denial; rather, because He has already secured our salvation, we can now freely choose the path of discipleship.

The Christian life isn’t about self-improvement or moral effort—it’s about daily surrender to the One who has already accomplished everything necessary for our eternal life. In losing ourselves in Him, we discover who we were always meant to be.

Confessional Tie-in: “The chief end of man is to glorify God, and to enjoy him forever.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Q&A 1

Prayer Prompt: Lord Jesus, You carried Your cross for my salvation; now help me carry mine for Your glory. Grant me grace to deny my selfish desires today and to find my true life in following You, even when the path leads through difficulty.

Go Deeper: Consider one specific area where you’re currently living for yourself rather than Christ. What would it look like to “take up your cross” in that situation today? Meditate on how Christ’s cross-bearing for you motivates and enables your cross-bearing for Him.
