In this is love, not that we have loved God but that he loved us and sent his Son to be the propitiation for our sins.
1 John 4:10 (ESV)

Love’s True Origin

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? John presents us with a stunning reversal of our natural understanding of love. The apostle declares that true love is not measured by our affection toward God, but by His prior and perfect love toward us. This isn’t merely a correction of our thinking—it’s a complete redefinition of love itself.

The phrase “in this is love” points to love’s very essence and origin. Love, in its truest form, begins not with human emotion or decision, but with God’s character and action. Before we ever thought to seek Him, before we could even comprehend our need, God loved us. This love wasn’t stirred by anything lovely in us, for we were dead in our trespasses and sins. Rather, it flowed from His own nature, for “God is love.”

The magnitude of this love is revealed in its cost: God “sent his Son to be the propitiation for our sins.” Propitiation means that Christ fully satisfied God’s just wrath against our sin. This wasn’t merely God overlooking our failures or deciding to be lenient. Justice demanded payment, and love provided it through the sacrifice of His beloved Son. The cross demonstrates both God’s holiness, which cannot tolerate sin, and His love, which refuses to abandon sinners.

This truth transforms how we understand our relationship with God. We don’t earn His love through our devotion, church attendance, or moral improvement. We don’t maintain it through our consistency or lose it through our failures. His love precedes and secures everything else in our Christian experience.

For daily Christian living, this means our service flows from gratitude, not guilt. Our obedience springs from joy, not fear of abandonment. When we fail, we don’t question whether God still loves us—His love was established before we were even born. When we succeed, we don’t imagine we’ve finally become worthy of His affection—we were chosen in His love from eternity past.

This foundational truth should humble the proud and comfort the broken. Are you striving to earn God’s approval? Rest in His prior love. Are you discouraged by your spiritual failures? Remember that His love sent Christ before you ever loved Him. This is the solid ground on which all Christian growth stands.

Confessional Tie-in: “We love him, because he first loved us. This love was not in us, but in God, and is the fountain and cause of all the love wherewith we love God and our neighbor.” — Heidelberg Catechism, Question 65

Prayer Prompt: Father, thank You that Your love for me doesn’t depend on my love for You. Help me to rest in the security of Your prior love and let this truth transform how I serve and obey You today. May my life overflow with gratitude for Christ’s propitiating sacrifice.

Go Deeper: Consider specific areas where you’ve been trying to earn God’s approval. How might resting in His prior love change your approach to prayer, service, or dealing with sin? Meditate on how this understanding of love should shape your relationships with others who also need to experience love that gives before it receives.
