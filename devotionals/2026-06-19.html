Husbands, love your wives, as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her, that he might sanctify her, having cleansed her by the washing of water with the word...
Ephesians 5:25-26 (ESV)

Love That Gives Itself Away

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Paul roots the command for husbands to love their wives not in cultural convention or personal preference, but in the very pattern of Christ’s love for his church. This is love defined by sacrifice: Christ “gave himself up for her.” Not love as feeling only, but love as action—costly, deliberate, self-giving action.

Notice the purpose clause: Christ gave himself up “that he might sanctify her.” His sacrifice was never merely to rescue and release, but to cleanse and transform. He washes his bride “by the water with the word”—an image evoking baptism, but pointing to something deeper: the ongoing work of Scripture and Spirit making the church holy. Christ doesn’t just save us from something; he saves us for something—a purified, radiant people bearing his likeness.

This passage speaks first and foremost about Christ and his church. The marriage application flows from that greater reality, not the reverse. Husbands don’t earn the right to be loved-like-Christ by their own merit; they’re called to reflect a love already perfectly accomplished for them. This matters, because none of us—husband or wife, married or single—loves like this naturally. Our love is often self-protective, conditional, quick to withdraw when costly. Left to ourselves, we love in order to receive, not to give until it hurts.

But here is good news: Christ loved us while we were unlovely, unclean, unfaithful. He didn’t wait for us to become presentable. He gave himself up so that we might become presentable. If you feel far from sanctified today—still wrestling with the same sins, still stumbling—remember that his washing is not a one-time event but a continual work. He is patient with his bride.

For every believer, married or not, this text calls us to consider: Do we love others—spouse, friend, neighbor—expecting them to earn it? Or do we reflect the self-giving love that first transformed us? Christ’s love for the church is both our comfort and our pattern.

Confessional Tie-in:

“Q. 1: What is your only comfort in life and death? A. That I am not my own, but belong—body and soul, in life and in death—to my faithful Savior, Jesus Christ, who at the cost of his own blood has fully paid for all my sins.” — Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 1

Prayer Prompt:

Lord Jesus, thank you for loving your church—loving me—not because I was worthy, but because you chose to give yourself up for my cleansing and holiness. Forgive me for the many ways I love conditionally, seeking my own comfort rather than the good of others. Teach me today to love as you have loved, sacrificially and patiently, trusting your Spirit to do in me what I cannot do myself.

Go Deeper:

Consider one relationship this week where love has grown conditional or tired. Ask: what would it look like to love there as Christ loved the church—giving without demanding return? For further meditation, read Philippians 2:5-8 alongside this passage, and reflect on how Christ’s humility shapes the pattern for all Christian love.
