Jesus answered them, “This is the work of God, that you believe in him whom he has sent.”
John 6:29 (ESV)

The Work That Isn’t Work

John 6:29 - “Jesus answered them, ‘This is the work of God, that you believe in him whom he has sent.’”

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? When the crowd asked Jesus what works they must do to accomplish the works of God, they expected a list of religious duties. Instead, Jesus turned their understanding upside down with a profound truth: the work God requires is belief in His Son.

This isn’t work in the ordinary sense. The crowd was thinking of earning God’s favor through human effort—following rules, performing rituals, or achieving moral excellence. But Jesus reveals that God’s “work” is entirely different. It’s trusting in the One God has sent, receiving rather than achieving, believing rather than striving.

Notice Jesus doesn’t say “works” plural, but “work” singular. God requires one thing: faith in Christ. This faith isn’t merely intellectual agreement but wholehearted trust in Jesus as our Savior and Lord. It means acknowledging that we cannot save ourselves and casting ourselves completely upon His finished work.

Yet even this faith is God’s gift, not our contribution. When Jesus calls belief “the work of God,” He’s pointing to God as both the author and object of our faith. We cannot manufacture genuine faith any more than we can earn salvation through good deeds. God must open our hearts to believe, just as He opened Lydia’s heart in Acts 16.

This transforms how we approach the Christian life. Instead of anxious striving to prove our worth to God, we rest in Christ’s perfect righteousness credited to us. Instead of fearful uncertainty about our standing, we enjoy the confidence that comes from trusting in God’s promises rather than our performance.

This doesn’t lead to passivity. True faith always bears fruit in good works, but these flow from gratitude, not desperation. We serve God not to earn His love but because we already have it in Christ. We obey not from fear of rejection but from joy in our acceptance.

When you feel burdened by religious expectations or overwhelmed by spiritual inadequacy, remember Jesus’ words. God doesn’t demand perfect performance but perfect trust. He’s not looking for flawless servants but faithful believers who rest in His perfect Son.

Confessional Tie-in: “We are saved by faith alone, which is the instrument by which we take hold of Christ our righteousness.” — Belgic Confession, Article 22

Prayer Prompt: Father, thank You that salvation isn’t about my work but Your work through Christ. Strengthen my faith when doubt creeps in, and help me rest in Your finished work rather than my failing efforts.

Go Deeper: Meditate on how this truth changes your daily relationship with God—do you approach Him as an employee seeking approval or a child enjoying acceptance? Consider reading Ephesians 2:8-10 to see how faith and works relate in the Christian life.
