And whatever you do, in word or deed, do everything in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God the Father through him.
Colossians 3:17 (ESV)

Living in Christ’s Name: The Heart of Christian Living

Reflection

What does it mean to do everything “in the name of the Lord Jesus”? Paul’s instruction in Colossians 3:17 isn’t merely about adding Jesus’ name to our prayers or ending conversations with “God bless.” This verse calls us to something far more profound—living under Christ’s authority, for His glory, and by His power in every aspect of our lives.

The phrase “in the name of” carries the weight of representation and authority. When we act in someone’s name, we act as their representative, carrying their reputation and reflecting their character. Paul is telling us that whether we’re speaking to a difficult coworker, washing dishes, or making major life decisions, we do so as Christ’s ambassadors, clothed in His righteousness and empowered by His Spirit.

This comprehensive vision of Christian living flows from our union with Christ. Because we belong to Him, there is no sacred-secular divide in our lives. The mundane becomes meaningful, the ordinary becomes an offering. Our work is worship, our words are witness, and our deeds are demonstrations of God’s grace.

Notice that Paul connects this all-encompassing obedience with thanksgiving to God the Father. Gratitude isn’t an afterthought—it’s the heartbeat of faithful living. When we recognize that every breath, every opportunity, and every ability comes from God’s gracious hand, thanksgiving naturally follows. This gratitude transforms our perspective, helping us see our daily tasks not as burdens but as privileges to serve the One who first served us.

Yet we must be honest about our failures. How often do we compartmentalize our lives, living for Christ on Sunday but for ourselves on Monday? How frequently do our words wound rather than heal, or our actions bring dishonor to His name? The beauty of this verse is that it points us not to our performance but to Christ Himself. We can do all things in His name precisely because He has done everything necessary for our salvation.

The call to live entirely for Christ’s glory isn’t meant to burden us with impossible standards, but to liberate us from the futility of self-centered living. In Christ, even our smallest acts of service carry eternal significance.

Confessional Tie-in

“Man’s chief end is to glorify God, and to enjoy him forever.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Question 1

Prayer Prompt

Lord Jesus, thank You that I can live under Your name and authority. Help me to represent You faithfully in both my words and actions today. Forgive me for the times I’ve brought dishonor to Your name, and fill me with gratitude that transforms even ordinary moments into offerings of worship.

Go Deeper

Before your next conversation or task today, pause and ask: “How can I do this in a way that honors Christ’s name?” Consider keeping a gratitude journal this week, noting specific ways God has provided for you and opportunities He’s given you to serve others.
