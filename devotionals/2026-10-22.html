To make an apt answer is a joy to a man, and a word in season, how good it is!
Proverbs 15:23 (ESV)

The Gift of Timely Words

Title: The Gift of Timely Words

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Solomon observes a simple but profound truth: there is deep joy in speaking the right word at the right moment. The Hebrew idea behind “apt answer” suggests a response that fits perfectly—like a key turning smoothly in a lock. And “a word in season” pictures ripened fruit, ready at exactly the right time. Proverbs consistently teaches that wisdom is not abstract knowledge but skillful living, and nowhere is this skill more tested than in our speech.

This verse reminds us that words carry weight. Scripture teaches that out of the heart the mouth speaks, and that the tongue has the power of life and death. Our speech is never neutral—it reveals what we treasure and it shapes the people around us. Because of sin, our natural tendency is toward careless, self-serving, or harmful words. Left to ourselves, we speak too quickly, too harshly, or not at all when courage is needed. This is why timely, fitting speech is not merely a social skill but a fruit of grace, cultivated by the Spirit who gives us wisdom we do not naturally possess.

Think of Christ himself, the perfect embodiment of this proverb. He spoke truth to the woman at the well, comfort to the grieving sisters at Lazarus’s tomb, correction to proud religious leaders, and forgiveness to a dying thief. Every word was perfectly suited to the moment—because he is Wisdom itself, speaking with perfect love and perfect truth together.

For us, growth in godly speech is part of ongoing sanctification. It requires listening before speaking, discerning what others actually need, and trusting God to give us words when we feel inadequate. Sometimes the “word in season” is correction, sometimes comfort, sometimes silence. The joy described here isn’t self-congratulation—it’s the quiet delight of having been useful in God’s hands, an instrument of grace to another soul.

Confessional Tie-in:

“The duty which God requireth of man, is obedience to his revealed will… which duty is summarily comprehended in the ten commandments.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Q. 39–40 (applied to the ninth commandment’s call for truthful, edifying speech)

Prayer Prompt:

Lord, you are the source of all wisdom, and I confess that my words often fall short—careless, harsh, or silent when I should speak. Thank you for Christ, whose every word was perfect grace and truth. Give me ears to listen well and a heart attuned to your Spirit, so my words might bring life to someone today.

Go Deeper:

Before your next difficult conversation, pause and pray simply: “Lord, give me the word this person needs.” Reflect on a time someone’s timely word changed your day—then ask God for opportunities to be that voice for another. Consider memorizing Ephesians 4:29 alongside this verse as a daily filter for your speech.
