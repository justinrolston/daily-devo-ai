I have been crucified with Christ. It is no longer I who live, but Christ who lives in me. And the life I now live in the flesh I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave himself for me.
Galatians 2:20 (ESV)

The Life That Is Not Our Own

Reflection

What does this remarkable text teach us? Paul makes three profound declarations that reveal the heart of Christian existence. First, “I have been crucified with Christ” - this speaks of our union with Christ in His death. When Christ died on the cross, we who belong to Him died with Him. Our old nature, our rebellion against God, our very identity as sinners was nailed to that cross. This is not merely symbolic but a spiritual reality that has already taken place.

Second, Paul declares a paradox: “It is no longer I who live, but Christ who lives in me.” The apostle is very much alive as he writes these words, yet he insists he no longer lives. What has died is the old Paul - the Pharisee who trusted in his own righteousness, who persecuted the church, who lived for his own glory. In place of that old self, Christ Himself has taken up residence. This is the mystery of our union with Christ - He doesn’t merely help us live better lives; He becomes our very life.

Third, Paul explains how this works practically: “The life I now live in the flesh I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave himself for me.” Though we still inhabit physical bodies and face daily struggles, we now live by an entirely different principle - faith in Christ rather than confidence in ourselves. This faith is not a work we perform but a looking away from ourselves to the One who demonstrated His love through self-sacrifice.

This transforms everything about daily Christian living. When we face temptation, we remember that our old self died with Christ and we now have His power within us. When guilt overwhelms us, we recall that Christ lives in us - we are accepted not based on our performance but on His perfect righteousness. When we feel inadequate for the tasks before us, we draw upon His strength rather than our own resources.

The Christian life is not about trying harder to be good; it’s about recognizing that Christ Himself is our life. Every moment becomes an opportunity to live by faith in this glorious reality, trusting not in our own love for God but in His unchanging love for us, demonstrated at the cross.

Confessional Tie-in

“They who are effectually called and regenerated, having a new heart and a new spirit created in them, are further sanctified, really and personally, through the virtue of Christ’s death and resurrection, by his Word and Spirit dwelling in them.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter XIII, Article 1

Prayer Prompt

Lord Jesus, help me to grasp more fully what it means that my old self died with You and that You now live in me. When I’m tempted to rely on my own strength or goodness, remind me that You are my life and my righteousness. Thank You for loving me enough to give Yourself for me.

Go Deeper

Consider specific areas where you’re still trying to “live” in your own strength rather than by faith in Christ’s life within you. Spend time meditating on what it means that Christ’s love motivated His self-sacrifice for you personally. How might this truth change your approach to both your struggles and your service today?
