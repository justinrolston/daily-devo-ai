March 21, 2026

“Blessed is the man who trusts in the LORD, whose trust is the LORD. He is like a tree planted by water, that sends out its roots by the stream, and does not fear when heat comes, for its leaves remain green, and is not anxious in the year of drought, for it does not cease to bear fruit.”
Jeremiah 17:7-8 (ESV)

Planted by the Water

Reflection

What does this text teach us? Jeremiah presents us with a striking contrast between two ways of living. In verses 5-6, he describes the cursed man who trusts in human strength, comparing him to a shrub in the desert wasteland. But here in verses 7-8, we encounter the blessed person whose trust rests entirely in the LORD. This isn’t merely about having general religious faith, but about placing our complete confidence in God’s character, promises, and provision.

The metaphor Jeremiah uses is both beautiful and instructive. The blessed person is like a tree planted by water—not accidentally growing there, but deliberately positioned by God’s sovereign hand near His streams of grace. Notice the progression: because this tree’s roots extend toward the stream, it possesses an unshakeable stability that external circumstances cannot touch.

This passage reveals a profound truth about the Christian life. When we truly trust in the LORD, our spiritual vitality doesn’t depend on favorable conditions around us. The heat will come—trials, disappointments, seasons of spiritual dryness—but these cannot wither the soul that draws its life from God Himself. While others may panic during “drought years,” the believer continues bearing fruit because their nourishment comes from an inexhaustible source.

But what does it mean practically to trust in the LORD? It means believing that God’s word is more reliable than our feelings, that His promises are more certain than our circumstances, and that His love is more constant than human approval. It means turning to Him first in crisis rather than scrambling for human solutions. It means finding our identity in His unchanging character rather than our changing performance.

The fruit-bearing mentioned here isn’t forced productivity through gritted teeth, but the natural overflow of a life connected to God’s life-giving grace. When we abide in Christ, drawing deeply from His finished work and ongoing presence, we cannot help but reflect His character and accomplish His purposes—even in difficult seasons.

This tree doesn’t fear the heat because it knows where its life comes from. Similarly, when our trust rests securely in the LORD, we can face uncertainty with confidence, knowing that our God who saved us will also sustain us.

Confessional Tie-in

“Those whom God effectually calls he also freely justifies; not by infusing righteousness into them, but by pardoning their sins, and by accounting and accepting their persons as righteous; not for anything wrought in them, or done by them, but for Christ’s sake alone.” — Westminster Confession of Faith 11.1

Prayer Prompt

Father, plant me deeper by Your streams of grace. Help me trust You completely, not just in easy seasons but especially when the heat of trials comes. Nourish my roots in Your word and promises so I might bear fruit that honors You.

Go Deeper

Examine your response to current pressures—are you drawing from God’s resources or scrambling for human solutions? Memorize verse 7 as a daily reminder of where true blessing is found. Consider: what “streams” has God provided for your spiritual nourishment, and how can you position yourself more intentionally near them?
