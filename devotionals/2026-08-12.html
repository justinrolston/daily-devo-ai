August 12, 2026

Or do you not know that your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit within you, whom you have from God? You are not your own, for you were bought with a price. So glorify God in your body.
1 Corinthians 6:19-20 (ESV)

Bought, Indwelt, and Free

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Paul is writing to a church wrestling with sexual immorality, tempted to believe that what they did with their bodies was spiritually irrelevant. His answer cuts to the heart: your body is not your own private territory—it is a temple, a dwelling place where the Holy Spirit himself resides. This is a staggering claim. In the Old Testament, God’s presence filled the tabernacle and temple in Jerusalem, set apart by elaborate rituals of holiness. Now, through Christ, God’s Spirit dwells within ordinary believers. The place of worship is no longer a building—it’s you.

Notice the reasoning Paul uses: “you are not your own, for you were bought with a price.” This is the gospel applied to the body. Christ’s blood didn’t just purchase your soul for some future heaven; it purchased all of you—your hands, your eyes, your desires, your time. Because we are naturally self-owners by nature, prone to using our bodies for whatever serves our appetites, this truth confronts our deepest instincts. We don’t drift naturally into bodily holiness; we drift away from it. That’s why Paul doesn’t appeal to willpower alone but to identity—you belong to Another.

This has immense practical weight. How you spend your body—in rest, in intimacy, in food, in labor, in recreation—is not a neutral, private matter. It is worship or it is rebellion. Yet this truth isn’t meant to crush you with anxious self-monitoring. It’s meant to free you. The Spirit who indwells you is not a harsh landlord inspecting for violations, but the very presence of God, sanctifying you patiently, conforming you increasingly to Christ. Glorifying God in the body is not a burden believers carry alone—it is the fruit of a Spirit already at work within.

Confessional Tie-in: “Q. What is your only comfort in life and death? A. That I am not my own, but belong—body and soul, in life and in death—to my faithful Savior Jesus Christ.” — Heidelberg Catechism, Question 1

Prayer Prompt: Lord, I confess I often treat my body as my own possession rather than your dwelling place. Thank you for purchasing me completely through Christ’s blood, and for sending your Spirit to live within me. Teach me today to honor you with how I rest, speak, and use my body, trusting your Spirit to sanctify what I cannot perfect on my own.

Go Deeper: Consider one specific area—sleep, screen use, food, speech, sexuality—where you tend to forget your body belongs to God. Ask: does this choice reflect that I am bought and indwelt, or that I am my own? Spend a moment in quiet gratitude, remembering that the Spirit’s presence in you is not merely a rule to keep but a gift to receive.
