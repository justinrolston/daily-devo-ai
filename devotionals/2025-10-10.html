Listen to advice and accept instruction, that you may gain wisdom in the future. Many are the plans in the mind of a man, but it is the purpose of the LORD that will stand.
Proverbs 19:20-21 (ESV)

God’s Purpose Above Our Plans

Reflection:

What does this passage teach us about wisdom and God’s sovereignty? Proverbs 19:20-21 presents us with two interconnected truths that should shape how we approach decision-making and life planning as believers.

The first verse calls us to “listen to advice and accept instruction.” This isn’t merely about being teachable—it’s about recognizing our fundamental need for wisdom beyond ourselves. In a culture that celebrates self-reliance and trusting our instincts, Scripture reminds us that true wisdom comes from outside ourselves. The Hebrew word for “instruction” here carries the idea of discipline and correction, suggesting that gaining wisdom often requires humble submission to truths that may challenge our natural inclinations.

But whose advice should we seek? While godly counsel from mature believers is invaluable, the ultimate source of wisdom is God’s Word itself. When we “listen to advice,” we must filter all human counsel through the lens of Scripture, recognizing that even well-meaning advisors can lead us astray if their guidance contradicts biblical truth.

The second verse provides the essential foundation for verse 20: “Many are the plans in the mind of a man, but it is the purpose of the LORD that will stand.” This isn’t meant to discourage planning—Scripture actually commends wise planning elsewhere. Rather, it reminds us that our plans must be held with open hands, submitted to God’s sovereign will.

We make our plans, but God establishes our steps. This truth should bring both humility and comfort to believers. Humility, because it reminds us that we are not the ultimate architects of our destiny. Comfort, because it assures us that when our carefully laid plans crumble, God’s purposes remain firm and good.

This doesn’t mean we become passive or fatalistic. Instead, we plan diligently while acknowledging God’s ultimate authority over outcomes. We seek wise counsel while remembering that human wisdom, apart from God’s truth, leads to foolishness. We make decisions with the information we have while trusting that God will work through both our successes and our apparent failures to accomplish His good purposes.

Living in light of these truths transforms how we approach both major life decisions and daily choices, anchoring us in God’s character rather than our circumstances.

Confessional Tie-in: “We believe that this good God, after he created all things, did not abandon them to chance or fortune, but leads and governs them according to his holy will, in such a way that nothing happens in this world without his orderly arrangement.” — Belgic Confession, Article 13

Prayer Prompt: Father, grant me wisdom to seek godly counsel and humility to receive instruction. Help me make plans according to Your will, holding them loosely while trusting completely in Your sovereign purposes for my life.

Go Deeper: Consider a current decision you’re facing—how might you better seek wise, biblical counsel? Reflect on a time when your plans didn’t work out as expected, and ask how you can see God’s good purposes even in disappointment.
