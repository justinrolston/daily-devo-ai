For if we live, we live to the Lord, and if we die, we die to the Lord. So then, whether we live or whether we die, we are the Lord's.
Romans 14:8 (ESV)

Belonging to the Lord in Life and Death

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Paul writes these words in the context of a dispute among believers in Rome over disputable matters—food, holy days, personal convictions. Rather than settling every argument, Paul pulls the camera back to a larger truth: our lives are not our own. Whether we are considering how to spend a Tuesday afternoon or facing the reality of our final breath, we belong to Christ. This is not a vague sentiment but a concrete claim of ownership. The word translated “we are the Lord’s” carries the weight of possession—we have been bought, claimed, and are now under new management.

This truth flows from the gospel itself. We did not save ourselves, and we do not sustain ourselves. Christ purchased us through his death and resurrection, and that purchase covers every square inch of our existence—our bodies, our decisions, our days, and yes, our deaths. Total dependence on grace does not end at conversion; it characterizes the whole of the Christian life, including its final chapter.

This is deeply practical. Many of us organize our lives around the illusion of self-ownership—my time, my body, my future, my plans. But Paul dismantles this illusion gently and firmly. If you live, live for him. If you suffer, suffer under his care. If you die, you die into his hands, not into the void.

This truth comforts the grieving believer who wonders if death has the final word—it does not; the Lord does. It also confronts the comfortable believer who has quietly reclaimed ownership over areas of life not yet surrendered to Christ’s lordship. Sanctification means increasingly living as those who know we are not our own, moment by moment, decision by decision, breath by breath.

Confessional Tie-in:

“That I am not my own, but belong with body and soul, both in life and in death, to my faithful Savior Jesus Christ.” — Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 1

Prayer Prompt:

Lord, I confess how often I live as though my life were my own to direct and protect. Thank you for the assurance that I belong to you completely, in life and in death, bought by the blood of Christ. Teach me today to surrender areas I have quietly withheld, and give me peace in knowing my future rests safely in your hands.

Go Deeper:

Consider one area of your life—a relationship, a habit, a fear about the future—where you have functionally acted as your own owner rather than Christ’s. Bring it honestly before him today. For further meditation, read Philippians 1:20–21 and reflect on how Paul’s confidence in belonging to Christ shaped his view of both living and dying.
