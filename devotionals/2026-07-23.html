...but Jesus said, “Let the little children come to me and do not hinder them, for to such belongs the kingdom of heaven.”
Matthew 19:14 (ESV)

Come, Little Ones: The Kingdom Belongs to Such as These

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? In first-century Jewish culture, children held little social status—they were loved, but not typically regarded as worthy of a rabbi’s time or attention. So when parents brought their children to Jesus for a blessing, the disciples reacted predictably: they saw this as an interruption, a distraction from “real” ministry to important adults. But Jesus rebukes them sharply. “Let the little children come to me,” He says, “for to such belongs the kingdom of heaven.”

This is not sentimental affection for children’s innocence—Scripture is clear that all humans, including children, are born in sin (Psalm 51:5). Rather, Jesus is teaching something profound about the nature of His kingdom itself. Children, in their vulnerability and dependence, illustrate the very posture required to receive grace. They bring nothing to earn a place in the kingdom. They cannot achieve, negotiate, or impress their way into blessing. They simply come, empty-handed, trusting.

This is the gospel in miniature. None of us enter the kingdom through merit, status, or spiritual accomplishment. We enter as children—dependent, needy, receiving what we could never earn. Jesus elsewhere says we must “receive the kingdom of God like a child” (Mark 10:15). This isn’t about childlike naivety, but childlike trust: an admission that we bring nothing and Christ supplies everything.

For believers, this text offers deep comfort. Do you feel unqualified, overlooked, too weak or ordinary for God’s notice? Jesus’s rebuke to His disciples reminds us that He delights precisely in receiving those the world dismisses. His arms are open to the weak, the dependent, the small.

This truth also shapes how we treat children and all vulnerable people in our lives and churches. If Jesus welcomed children with such tenderness, His people should reflect that same welcome—valuing the young, the weak, and the overlooked as image-bearers precious to God.

Confessional Tie-in: “Q. 74: Are infants also to be baptized? Yes: for since they, as well as their parents, belong to the covenant and people of God… they must also be baptized as a sign and seal of the covenant of grace.” — Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 74

Prayer Prompt: Father, thank You for welcoming the weak and dependent into Your kingdom through Christ alone. Forgive me when I rely on my own strength rather than coming to You with childlike trust. Help me to welcome others—especially the vulnerable and overlooked—the way Jesus welcomed the children.

Go Deeper: Consider a specific area of your life where you’re striving to “earn” God’s favor rather than simply coming to Him in trusting dependence. This week, look for practical ways to welcome and bless children or vulnerable people in your church or community, reflecting Christ’s own heart toward them.
