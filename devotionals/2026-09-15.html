So we have come to know and to believe the love that God has for us. God is love, and whoever abides in love abides in God, and God abides in him.
1 John 4:16 (ESV)

Resting in the Love That Never Changes

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? John writes not as a distant theologian but as an eyewitness who walked with Jesus, watched Him weep, and saw Him die. When he says believers have “come to know and believe” God’s love, he describes a settled confidence, not a fleeting feeling. This knowledge rests on something outside ourselves—the historical reality of Christ’s incarnation, death, and resurrection, described just verses earlier: “In this is love, not that we have loved God but that he loved us and sent his Son to be the propitiation for our sins” (1 John 4:10).

Notice the order: God’s love comes first. We don’t earn our way into His affection through spiritual performance or moral improvement. Before we loved Him, He loved us—decisively, sacrificially, at the cross. This is the bedrock of Christian assurance. Our standing with God doesn’t fluctuate with our emotional temperature or our latest failure. It rests on what Christ has already accomplished.

But John doesn’t stop at doctrine—he moves to communion. “Whoever abides in love abides in God, and God abides in him.” This is the language of relationship, of dwelling, of home. To abide means to remain, to stay rooted, the way a branch remains in a vine. This isn’t a one-time transaction but an ongoing life shared with God Himself.

For those weighed down by guilt or exhausted by trying to feel “close enough” to God, this text offers rest: your abiding isn’t sustained by your grip on God, but by His grip on you. Yet this truth also confronts complacency—if we claim to abide in God while harboring bitterness or indifference toward others, John has already told us such love is not genuine (1 John 4:7-8, 20). Real abiding produces real love, not as a burden, but as fruit growing naturally from a life connected to its source.

Where do you need to rest today in the settled truth of God’s love? Where might that love be calling you to open your hands toward someone else?

Confessional Tie-in:

“What is your only comfort in life and death? That I am not my own, but belong—body and soul, in life and in death—to my faithful Savior, Jesus Christ.” — Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 1

Prayer Prompt:

Father, thank You that Your love for me was settled at the cross before I ever turned toward You. Forgive me for the moments I try to earn what You’ve already freely given, and for the times I withhold love from others while claiming to abide in You. Teach me today to rest in Your love and let it overflow toward those around me.

Go Deeper:

Take a moment to identify one relationship where love feels difficult right now. Ask God to help you extend to that person even a small measure of the grace He has lavished on you. For further meditation, read 1 John 4:7-21 in full, noting each place where love and knowledge of God are directly connected.
