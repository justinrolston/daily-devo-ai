“Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God, the LORD is one. You shall love the LORD your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your might.
Deuteronomy 6:4-5 (ESV)

The Greatest Commandment: Love with Everything You Are

Reflection

What does this foundational text teach us? The Shema, as this passage is known, stands as Israel’s central confession of faith and remains equally vital for Christians today. It begins with a call to attention—”Hear, O Israel”—demanding our focused concentration on what follows. The declaration that “the LORD our God, the LORD is one” establishes the fundamental truth of monotheism in a world filled with competing gods and divided loyalties.

This unity of God goes deeper than mere numerical oneness. The Hebrew word “echad” suggests a complex unity—God is undivided in His essence, perfect in His attributes, and singular in His deserving of our worship. In light of the Trinity, we understand that our one God exists in three persons, yet remains perfectly unified in purpose, character, and glory.

The command that flows from this truth is comprehensive: we must love God with all our heart, soul, and might. This isn’t merely emotional affection, but the Hebrew concept of love that encompasses loyalty, commitment, and wholehearted devotion. Every aspect of our being—our emotions, will, mind, and strength—belongs to Him.

Notice the totality demanded here. We cannot compartmentalize our faith, loving God on Sundays while living for ourselves the rest of the week. Our heart (the center of our emotions and will), our soul (our very life and being), and our might (our strength and resources) must all be oriented toward Him.

Yet we must be honest about our failure. None of us loves God as we ought. Our hearts are divided, our souls distracted, our might often directed toward selfish ends. This is where the gospel brings hope—Christ perfectly fulfilled this command, loving the Father with undivided devotion, and His righteousness becomes ours through faith.

As believers, the Spirit works within us to increasingly align our affections with God’s glory. This love grows not through willpower alone, but through deepening understanding of God’s character and grace toward us in Christ. The more we grasp His love for us, the more our hearts are captivated by His worth.

Living out this command means examining where our ultimate allegiances lie and surrendering every competing loyalty to His lordship.

Confessional Tie-in

“Man’s chief end is to glorify God, and to enjoy him forever.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Question 1

Prayer Prompt

Father, You alone are worthy of my complete devotion. Forgive me for the ways I’ve loved You with divided loyalty, and by Your Spirit, unite my heart to fear Your name and love You above all else.

Go Deeper

Examine your schedule, finances, and thought life this week—do they reflect wholehearted love for God? Consider memorizing the Shema as a daily reminder of your fundamental calling as His child.
