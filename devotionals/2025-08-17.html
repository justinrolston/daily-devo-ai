August 17, 2025

“Let the one who boasts, boast in the Lord.” For it is not the one who commends himself who is approved, but the one whom the Lord commends.
2 Corinthians 10:17-18 (ESV)

The Lord’s Approval Alone

Reflection

What does this text teach us? Paul concludes his defense against false apostles with a striking principle: true boasting belongs to the Lord alone, and genuine approval comes only from Him. The apostle quotes from Jeremiah 9:24, where God declares that if anyone must boast, let them boast in knowing and understanding the Lord who exercises steadfast love, justice, and righteousness.

This passage exposes the futility of self-commendation. The false teachers troubling the Corinthian church were masters of self-promotion, touting their credentials, eloquence, and achievements. But Paul reveals the poverty of such boasting. Human approval, even our own assessment of ourselves, carries no ultimate weight. What matters is divine approval—being commended by the One who sees the heart and judges with perfect knowledge.

The contrast Paul draws is profound: self-commendation versus divine commendation. Those who commend themselves are not “approved”—a word that means tested and found genuine, like precious metals refined by fire. Only God can truly test and approve His servants because only He knows the heart’s true motivations and the genuine fruit of our labors.

This truth strikes at the root of our natural pride. We instinctively seek recognition, validation, and approval from others. We craft our image, highlight our successes, and subtly promote ourselves. Yet Paul calls us to a radically different way—boasting only in the Lord and seeking His approval alone.

What does it mean to boast in the Lord? It means recognizing that every good thing in our lives flows from His grace. Our talents, opportunities, successes, and even our faith itself are gifts from above. When we boast in the Lord, we magnify His goodness rather than our own achievements.

Living for God’s approval rather than human applause transforms everything. It frees us from the exhausting treadmill of performance and comparison. It enables us to serve faithfully in obscurity, knowing that our heavenly Father sees in secret and will reward openly. It also humbles us in success, recognizing that “every good gift and every perfect gift is from above.”

The question for us today is simple yet searching: Whose approval are we ultimately seeking?

Confessional Tie-in

“The chief end of man is to glorify God, and to enjoy him forever.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Question 1

Prayer Prompt

Lord, forgive me for seeking the approval of others more than Your approval. Help me to boast only in You, recognizing that every good thing in my life comes from Your gracious hand. Free me from the need to commend myself and grant me contentment in Your perfect assessment of my heart.

Go Deeper

Consider the areas where you most naturally seek human approval or are tempted toward self-promotion. How might your ministry, work, or relationships change if you truly lived for God’s approval alone? Meditate on what it means that the God who knows you completely still loves you perfectly in Christ.
