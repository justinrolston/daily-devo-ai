When Everything Else Fails

Psalm 73:25-26 (ESV) “Whom have I in heaven but you? And there is nothing on earth that I desire besides you. My flesh and my heart may fail, but God is the strength of my heart and my portion forever.”

Reflection

What does this text teach us? The psalmist Asaph makes one of Scripture’s most profound declarations of complete devotion to God. This isn’t merely religious sentiment—it’s a hard-won confession born from struggle. Earlier in Psalm 73, Asaph nearly stumbled when he saw the prosperity of the wicked and questioned God’s justice. But after entering God’s sanctuary and gaining divine perspective, his heart is transformed.

Notice the comprehensive nature of his declaration: “in heaven” and “on earth”—covering all of reality. Asaph acknowledges that God alone satisfies the deepest longings of his soul. This isn’t asceticism that denies God’s good gifts, but rather proper ordering that recognizes all earthly blessings as secondary to the Giver himself.

The second verse confronts our mortality honestly. “My flesh and my heart may fail”—not “if” but “may,” acknowledging the certainty of human frailty. Our bodies weaken, our emotions fluctuate, our minds grow dim. Yet in the face of inevitable decline, Asaph finds unshakeable hope: God remains “the strength of my heart and my portion forever.”

The word “portion” is rich with covenant meaning—God is not just our helper but our inheritance, our share, our treasure. While others may find their security in wealth, relationships, or achievements, the believer’s ultimate security rests in God’s unchanging character and eternal promises.

This truth transforms how we face life’s inevitable disappointments and losses. When health fails, when relationships fracture, when dreams crumble, we’re not left empty-handed. Our deepest joy doesn’t depend on circumstances but on our relationship with the eternal God who never fails.

Yet this isn’t mere stoicism—it’s vibrant faith. To say “there is nothing on earth that I desire besides you” doesn’t mean we love nothing else, but that we love everything else properly through our supreme love for God. When He is our greatest treasure, all other gifts find their proper place.

This psalm challenges our divided hearts. What competes with God for our ultimate affection? What do we turn to when life gets difficult? Asaph’s confession invites us to the same radical reorientation of our desires.

Confessional Tie-in

“What is your only comfort in life and death? That I, with body and soul, both in life and in death, am not my own, but belong to my faithful Savior Jesus Christ… Therefore, by his Holy Spirit, he also assures me of eternal life, and makes me heartily willing and ready from now on to live for him.” — Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 1

Prayer Prompt

Father, you are my ultimate treasure and portion forever. Forgive me for the times I’ve sought satisfaction in temporary things rather than finding my joy in you. When my flesh and heart fail, remind me that you remain my strength and my inheritance through all eternity.

Go Deeper

Consider what you turn to first when facing stress or disappointment—does it reveal divided loyalties in your heart? Meditate on God’s unchanging character as your eternal portion, and ask how this truth might change your perspective on current challenges or losses.
