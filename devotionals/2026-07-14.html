If you keep my commandments, you will abide in my love, just as I have kept my Father's commandments and abide in his love.
John 15:10 (ESV)

Abiding Love, Obedient Hearts

Title: The Path That Leads to His Heart

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Jesus speaks these words on the night of His betrayal, drawing His disciples into the intimacy He shares with the Father. He tells them plainly: obedience is not the price of His love, but the pathway of dwelling in it. Notice the pattern He sets—”just as I have kept my Father’s commandments and abide in his love.” Jesus Himself models what He calls His disciples to do. His entire life was one of perfect, joyful submission to the Father’s will, and this obedience was not a burden that strained their relationship but the very expression of a love that never wavered.

This matters deeply for how we understand our own walk with God. We do not obey in order to earn love we don’t yet have. In Christ, we are already loved with an everlasting love, secured not by our performance but by His finished work on the cross. Yet Scripture never separates being loved from walking in obedience. To abide—to remain, to dwell, to stay close—is to experience the love that is already ours in a felt, tangible, daily way. Sin does not sever our union with Christ, but it does disrupt our communion with Him. Think of a child who never doubts a parent’s love, yet still feels the warmth of that relationship most fully when walking in harmony with the household, not rebellion against it.

This is the quiet, daily invitation of the Christian life: not striving to be loved, but resting in love already given, and letting that rest produce a heart that delights to obey. Where have you drifted—not from your standing in Christ, but from the felt nearness of His presence? Obedience, rightly understood, is not restriction but restoration—the road back to conscious fellowship with the One who has never let you go.

Confessional Tie-in: “Good works are only such as God hath commanded in his holy Word… yet their ability to do good works is not at all of themselves, but wholly from the Spirit of Christ.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter 16

Prayer Prompt: Father, thank You that Your love for me rests on Christ’s obedience, not my own. Forgive me for the ways I have wandered from close fellowship with You this week. Teach me, by Your Spirit, to walk in glad obedience—not to earn Your love, but to dwell more fully within it.

Go Deeper: Read John 15:1–17 in full to see this verse in the context of the vine and branches. Ask yourself: is there a specific area of disobedience creating distance in your daily communion with God? Bring it to Him honestly today, and ask the Spirit to renew in you a love for His commands.
