One Family in Christ

Galatians 3:26-28 (ESV) “…for in Christ Jesus you are all sons of God, through faith. For as many of you as were baptized into Christ have put on Christ. There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither slave nor free, there is no male and female, for you are all one in Christ Jesus.”

Reflection

What does this remarkable passage teach us about our identity as believers? Paul reveals a breathtaking truth: through faith in Christ Jesus, we have been adopted as God’s children and brought into one unified family that transcends every earthly division.

The phrase “sons of God” uses legal adoption language from Paul’s time. In the ancient world, adopted sons received full inheritance rights—they became heirs with complete family privileges. Through faith alone, God has granted us this same status. We are not second-class citizens in His kingdom but beloved children with full access to our heavenly Father.

Paul then connects this adoption to our baptism, explaining that we “have put on Christ.” Like putting on new clothes, baptism symbolizes our complete identity change. We are clothed with Christ’s righteousness, bearing His name and sharing His inheritance. This is not merely symbolic but describes our actual spiritual reality—we are united to Christ Himself.

The revolutionary nature of this unity becomes clear in verse 28. In Christ, the barriers that divided the ancient world—ethnicity, social status, and gender—no longer determine our spiritual standing. Jews and Gentiles, slaves and free persons, men and women all share equal status as God’s adopted children. This doesn’t erase our distinct roles or gifts, but establishes our fundamental equality before God.

Consider how this truth should reshape our daily interactions. When we encounter fellow believers, we’re not primarily seeing their earthly characteristics but family members who share our adoption. The executive and janitor at your church, the elderly saint and young convert, believers from different cultures—all are equally “sons of God” through the same faith that saved you.

This unity also confronts our natural tendency toward favoritism and prejudice. If God shows no partiality in salvation, adopting people from every background into His family, how can we show favoritism toward some of our spiritual siblings while neglecting others? Our common identity in Christ calls us to love, serve, and fellowship with all believers as the family we truly are.

Confessional Tie-in

“All those that are justified, God vouchsafeth, in and for his only Son Jesus Christ, to make partakers of the grace of adoption, whereby they are taken into the number, and enjoy the liberties and privileges of the children of God.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter 12.1

Prayer Prompt

Father, thank You for adopting me into Your family through faith in Christ. Help me to see and treat all my fellow believers as the spiritual siblings they are, regardless of earthly differences. Unite our hearts in the love that flows from our shared adoption.

Go Deeper

Look for opportunities this week to fellowship with believers who are different from you—perhaps different in age, background, or life circumstances. Reflect on how your adoption as God’s child changes how you view yourself and others in the church family.
