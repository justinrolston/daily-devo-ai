And he sat down and called the twelve. And he said to them, “If anyone would be first, he must be last of all and servant of all.”
Mark 9:35 (ESV)

True Greatness: The Upside-Down Kingdom

Reflection

What does this text teach us? Jesus has just witnessed His disciples arguing about who among them was the greatest (Mark 9:34). In response, He gathers them together for one of His most counter-cultural teachings. Christ declares that in His kingdom, greatness is measured not by power over others, but by service to others. To be first, one must choose to be last. To lead, one must serve.

This wasn’t merely moral advice—it was a revelation of God’s character and His kingdom’s values. Throughout Scripture, we see this pattern: God exalts the humble and humbles the proud. The Son of God Himself demonstrated this principle by taking on human flesh, living among sinners, and ultimately dying the death of a criminal for our salvation. Jesus didn’t just teach servanthood; He embodied it perfectly.

The disciples’ desire for greatness revealed the sinful ambition that lurks in every human heart. Even after walking with Jesus, they still craved recognition, status, and power over others. How often do we find ourselves competing for position, seeking recognition, or measuring our worth by comparing ourselves to others? These desires flow from our fallen nature and our tendency to seek glory for ourselves rather than for God.

But Jesus offers us a different path—the way of humble service. In God’s kingdom, the servant is greatest because they most closely resemble their Master. When we serve others, we’re not diminishing ourselves; we’re reflecting the very character of God. This service isn’t driven by duty alone, but by gratitude for Christ’s service to us. Because He became last so that we might be first in God’s eyes, we’re freed to serve others without needing to protect our own status.

This calling to servanthood transforms our relationships—in our families, churches, workplaces, and communities. Instead of asking “What can I gain?” we learn to ask “How can I serve?” Instead of demanding our rights, we consider others’ needs. This doesn’t make us doormats, but rather imitators of Christ who willingly laid down His life for others.

The beauty of Jesus’s teaching is that it liberates us from the exhausting pursuit of worldly greatness while opening the door to true significance—becoming instruments of God’s grace in the lives of others.

Confessional Tie-in

“What is the chief end of man? Man’s chief end is to glorify God, and to enjoy him forever.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Question 1

Prayer Prompt

Father, forgive me for the ways I seek my own glory rather than Yours. Thank You for Jesus, who served perfectly in my place. Transform my heart to find joy in serving others as You have served me, and help me see opportunities today to put others’ needs before my own.

Go Deeper

Look for one specific way to serve someone in your life today—perhaps through a task they dislike or by offering encouragement they need. Reflect on this question: In what areas of your life are you still striving for worldly recognition rather than faithful service? Consider how Christ’s perfect service frees you from the need to prove your worth.
