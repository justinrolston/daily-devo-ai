Older men are to be sober-minded, dignified, self-controlled, sound in faith, in love, and in steadfastness.
Titus 2:2 (ESV)

The Marks of Mature Manhood

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Paul’s instruction to Titus reveals God’s design for older men in the church—not merely those advanced in years, but those who have grown in spiritual maturity. The apostle outlines six essential qualities that should characterize men who have walked with Christ and can now model godliness for younger believers.

“Sober-minded” speaks to mental clarity and sound judgment, free from the intoxication of worldly pursuits or foolish thinking. This isn’t dullness, but the sharp discernment that comes from viewing life through Scripture’s lens. “Dignified” calls for a gravity and respectability that commands respect—not through demanding it, but through consistent godly character that naturally draws honor.

“Self-controlled” reveals the Spirit’s work in taming our fallen nature. Where once passions ruled, now wisdom governs. This self-mastery extends beyond obvious sins to encompass our words, emotions, and desires. It’s the fruit of years spent learning to die to self and live for Christ.

The final three qualities form a trinity of Christian virtues. Being “sound in faith” means holding fast to biblical truth without wavering, having weathered storms that might shake younger believers. “Sound in love” demonstrates that doctrinal fidelity never exists apart from genuine care for God and others—theology must always be wed to doxology and compassion. “Sound in steadfastness” reveals the patient endurance that marks those who’ve learned to trust God’s timing and sovereignty through trials.

These aren’t achievements to boast about, but evidences of God’s transforming grace. Every believer, regardless of age, should aspire to these qualities, knowing that spiritual maturity comes not through human effort but through surrendering to the Holy Spirit’s sanctifying work. The older men who embody these virtues become living testimonies of what God can accomplish in a surrendered life, encouraging the entire church community that transformation is possible.

Confessional Tie-in: “They who are effectually called and regenerated, having a new heart and a new spirit created in them, are further sanctified, really and personally, through the virtue of Christ’s death and resurrection, by his Word and Spirit dwelling in them.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, XIII.1

Prayer Prompt: Father, grow in me the spiritual maturity that honors You and serves Your church. Where I lack self-control, dignity, or steadfastness, work through Your Spirit to conform me to Christ’s image for the good of others and the glory of Your name.

Go Deeper: Consider which of these six qualities needs the most attention in your life, and ask God to show you specific ways to grow in that area. Reflect on how mature believers have influenced your own spiritual journey, and pray about how God might use you to encourage others in their walk with Christ.
