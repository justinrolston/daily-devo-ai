[The Fool Says, There Is No God] To the choirmaster. Of David. The fool says in his heart, “There is no God.” They are corrupt, they do abominable deeds; there is none who does good.
Psalm 14:1 (ESV)

The Folly of Godlessness

Reflection:

What does this text teach us about the human condition apart from God? Psalm 14:1 confronts us with a stark diagnosis of spiritual foolishness. When David writes that “the fool says in his heart, ‘There is no God,’” he’s not primarily addressing intellectual atheism, but practical atheism—living as if God doesn’t matter, doesn’t see, or won’t judge.

The Hebrew word for “fool” (nabal) describes someone morally deficient, not mentally lacking. This fool may acknowledge God’s existence but lives in willful ignorance of His character and claims. The corruption David describes flows naturally from this godless heart posture. When we functionally deny God’s authority, moral chaos follows inevitably.

Notice the progression: heart denial leads to corrupt character, which produces abominable deeds. This isn’t merely about unbelievers—believers can slip into practical atheism when we compartmentalize our lives, keeping God out of our decisions, relationships, or ambitions. We become functional fools when we live as if God’s Word doesn’t apply to our current circumstances.

The sobering phrase “there is none who does good” reminds us that apart from God’s grace, we’re all trapped in this foolishness. This isn’t hyperbole but theological reality. Even our best efforts, when divorced from faith and God’s glory, fall short of true goodness.

Yet this psalm points us toward hope. David’s very ability to identify foolishness implies there’s wisdom to be found. True wisdom begins with the fear of the Lord—acknowledging His reality, authority, and grace. The gospel transforms us from practical atheists into people who live consciously in God’s presence.

For believers, this passage serves as both warning and encouragement. Warning: we must examine our hearts for areas where we’re living as functional fools. Encouragement: Christ has delivered us from the futility of godless living and given us His Spirit to walk wisely.

Today, let’s ask: Where might I be living as a practical atheist? What decisions am I making without reference to God’s will? True wisdom means bringing every thought, plan, and action under His loving lordship.

Confessional Tie-in: “The whole counsel of God, concerning all things necessary for his own glory, man’s salvation, faith, and life, is either expressly set down in Scripture, or by good and necessary consequence may be deduced from Scripture.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, 1.6

Prayer Prompt: Father, forgive me for the times I’ve lived as a practical fool, making decisions without reference to Your will. Grant me wisdom to acknowledge You in all my ways, and transform my heart to delight in Your truth rather than my own understanding.

Go Deeper: Examine one area of your life where you might be functioning as a “practical atheist”—making decisions without considering God’s will. How might bringing that area under Christ’s lordship change your perspective and actions?
