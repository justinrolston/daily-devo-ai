But seek first the kingdom of God and his righteousness, and all these things will be added to you.
Matthew 6:33 (ESV)

Seeking First: The Reordering of Our Deepest Loyalties

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Jesus speaks these words in the middle of a longer teaching on anxiety—about food, clothing, and the basic sustenance of life. He has just told His listeners not to worry as the pagans do, chasing after material security as though it were their ultimate treasure. Then comes the pivot: seek first the kingdom of God and His righteousness. Not seek it eventually, not seek it alongside everything else, but first—as the organizing priority that reorders all lesser loyalties.

This command exposes something true about every human heart: we are worshippers by nature, and we will seek something first. The question is never whether we will have an ultimate concern, but what that concern will be. Left to ourselves, we default to seeking security, comfort, reputation, or control—good things twisted into idols that promise rest but only deliver anxiety.

Notice that Jesus doesn’t simply command better behavior; He points us toward a kingdom and a righteousness that is not fundamentally our own achievement. The righteousness we’re called to seek is ultimately found in Christ, who fulfills all righteousness on our behalf and then works that righteousness into us by His Spirit. We seek what has already been secured for us in the gospel. This is not bootstrapping morality—it is resting in Christ’s finished work while actively pursuing the life that flows from union with Him.

The promise attached—”all these things will be added to you”—is not a formula for prosperity but a Father’s assurance. The God who clothes lilies and feeds sparrows will not neglect His children who seek Him first. This frees us from anxious grasping and invites us into trusting dependence.

Where has anxiety about provision, status, or control quietly become your first pursuit? Seeking the kingdom first doesn’t mean neglecting work, family, or daily responsibilities—it means holding them all under Christ’s lordship, trusting your Father’s care as you do.

Confessional Tie-in:

“Man’s chief end is to glorify God, and to enjoy him forever.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Q.1

Prayer Prompt:

Father, I confess how easily anxiety about my needs crowds out my pursuit of You and Your righteousness. Thank You for the assurance that You already know what I need and care for me as Your child. Teach me today to seek Your kingdom first, trusting You with the rest.

Go Deeper:

Take one specific worry occupying your mind today, and consciously entrust it to your Father in prayer before addressing it practically. Read Matthew 6:25-34 in full context, and ask: what would change in my daily rhythms—work, finances, relationships—if God’s kingdom truly held first place?
