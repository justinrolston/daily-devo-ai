Do not boast about tomorrow, for you do not know what a day may bring.
Proverbs 27:1 (ESV)

The Wisdom of Humility About Tomorrow

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Solomon’s proverb cuts through human presumption with surgical precision: “Do not boast about tomorrow, for you do not know what a day may bring.” This isn’t merely practical advice about planning; it’s a profound theological statement about the nature of God’s sovereignty and our creatureliness.

The Hebrew word for “boast” carries the idea of arrogant self-confidence, the kind that assumes we control our circumstances and future. Solomon exposes the fundamental folly of such thinking—we simply don’t know what tomorrow holds. James echoes this wisdom when he writes, “You do not know what tomorrow will bring. What is your life? For you are a mist that appears for a little time and then vanishes” (James 4:14).

This proverb reveals our complete dependence on God’s providence. Every breath, every heartbeat, every moment of consciousness is a gift from our sovereign Creator. The farmer who boasts of next year’s harvest, the businessman confident in next quarter’s profits, the student certain about graduation—all demonstrate the same fundamental error: forgetting that God alone governs the affairs of men.

Yet this isn’t a call to fatalistic passivity. Scripture consistently encourages wise planning and diligent work. The ant stores food for winter (Proverbs 6:6-8), and Jesus himself spoke of counting the cost before building (Luke 14:28). The difference lies in our heart attitude—do we plan with humble dependence on God’s will, or with arrogant presumption about our control?

This proverb also confronts our anxiety about the future. If we cannot know what tomorrow brings, why exhaust ourselves with worry? Jesus taught us to pray for “daily bread,” not monthly supplies. Our Father knows our needs and promises to provide what we require for faithful obedience each day.

True wisdom holds our plans with open hands, acknowledging that “many are the plans in the mind of a man, but it is the purpose of the Lord that will stand” (Proverbs 19:21). This perspective frees us from both paralyzing anxiety and destructive pride, enabling us to work diligently while resting confidently in God’s perfect timing and provision.

Confessional Tie-in: “God’s works of providence are his most holy, wise, and powerful preserving and governing all his creatures, and all their actions.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Question 11

Prayer Prompt: Father, forgive my prideful presumption about tomorrow. Help me plan wisely while holding my future with open hands, trusting your perfect providence. Grant me freedom from both anxiety and arrogance as I rest in your sovereign care.

Go Deeper: Consider how this wisdom might change your approach to major decisions or sources of worry. Reflect on specific areas where you might be “boasting about tomorrow” rather than humbly depending on God’s provision and timing.
