November 03, 2026

[Submission to the Authorities] Let every person be subject to the governing authorities. For there is no authority except from God, and those that exist have been instituted by God.
Romans 13:1 (ESV)

God’s Hidden Hand Over Every Throne

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Paul writes to believers in Rome—a city ruled by an emperor who would later persecute the church—and tells them to submit to governing authorities. This was not naive political theory. Paul understood Roman power intimately, having faced its courts and chains. Yet he makes an astonishing claim: all authority, even flawed human authority, ultimately derives from God. There is no ruler, government, or institution that exists outside the scope of God’s sovereign ordination.

This connects to a theme woven throughout Scripture: God raises up and brings down kings (Daniel 2:21), uses pagan rulers like Cyrus for His purposes (Isaiah 45:1), and declares that “the king’s heart is a stream of water in the hand of the Lord” (Proverbs 21:1). Nothing—not Caesar, not Congress, not any earthly power—operates beyond the reach of divine providence. This doesn’t mean every ruler is righteous or every law is good. It means God is weaving even imperfect human governance into His purposes for restraining evil and preserving order in a fallen world.

For daily living, this truth offers both comfort and challenge. Comfort, because you are not at the mercy of chaotic, uncontrolled power—God reigns above every headline and every election. Challenge, because submission to authority tests our pride, our desire for control, and our trust in God’s timing. Where do you chafe against structures God has placed over you—whether civil, workplace, or church authority? Such resistance often reveals a deeper resistance to God’s own rule over your life.

This verse doesn’t demand blind obedience to sin (Acts 5:29 shows limits), but it does call believers away from a posture of rebellion and toward quiet trust. Your citizenship is ultimately in heaven, but your witness here matters. A believer marked by respect, prayer for leaders, and peaceable living often shines brighter than one marked by constant outrage.

Confessional Tie-in:

“God, the great Creator of all things, doth uphold, direct, dispose, and govern all creatures, actions, and things, from the greatest even to the least, by His most wise and holy providence.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter 5.1

Prayer Prompt: Lord, You alone reign over every nation and every ruler; I confess my impatience and pride when I resist the authorities You have placed over me. Thank You that Your purposes stand firm even when earthly leaders fail or disappoint me. Teach me to pray for those in authority and to trust Your hidden hand even when I cannot see where history is headed.

Go Deeper: This week, choose one governing authority in your life—a leader, employer, or civic official—and commit to praying for them by name (1 Timothy 2:1-2). Ask yourself honestly: is my submission rooted in trust of God’s sovereignty, or do I only submit when it’s convenient? Consider reading Daniel 4 for a vivid picture of how God humbles the proudest of rulers to declare His supremacy.
