August 12, 2025

Or do you not know that your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit within you, whom you have from God? You are not your own, for you were bought with a price. So glorify God in your body.
1 Corinthians 6:19-20 (ESV)

A Purchased People: Living as God’s Temple

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Paul confronts the Corinthian believers with a stunning reality: their bodies are not their own property to do with as they please. Instead, each Christian’s body is “a temple of the Holy Spirit” – the very dwelling place of God himself. This isn’t merely poetic language; it’s a theological declaration about what happens when someone becomes a believer.

The apostle grounds this truth in the gospel itself: “you were bought with a price.” This purchasing language echoes the marketplace, where slaves were bought and freed, but Paul uses it to describe something far greater. Christ paid the ultimate price – his own blood – to purchase us from sin’s slavery and make us God’s possession. We belong to him by right of redemption.

Notice the progression of Paul’s argument. Because the Holy Spirit dwells within us, and because we were purchased by Christ’s sacrifice, therefore we must “glorify God in your body.” This isn’t burdensome legalism but the natural response of a purchased people. When we truly grasp that our bodies are sacred spaces where God himself resides, it transforms how we treat them.

This truth speaks to every area of our physical existence – what we eat, how we rest, our sexual conduct, how we care for our health, even how we use our hands and tongues. Paul originally applied this principle to sexual immorality, but the implications extend to all bodily life. Our physical choices either honor or dishonor the God who dwells within us.

Yet this passage also brings great comfort. If you struggle with shame about your body or past sins, remember: God has chosen to make his temple in you. The same Spirit who raised Christ from the dead lives in your mortal body. You are not defined by your failures but by Christ’s purchase price.

For believers wrestling with how to live faithfully in a culture that treats the body as merely physical, Paul’s words provide clear direction. We glorify God not through severe asceticism that denies the body’s goodness, nor through indulgence that ignores the body’s sacred purpose, but through grateful stewardship of what belongs to our Redeemer.

Confessional Tie-in:

“The bodies of men, after death, return to dust, and see corruption: but their souls, which neither die nor sleep, having an immortal subsistence, immediately return to God who gave them.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter 32, Article 1

Prayer Prompt:

Lord, thank you that you have made my body your temple through your Spirit’s presence. Help me honor you in how I care for and use this body you’ve purchased with Christ’s precious blood. Grant me wisdom to live as one who truly belongs to you.

Go Deeper:

Consider one specific area of your physical life where you need to better honor God as the owner of your body. Meditate on what it means that the Creator of the universe has chosen to dwell within you through his Spirit.
