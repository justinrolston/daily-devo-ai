[My Soul Waits for God Alone] To the choirmaster: according to Jeduthun. A Psalm of David. For God alone my soul waits in silence; from him comes my salvation.
Psalm 62:1 (ESV)

Finding Rest in God’s Silence

Reflection

What does this text teach us about the nature of genuine faith? David opens this psalm with a profound declaration: “For God alone my soul waits in silence; from him comes my salvation.” The Hebrew word for “waits” here suggests more than passive hoping—it conveys a settled confidence, like a servant who knows his master will provide. This isn’t anxious waiting, but restful expectation.

Notice that David’s soul waits “in silence.” In our noise-filled world, we often equate activity with faithfulness. We think we must constantly petition God, organize solutions, or manufacture outcomes. Yet David models something radically different: the discipline of spiritual stillness. This silence isn’t empty; it’s full of trust. It’s the quieting of our striving hearts before the God who needs no prompting to accomplish His perfect will.

The phrase “God alone” strikes at the heart of our struggle with idolatry. David isn’t merely saying God is his primary hope among many options—he’s declaring God as his exclusive source of deliverance. We live in a culture that offers countless alternative saviors: financial security, human relationships, career success, or political solutions. While these aren’t necessarily evil, they become destructive when we look to them for the ultimate satisfaction and security that only God provides.

“From him comes my salvation” reminds us that deliverance is God’s work, not ours. The Hebrew word for salvation encompasses both spiritual redemption and practical rescue from life’s troubles. David understood that whether facing external enemies or internal turmoil, his help originated entirely from heaven.

This psalm confronts our anxiety and restlessness with a better way. Instead of frantically working to secure our futures or manipulate our circumstances, we’re invited into the rest of faith. This doesn’t mean passivity in our responsibilities, but rather a deep assurance that our ultimate security rests in God’s character and promises, not in our efforts or earthly circumstances.

When we truly grasp that God alone is our salvation, our souls can find that sacred silence David describes—not the silence of despair, but the quiet confidence of children who know their Father’s love is sure.

Confessional Tie-in

“This faith is not only a certain knowledge, whereby I hold for truth all that God has revealed to us in his word, but also an assured confidence, which the Holy Ghost works by the gospel in my heart; that not only to others, but to me also, remission of sins, everlasting righteousness, and salvation, are freely given by God, merely of grace, only for the sake of Christ’s merits.” — Heidelberg Catechism, Question 21

Prayer Prompt

Father, quiet my restless heart before You today. Help me to cease my striving and find my rest in Your perfect character and promises. Teach me to wait on You alone, trusting that my salvation and daily provision flow entirely from Your gracious hand.

Go Deeper

Practice five minutes of silent prayer today, simply resting in God’s presence without requests or words. Consider what “alternative saviors” you might be tempting to trust alongside God. How might cultivating this holy silence change your approach to current anxieties or challenges?
