[My Help Comes from the LORD] A Song of Ascents. I lift up my eyes to the hills. From where does my help come? My help comes from the LORD, who made heaven and earth.
Psalm 121:1-2 (ESV)

When Anxiety Rises, Where Do You Look?

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? This psalm opens a collection known as the Songs of Ascents, sung by pilgrims traveling up to Jerusalem for worship. The journey was not safe. Hills surrounding the roads could hide bandits; steep terrain threatened injury; the ancient world associated high places with pagan shrines where false gods were worshiped. So when the psalmist lifts his eyes to the hills, he asks a question loaded with real anxiety: from where does my help come?

The answer is immediate and unshakable: help comes from the LORD—Yahweh, the covenant God who does not change—”who made heaven and earth.” This is not vague spirituality or wishful thinking. It is a declaration that the One who holds our circumstances is the same One who spoke galaxies into existence. If God has the power to create all things, He certainly has the power to sustain the one who trusts Him.

This truth confronts our natural instinct to seek help from lesser sources—our own strength, financial security, human relationships, or endless self-improvement. Scripture consistently teaches that these things, while not evil in themselves, cannot bear the weight we place on them. Only the Creator can sustain the creature. This is total dependence, not as a burden, but as glorious relief: we were never meant to be our own saviors.

For the believer in Christ, this psalm points forward to a deeper reality. Jesus himself lifted his eyes toward a hill—Calvary—where he secured help for us that we could never provide ourselves. Our justification rests entirely on his finished work, not our vigilance or performance. And now, in daily sanctification, we continue to lift our eyes, not to our own resources, but to the same faithful God who raised Christ from the dead.

Where are you looking today? What “hills” loom over your life—health concerns, financial pressure, relational conflict, spiritual dryness? The psalmist’s answer redirects your gaze from the size of the problem to the sufficiency of the Helper.

Confessional Tie-in:

“What is thy only comfort in life and death? That I with body and soul, both in life and death, am not my own, but belong unto my faithful Saviour Jesus Christ, who… so preserves me that without the will of my heavenly Father not a hair can fall from my head; yea, that all things must be subservient to my salvation.” — Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 1

Prayer Prompt:

Lord, you alone made heaven and earth, and you alone sustain my life today. Forgive me for the times I’ve looked to my own strength or worldly securities instead of to you. Thank you for the help secured through Christ; help me now to lift my eyes to you in every anxious moment.

Go Deeper:

Take a few moments today to name the specific “hills” causing you worry, then speak Psalm 121:2 aloud as a direct response to each one. For further study, read all of Psalm 121 and notice how the psalm develops—from questioning to confident assurance of God’s constant, personal watchfulness over your life.
