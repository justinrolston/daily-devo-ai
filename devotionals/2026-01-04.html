He has told you, O man, what is good; and what does the LORD require of you but to do justice, and to love kindness, and to walk humbly with your God?
Micah 6:8 (ESV)

What the Lord Requires

Reflection

What does this text teach us? The prophet Micah delivers one of Scripture’s most memorable declarations about God’s expectations for His people. After chapters of calling out Israel’s ritualistic religion divorced from righteousness, Micah crystallizes divine truth: God has already revealed what He requires, and it’s beautifully simple yet profoundly challenging.

Notice the structure of God’s threefold requirement. First, “do justice” - this speaks to our relationships with others, particularly how we treat the vulnerable and marginalized. Justice here isn’t merely legal fairness but active righteousness that reflects God’s own character. We’re called to pursue what is right, even when it costs us personally.

Second, “love kindness” - or as some translations render it, “love mercy.” This Hebrew word hesed captures God’s covenant faithfulness and loyal love. We’re not merely to show kindness when convenient, but to love it, to delight in extending grace and compassion as our default posture toward others.

Third, “walk humbly with your God” - this addresses our relationship with the Almighty Himself. Humility isn’t self-deprecation but right-sizing ourselves before our Creator. It’s recognizing our complete dependence on His grace and walking in ongoing fellowship with Him.

These three requirements work together beautifully. Justice without mercy becomes harsh legalism. Mercy without justice enables sin. Both without humble dependence on God become self-righteous works. But together, they paint a picture of whole-life discipleship.

The gospel empowers this calling. We can pursue justice because Christ secured perfect justice for us at the cross. We can love kindness because we’ve received God’s ultimate kindness in our salvation. We can walk humbly because we know our righteousness comes from Christ alone, not our performance.

This isn’t a burden but a blueprint for flourishing - in our marriages, workplaces, neighborhoods, and churches. When believers embody justice, kindness, and humility, we become living testimonies to the character of our God.

Confessional Tie-in: “The moral law does forever bind all, as well justified persons as others, to the obedience thereof; and that not only in regard of the matter contained in it, but also in respect of the authority of God the Creator who gave it.” — Westminster Confession of Faith 19.5

Prayer Prompt: Lord, You have made Your requirements clear - help me to pursue justice in my relationships, to genuinely love showing kindness to others, and to walk each day in humble dependence upon You. Transform my heart to delight in what You delight in.

Go Deeper: Consider one specific relationship or situation where you could better embody justice, kindness, or humility this week. Reflect on how the gospel’s assurance of your acceptance in Christ frees you to serve others without seeking approval or reward.
