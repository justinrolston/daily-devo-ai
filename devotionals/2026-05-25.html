[Hearing and Doing the Word] Know this, my beloved brothers: let every person be quick to hear, slow to speak, slow to anger...
James 1:19 (ESV)

The Art of Godly Communication

Reflection

What does this text teach us? James presents three interconnected commands that form the foundation of godly communication: be quick to hear, slow to speak, and slow to anger. These aren’t mere social etiquette suggestions—they reflect the character of God himself and reveal how his people should interact with one another.

Being “quick to hear” means more than simply listening to words. It involves humble receptivity to truth, whether from Scripture, wise counsel, or even correction. This requires setting aside our natural defensiveness and pride. When we’re truly quick to hear, we demonstrate the teachable spirit that marks genuine wisdom. We listen not to formulate our response, but to understand deeply.

The command to be “slow to speak” directly counters our flesh’s impulse to defend, explain, or impress. Our words carry weight—they can build up or tear down, encourage or discourage. Slowness to speak allows time for reflection, prayer, and consideration of others’ needs above our own desire to be heard. It acknowledges that our immediate thoughts may not be worth expressing, and that silence can be more valuable than speech.

Being “slow to anger” addresses the heart condition behind our words. Anger often springs from wounded pride, unmet expectations, or the illusion that we deserve better treatment. While righteous anger exists, most of our anger stems from selfishness. Slowness to anger demonstrates trust in God’s sovereignty—believing that he permits circumstances for our good and his glory, even when others sin against us.

These commands work together as a unified approach to relationships. When we listen well, we understand before seeking to be understood. When we speak carefully, our words carry more weight and wisdom. When we control our anger, we create space for the Holy Spirit to work rather than quenching his influence through our flesh.

Notice James addresses “beloved brothers”—this instruction comes within the context of Christian community. Our communication patterns bear witness to the transforming power of the gospel. They demonstrate whether we truly believe God is sovereign, whether we trust his timing, and whether we value others above ourselves as Christ commands.

Confessional Tie-in

“The end of God’s appointing this ordinance [of church discipline] unto us is, that we may appear before the presence of our God pure and without offense, till the day of Jesus Christ.” — Belgic Confession, Article 32

Prayer Prompt

Father, help me to listen with the humility of your Son, speak with the wisdom of your Word, and respond with the patience you’ve shown me. Guard my heart from pride that leads to hasty words and sinful anger.

Go Deeper

This week, practice the “24-hour rule” before responding to challenging conversations—allowing time for prayer and reflection. Consider how these principles might transform one specific relationship where communication has been difficult.
