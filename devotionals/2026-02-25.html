And he said to him, “You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your mind. This is the great and first commandment. And a second is like it: You shall love your neighbor as yourself.
Matthew 22:37-39 (ESV)

The Greatest Commandment: Love’s True Priority

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? When a lawyer tested Jesus by asking about the greatest commandment, our Lord responded with stunning clarity. He quoted Deuteronomy 6:5, declaring that loving God with our entire being—heart, soul, and mind—stands as the supreme commandment. Yet Jesus didn’t stop there. He immediately added a second commandment from Leviticus 19:18: love your neighbor as yourself.

These aren’t merely moral suggestions or religious duties. Jesus presents them as the heart of God’s law, the foundation upon which “all the Law and the Prophets hang” (v. 40). Notice the comprehensiveness of the first commandment—it demands our affections (heart), our spiritual essence (soul), and our rational faculties (mind). God doesn’t want partial devotion; He calls for the totality of our being.

But here’s where we must face an uncomfortable truth: none of us love God or our neighbors perfectly. Our hearts are divided, our souls distracted, our minds clouded by sin. We love ourselves supremely and struggle to love others as we ought. This commandment, rather than merely instructing us, reveals our desperate need for grace.

Yet Christ didn’t simply give us these commandments—He perfectly fulfilled them. He loved the Father with complete devotion, even unto death on a cross. His love for neighbors was so complete that He laid down His life for them. Through faith in Christ, His perfect obedience becomes ours, and His Spirit begins transforming our cold hearts into hearts that can truly love.

This transformation isn’t instantaneous; it’s progressive. The Spirit works within us, gradually reordering our affections so that God becomes our supreme delight and our neighbors become objects of genuine care rather than mere obstacles to our comfort.

Notice too that these commandments are inseparable. We cannot claim to love God while harboring hatred, indifference, or chronic selfishness toward others. John reminds us: “If anyone says, ‘I love God,’ and hates his brother, he is a liar” (1 John 4:20). Our love for God necessarily flows outward in love for those made in His image.

Confessional Tie-in: “The sum of the ten commandments is, to love the Lord our God with all our heart, with all our soul, with all our strength, and with all our mind; and our neighbor as ourselves.” — Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 4

Prayer Prompt: Father, I confess that my love for You and others falls far short of Your holy standard. Thank You that Christ’s perfect love covers my failures. By Your Spirit, continue reshaping my heart to love You supremely and to love my neighbors with the same care I naturally show myself.

Go Deeper: Consider one specific relationship where you struggle to show Christlike love—perhaps someone who irritates you or whom you’ve written off. How might viewing them as made in God’s image change your interactions this week? Reflect also on which aspect of loving God (heart, soul, mind) feels most neglected in your current spiritual life.
