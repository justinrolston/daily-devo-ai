[Lay Up Treasures in Heaven] “Do not lay up for yourselves treasures on earth, where moth and rust destroy and where thieves break in and steal, but lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven, where neither moth nor rust destroys and where thieves do not break in and steal. For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also.
Matthew 6:19-21 (ESV)

Where Your Heart Lives

Reflection

What does this text teach us? Jesus presents us with a fundamental choice about where we will invest our lives. He’s not merely giving financial advice—He’s revealing a spiritual principle that governs the human heart. The contrast Jesus draws is stark: earthly treasures are temporary and vulnerable, while heavenly treasures are eternal and secure.

The imagery Jesus uses would have resonated powerfully with His first-century audience. Moths destroyed fine clothing, rust corroded metals, and thieves broke through mud-brick walls to steal valuables. No earthly security system could guarantee protection. But notice—Jesus doesn’t condemn wealth itself; rather, He warns against the dangerous act of “laying up” treasures on earth, making them our primary focus and source of security.

What exactly are heavenly treasures? They include acts of love, mercy, and justice done in Christ’s name, investments in the eternal welfare of others, and the cultivation of godly character. These treasures cannot be destroyed because they participate in God’s eternal kingdom. They represent the fruit of a heart aligned with God’s purposes rather than worldly ambitions.

The final verse reveals the diagnostic truth: “For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also.” Our investment patterns reveal our heart’s true allegiance. This isn’t primarily about money management—it’s about heart management. Jesus is calling us to examine what captivates our affections and drives our decisions.

The gospel frees us from the anxious pursuit of earthly security. Because Christ has secured our ultimate treasure—eternal life with God—we’re liberated to hold earthly possessions with open hands. We can be generous because our security rests not in our portfolios but in our Savior.

This passage challenges us to regularly audit our hearts. Are we spending our days building something that will outlast moth and rust, or are we pouring ourselves into what will ultimately crumble? The question isn’t whether we’ll store up treasure somewhere—we will. The question is where.

As believers, we’re called to live as citizens of heaven while dwelling on earth, investing our resources, time, and energy in ways that reflect our true citizenship and ultimate hope.

Confessional Tie-in

“The chief end of man is to glorify God, and to enjoy him forever.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Question 1

Prayer Prompt

Lord, search my heart and reveal where my true treasures lie. Help me hold earthly blessings with gratitude but not with grasping hands. Align my investments of time, energy, and resources with Your eternal kingdom, and give me joy in storing up treasures that will last forever.

Go Deeper

This week, honestly evaluate your calendar, bank account, and thought life—what do they reveal about where your heart truly resides? Consider one practical way you can invest in heavenly treasures, whether through generous giving, serving others, or pursuing godly character. Ask yourself: What would change in my daily life if I truly believed that my security comes from Christ alone?
