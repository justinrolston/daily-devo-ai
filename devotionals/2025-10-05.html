“Seek the LORD while he may be found; call upon him while he is near...
Isaiah 55:6 (ESV)

The Urgency of Grace

Reflection

What does this text teach us? Isaiah 55:6 presents us with both a gracious invitation and an urgent command: “Seek the LORD while he may be found; call upon him while he is near.” The prophet’s words carry a divine tension—God is both accessible and sovereignly selective about when He draws near to His people.

The phrase “while he may be found” doesn’t suggest that God plays hide-and-seek with desperate souls. Rather, it acknowledges the reality of divine timing and human responsibility. There are seasons when God’s Spirit moves with particular power, moments when hearts are softened and consciences awakened. The wise believer recognizes these appointed times and responds with urgency.

Notice the parallel structure: seeking and calling, being found and being near. This isn’t mere repetition but biblical emphasis. To seek God means to pursue Him with our whole being—our minds engaged in His truth, our hearts turned toward His character, our wills submitted to His purposes. To call upon Him is to cry out in prayer, acknowledging our desperate need for His mercy and intervention.

But here lies the beautiful mystery of God’s grace: even our seeking is a response to His prior work in our hearts. We seek because He first sought us. We call because He first called our names. The very desire to find God is evidence that He is already drawing us to Himself.

For the believer, this passage speaks both to conversion and to ongoing spiritual renewal. Perhaps you first heard this call years ago when the Spirit convicted you of sin and drew you to Christ. But the call continues throughout the Christian life. There are times when God seems especially near—in corporate worship, during personal crisis, through His Word, or in the quiet moments of prayer. These are not occasions to waste or postpone.

The urgency isn’t born from fear that God will abandon us, for He promises never to leave nor forsake His children. Rather, it springs from wisdom—recognizing that spiritual seasons change, that hearts can grow cold, and that today’s openness to God’s voice is a gift not guaranteed for tomorrow. The writer of Hebrews echoes this urgency: “Today, if you hear his voice, do not harden your hearts.”

Confessional Tie-in

“All those whom God hath predestinated unto life, and those only, he is pleased in his appointed and accepted time effectually to call, by his word and Spirit, out of that state of sin and death, in which they are by nature, to grace and salvation by Jesus Christ.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter 10.1

Prayer Prompt

Father, thank You that You are a God who may be found, who draws near to Your people in mercy. Help me to recognize the seasons when You are especially present and to respond with urgency and faith. Keep my heart tender to Your voice today.

Go Deeper

Consider: What evidence do you see in your life right now that God is drawing near? Set aside extra time this week for seeking God through prayer and Scripture reading, asking Him to reveal areas where your heart may have grown cold. Remember that every spiritual hunger you feel is actually God’s invitation to come closer to Him.
