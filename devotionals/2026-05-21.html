May 21, 2026

[Divisions in the Church] I appeal to you, brothers, by the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, that all of you agree, and that there be no divisions among you, but that you be united in the same mind and the same judgment.
1 Corinthians 1:10 (ESV)

Called to Unity in Christ

Reflection

What does this text teach us? Paul’s appeal to the Corinthian church cuts straight to the heart of Christian community. Notice that his plea comes “by the name of our Lord Jesus Christ” – this isn’t merely Paul’s personal preference or organizational advice. Unity among believers is grounded in the very authority and character of Christ himself.

The apostle’s words reveal something profound about the nature of the church. When Paul calls for believers to “agree” and be “united in the same mind and the same judgment,” he’s not advocating for uniformity in every opinion or preference. Rather, he’s pointing to a deeper unity – one rooted in our shared identity in Christ and our common commitment to the gospel.

The Corinthian church was fracturing along human lines. Some claimed allegiance to Paul, others to Apollos, still others to Peter. Sound familiar? Today’s church often divides over personalities, preferences, or peripheral issues while losing sight of our fundamental unity in Christ. We align ourselves with particular teachers, worship styles, or cultural expressions, forgetting that our primary identity is as brothers and sisters redeemed by the same Savior.

This unity Paul describes isn’t achieved through human effort or clever compromise. It flows from the gospel itself. When we truly grasp that we are all equally sinful, equally in need of grace, and equally justified by faith alone in Christ alone, the grounds for division begin to crumble. Our shared desperate need for mercy and our common hope in Christ’s finished work become the foundation for genuine community.

Yet this unity doesn’t ignore real differences or gloss over sin. Biblical unity actually requires truth-telling, gentle correction, and sometimes difficult conversations. The “same mind” Paul describes is the mind of Christ – one that prioritizes the gospel above personal preferences and seeks the good of others above our own comfort.

Consider your own heart toward fellow believers. Are you more concerned with being right or being reconciled? Do you find yourself gravitating toward division over disputable matters while neglecting the weightier matters of love, mercy, and faithfulness? Christ’s prayer in John 17 was that his followers would be one, so that the world might believe. Our unity – or lack thereof – bears witness to the power of the gospel we claim to embrace.

Confessional Tie-in

“The catholic or universal Church, which is invisible, consists of the whole number of the elect, that have been, are, or shall be gathered into one, under Christ the Head thereof; and is the spouse, the body, the fullness of Him that filleth all in all.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter 25.1

Prayer Prompt

Father, forgive us for the ways we have prioritized our preferences over the unity you desire for your people. Help us to see our brothers and sisters through the lens of Christ’s love, and grant us wisdom to pursue peace without compromising truth. Unite our hearts around the gospel that saves us all.

Go Deeper

Examine your relationships within the church this week – are there any divisions you’ve contributed to that need addressing? Consider memorizing Ephesians 4:3 alongside today’s passage, asking God to show you practical ways to “maintain the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace.”
