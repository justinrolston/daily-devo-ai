[A New Prophet like Moses] “The LORD your God will raise up for you a prophet like me from among you, from your brothers—it is to him you shall listen—
Deuteronomy 18:15 (ESV)

The Prophet We Must Hear

Reflection:

What does this remarkable promise teach us? Moses speaks here of a coming prophet who will be “like him”—raised up by God from among the Israelites, one to whom they must listen. This isn’t merely about the line of prophets that would follow Moses, though it includes them. This is ultimately about the Prophet who would surpass even Moses himself: Jesus Christ.

Moses was the great mediator between God and Israel, the one who received the Law and led God’s people from bondage to freedom. Yet Moses himself acknowledged his limitations and looked forward to one greater. The prophet “like Moses” would be a superior mediator, bringing not just law but grace and truth. Where Moses could only point toward God’s promises, this Prophet would fulfill them.

The phrase “it is to him you shall listen” carries divine authority. This isn’t a suggestion but a command. When God raises up His ultimate Prophet, listening becomes a matter of life and death, blessing and curse. Jesus himself would later say, “My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me” (John 10:27).

How does this challenge us today? In a world filled with competing voices—cultural influencers, political leaders, even well-meaning friends—God calls us to discernment. The question isn’t whether we’re listening, but to whom we’re listening. Are we tuning our hearts primarily to Christ’s voice through His Word, or are we allowing other voices to drown out the Prophet God has given us?

This listening requires intentionality. Just as Israel needed to actively attend to God’s prophet rather than the pagan voices around them, we must cultivate habits that keep us close to Christ’s voice. Daily time in Scripture isn’t just spiritual discipline—it’s survival training for souls bombarded by competing messages.

The beautiful truth is that unlike the distant thunder of Sinai that terrified Israel, our Prophet speaks with the voice of a shepherd. He calls us by name, leads us beside still waters, and guides us in paths of righteousness. His voice brings peace, not terror; hope, not condemnation; life, not death.

Confessional Tie-in: “Christ, as he is anointed with the Holy Ghost, is our Prophet, teaching us the will of God for our salvation.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Answer 24

Prayer Prompt: Lord Jesus, our great Prophet, tune our hearts to hear Your voice above all others. Help us to listen with faith and obedience, knowing that in Your words we find life and peace. Give us discerning hearts that recognize Your voice and follow wherever You lead.

Go Deeper: Consider: What voices compete for your attention each day, and how can you create space to hear Christ more clearly? Reflect on how Jesus fulfills the role of prophet throughout the Gospels, teaching with authority that even Moses couldn’t claim.
