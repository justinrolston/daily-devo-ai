But to all who did receive him, who believed in his name, he gave the right to become children of God, who were born, not of blood nor of the will of the flesh nor of the will of man, but of God.
John 1:12-13 (ESV)

Born of God, Not of Ourselves

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? John has just declared the staggering truth that the eternal Word became flesh and entered the world he made—yet the world did not know him, and his own people did not receive him. But then comes this precious turn: “to all who did receive him, who believed in his name, he gave the right to become children of God.” Here is the gospel in miniature—rejection met with grace, and grace met with a new identity.

Notice carefully how John describes this new birth. It is not produced “of blood”—not inherited through family lineage or religious heritage. It is not “of the will of the flesh”—not the result of human effort, willpower, or self-improvement. It is not “of the will of man”—not something another person can generate in us through persuasion or ceremony. This new birth is “of God” alone. Before we ever reached for him, he reached for us.

This is sobering and liberating at once. Sobering, because it strips away any illusion that we contributed to our own spiritual rebirth. Just as an infant does nothing to cause their own conception or birth, we were spiritually dead until God, in his mercy, made us alive (Ephesians 2:1-5). Liberating, because our standing as God’s children never rested on our performance—it rests entirely on his sovereign, initiating love.

Yet notice the beautiful pairing: those “who believed in his name” are the same ones “born…of God.” Regeneration and faith are not at odds—God’s grace doesn’t bypass our will, it awakens it. He grants new life, and that new life reaches out in genuine faith to receive Christ.

If you are in Christ today, remember: you are not merely forgiven—you are family. You have been given “the right to become children of God.” This truth should steady you when you feel unworthy, humble you when you feel self-sufficient, and fill you with wonder that the God of the universe calls you his own child, not because of your pedigree or effort, but because of his grace alone.

Confessional Tie-in:

“Effectual calling is the work of God’s Spirit, whereby, convincing us of our sin and misery, enlightening our minds in the knowledge of Christ, and renewing our wills, he doth persuade and enable us to embrace Jesus Christ, freely offered to us in the gospel.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Q. 31

Prayer Prompt: Father, I praise you that my adoption into your family rests on your will, not mine. Forgive me for the times I’ve tried to earn what you have freely given, or doubted my place as your child. Thank you for choosing to make me your own—help me live today in the confidence and joy of that identity.

Go Deeper: Spend a few moments today reflecting on 1 John 3:1—”See what kind of love the Father has given to us, that we should be called children of God.” Ask yourself: Do I relate to God more as a servant trying to earn approval, or as a child resting in his love? Consider journaling about one area where you need to remember your identity as his child rather than striving to prove your worth.
