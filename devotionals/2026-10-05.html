“Seek the LORD while he may be found; call upon him while he is near...
Isaiah 55:6 (ESV)

Seek Him While He May Be Found

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Isaiah pens these words to a people in exile, longing for restoration, standing at a crossroads of decision. The prophet issues an urgent invitation: seek the Lord, call upon him—but notice the qualifying phrases. While he may be found. While he is near. This is not a promise that God’s presence is perpetually and casually available on our own timetable. There is an urgency embedded in this verse that demands our attention.

This urgency flows from who God is. He is not a vending machine god, dispensing grace whenever we finally get around to pressing the button. He is the sovereign Lord who, in his kindness, draws near to sinners—and that nearness is itself a gift of grace, not a human achievement. The same God who declares “my thoughts are not your thoughts” (Isaiah 55:8) is the one who stoops to invite rebels into fellowship with himself.

Think of the broader biblical pattern: Noah preached while the ark was being built, but a door was eventually shut. Israel wandered forty years because of unbelief when the land was within reach. Jesus wept over Jerusalem because she did not know the time of her visitation. Scripture consistently reveals that grace, though abundant, is not indefinitely deferred. Today is the day of salvation (2 Corinthians 6:2).

For the believer already in Christ, this verse isn’t primarily about the fear of missing a final chance—our standing rests secure in Christ’s finished work, not our timing. Rather, it’s a call to daily nearness. How easily we let the noise of life create distance in our hearts, even while our position in Christ remains unshaken! The invitation to seek and call is renewed every morning, not because God has moved, but because we so often drift.

Is there a hardness creeping into your heart this week? A delay in prayer, a coldness in worship, a resistance to his Word? Don’t presume upon tomorrow’s softness of heart. Seek him now, in this moment, while he is near.

Confessional Tie-in:

“God requireth of us, that we pray with our whole heart to the only true God…who, without ceasing, giveth all good things to us his creatures.” — Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 116

Prayer Prompt: Lord, you have drawn near to me in Christ when I deserved only distance. Forgive me for the seasons I treat your presence as always available on my terms rather than seeking you with urgency today. Soften my heart now, and teach me to call upon you while you are near.

Go Deeper: Spend a few minutes today in unhurried prayer, not waiting for a “better time” later. Ask the Holy Spirit to reveal any area where you’ve grown presumptuous about tomorrow rather than faithful today. Consider memorizing Isaiah 55:6-7 together, noting how verse 7 ties seeking the Lord to turning from sin and receiving abundant pardon.
