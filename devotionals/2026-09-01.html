Train up a child in the way he should go; even when he is old he will not depart from it.
Proverbs 22:6 (ESV)

Roots That Hold

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Proverbs 22:6 is not a magic formula but a covenantal principle rooted in the wisdom tradition of Israel. The phrase “train up” carries the sense of dedicating or initiating—like consecrating something for its intended purpose. The Hebrew idea behind “in the way he should go” can also be understood as training according to a child’s particular nature or bent, guiding them toward the path God has designed for their good. This is not a guarantee that every child raised in a godly home will never wander, but a proverbial truth: the patterns instilled in youth create grooves that shape a lifetime.

This verse fits within a larger biblical theme—that faith is meant to be transmitted generationally. Deuteronomy 6 commands parents to teach God’s commands diligently, talking of them at home, on the road, morning and night. Psalm 78 speaks of telling “the glorious deeds of the Lord” to the next generation so that they might set their hope in God. Training children is not merely about behavior management; it is about forming hearts to know and love the Lord.

Yet we must hold this text with humility. No parent trains perfectly, and no child responds automatically. We are all born with hearts inclined toward sin, and even the best upbringing cannot manufacture saving faith—that is the Spirit’s sovereign work. This proverb is wisdom literature, not an ironclad promise; it describes the ordinary pattern of God’s moral order, not a contract that removes human freedom or divine grace from the equation.

For parents, this is both comfort and calling: keep planting, keep praying, keep pointing your children to Christ, trusting that God works through ordinary means. For those who feel their upbringing failed them, or whose children have wandered, remember that God’s grace is not bound by our failures. He delights in redeeming prodigals and rewriting stories we thought were finished.

Confessional Tie-in:

“Q. 74. Is baptism also for infants?… For the promise of the Gospel applies to them as much as to their parents… The Holy Spirit works faith, and, before we are conscious of it, confirms it in infants through baptism.” — Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 74 (on covenant nurture and God’s promises to children)

Prayer Prompt:

Lord, thank You for entrusting children—whether our own, or those in our church and community—into our care and instruction. Forgive us for the moments we’ve trained by our own strength rather than leaning on Your grace, and for growing weary or fearful over outcomes we cannot control. Give us faithfulness in the small, daily moments of teaching Your ways, and grant peace to trust You with the results.

Go Deeper:

This week, consider one ordinary rhythm—a meal, a commute, a bedtime routine—where you might intentionally speak of God’s faithfulness to a child in your life. If you are not a parent, ask how you might mentor, disciple, or pray for the next generation in your church family. Reflect: What “training” shaped your own faith, and where do you see God’s grace filling in the gaps your upbringing left behind?
