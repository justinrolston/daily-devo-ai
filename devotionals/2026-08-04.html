“Thus says the LORD who made the earth, the LORD who formed it to establish it—the LORD is his name: Call to me and I will answer you, and will tell you great and hidden things that you have not known.
Jeremiah 33:2-3 (ESV)

The God Who Answers from a Prison Cell

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Jeremiah spoke these words while chained in a prison courtyard, watching Jerusalem crumble under Babylonian siege. The city he loved was days from destruction. Yet in this bleak hour, God identified himself not as a distant deity but as the LORD who made the earth, who formed it, who established it. The same sovereign power that spoke galaxies into existence now speaks tenderly to a suffering prophet: “Call to me and I will answer you.”

This is staggering. The God of creation, the one who needs nothing from us, invites prayer. He doesn’t merely tolerate our cries—he promises to answer them, and not with vague platitudes but with “great and hidden things” beyond our knowledge. This wasn’t a formula for getting what we want; it was a summons to trust the character of the God who holds all things, even when circumstances suggest otherwise.

Notice the order: God first declares who he is before he invites us to call. Prayer isn’t rooted in our eloquence or worthiness but in the character of the One we address. This matters for believers today, especially in seasons when circumstances feel like Jeremiah’s prison—diagnoses, financial collapse, broken relationships, or simply the slow grind of waiting. We don’t pray to a God who might help; we pray to the God who formed the earth and sustains it by his word, the same God who in Christ has drawn near to sinners once far off.

The gospel fulfills this promise supremely. Through Jesus, our great High Priest, we have been given access to call upon God—not as strangers, but as adopted children. The “hidden things” Jeremiah would learn concerned God’s plan to restore his people; the deepest hidden thing revealed to us is Christ himself, “in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge” (Colossians 2:3).

Are you calling on him today, or trying to manage your prison alone?

Confessional Tie-in:

“Prayer is an offering up of our desires unto God, for things agreeable to his will, in the name of Christ, with confession of our sins, and thankful acknowledgment of his mercies.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Q. 98

Prayer Prompt:

Lord, you formed the earth and hold my life in your hands—I confess how quickly I forget your power when trouble comes. Thank you for inviting me to call on you through Christ rather than facing my burdens alone. Teach me to trust you enough to bring my deepest fears to you today, and open my eyes to see your hand at work.

Go Deeper:

Set aside five minutes today to name one “prison” circumstance in your life, and consciously call out to God about it rather than only strategizing on your own. Read Colossians 2:1–3 alongside this passage and consider how the “hidden things” of Jeremiah’s day point forward to the wisdom hidden in Christ. Ask yourself: what would change in my daily rhythm if I truly believed God delights to answer when I call?
