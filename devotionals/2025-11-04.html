...who has made us sufficient to be ministers of a new covenant, not of the letter but of the Spirit. For the letter kills, but the Spirit gives life.
2 Corinthians 3:6 (ESV)

The Life-Giving Ministry of the Spirit

Reflection

What does this text teach us? Paul reminds the Corinthians—and us—of an extraordinary truth: God has made us sufficient to be ministers of a new covenant. Notice that word “sufficient.” Paul isn’t speaking of natural human ability or earned qualification. The Greek word suggests being made adequate or competent by Another. This sufficiency comes entirely from God’s gracious work, not our own preparation or worthiness.

The contrast Paul draws is striking: the old covenant of “the letter” versus the new covenant of “the Spirit.” The old covenant, written on stone tablets, was perfect and holy—but it could only expose sin, not remedy it. It served as a mirror, showing us our desperate need but offering no power to change. This is what Paul means when he says “the letter kills.” The law reveals our spiritual death and condemnation.

But the Spirit gives life! Through Christ’s perfect obedience and atoning sacrifice, we receive not just forgiveness but new spiritual life. The Holy Spirit writes God’s law on our hearts, transforming us from within. Where the old covenant could only condemn, the new covenant justifies, regenerates, and sanctifies.

This has profound implications for how we understand our calling as believers. Every Christian is a minister of this new covenant—not because we’re qualified in ourselves, but because God has made us sufficient. Whether you’re sharing the gospel with a neighbor, encouraging a struggling friend, or serving in your local church, you operate under this same divine sufficiency.

When we feel inadequate for the spiritual challenges before us—and we will—we remember that our competence comes from God. When we’re tempted to minister in the flesh, relying on technique or human wisdom, we return to dependence on the Spirit who gives life. The same power that raised Christ from the dead works in us and through us.

This also transforms how we approach Scripture itself. We don’t read the Bible merely for moral instruction or life principles, but as the Spirit’s instrument for spiritual transformation. The Word, empowered by the Spirit, doesn’t just inform—it regenerates, sanctifies, and gives life to dead hearts.

Confessional Tie-in

“The whole counsel of God, concerning all things necessary for his own glory, man’s salvation, faith, and life, is either expressly set down in Scripture, or by good and necessary consequence may be deduced from Scripture: unto which nothing at any time is to be added, whether by new revelations of the Spirit, or traditions of men.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter 1, Article 6

Prayer Prompt

Father, thank You for making us sufficient ministers of Your new covenant through Christ. Help us depend not on our own abilities but on Your Spirit who gives life. Transform our hearts daily through Your Word and use us as instruments of Your life-giving grace to others.

Go Deeper

Consider one relationship or situation where you feel spiritually inadequate. How might trusting in God’s sufficiency rather than your own abilities change your approach? Meditate on the contrast between law and gospel in your own spiritual journey—where do you still need the Spirit’s life-giving power to replace the deadness of mere rule-keeping?
