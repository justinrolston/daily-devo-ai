“Let the one who boasts, boast in the Lord.” For it is not the one who commends himself who is approved, but the one whom the Lord commends.
2 Corinthians 10:17-18 (ESV)

Whose Approval Are You Seeking?

Title: The Only Commendation That Matters

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Paul writes to a Corinthian church infatuated with credentials—rhetorical skill, impressive résumés, self-promotion. False teachers had crept in, boasting of their qualifications and comparing themselves favorably to Paul, who seemed weak and unimpressive by worldly standards. Into this competitive, ego-driven culture, Paul drops a piercing truth: self-commendation proves nothing. Only the Lord’s approval carries weight.

This strikes at something deep in the human heart. We are wired to seek validation—from parents, peers, employers, even ourselves. Left to our sinful nature, we construct elaborate systems of self-justification, measuring our worth against others and often finding ways to come out ahead in our own estimation. But Scripture exposes this entire enterprise as futile. Jeremiah 9:23-24 anticipates Paul’s words: let not the wise, mighty, or wealthy boast in themselves, but let him who boasts boast in this—that he understands and knows the Lord.

Here is the gospel’s great reversal: we cannot commend ourselves to God through achievement, comparison, or performance. Our righteousness, Isaiah reminds us, is like filthy rags. Yet in Christ, God does something astonishing—he commends us. Not because we’ve earned it, but because Christ’s perfect righteousness is credited to those who trust him. The verdict of “approved” doesn’t originate in our resume; it originates in the finished work of Jesus on our behalf.

This truth liberates us from two deadly temptations: despair and pride. Despair says, “I’ll never measure up.” Pride says, “Look how I measure up.” Both are rooted in self-reference. The gospel says, “Look to Christ.” Your standing before God rests not on your boasting, but on his commending—and if he has declared you righteous through faith, no other verdict matters.

This doesn’t eliminate effort or growth; it reorients its purpose. We no longer serve to be approved, but because we are approved, freeing us to serve without anxious self-promotion or comparison, resting instead in grace that has already spoken the final word over us.

Confessional Tie-in: “Faith, thus receiving and resting on Christ and His righteousness, is the alone instrument of justification: yet is it not alone in the person justified, but is ever accompanied with all other saving graces, and is no dead faith, but worketh by love.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter XI

Prayer Prompt: Lord, forgive me for the many times I’ve sought approval from people rather than resting in your commendation of me in Christ. Thank you that my standing before you depends not on my performance but on Jesus’ perfect righteousness. Teach me today to serve you freely, without the exhausting need to prove myself.

Go Deeper: Notice today when you feel the pull to justify yourself to others or compare your accomplishments to theirs. In that moment, pause and ask: “Whose commendation am I actually seeking?” Consider memorizing Jeremiah 9:23-24 as a companion text, and reflect on how resting in God’s approval might change one specific relationship or pressure you’re facing this week.
