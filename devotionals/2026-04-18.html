For because of this you also pay taxes, for the authorities are ministers of God, attending to this very thing. Pay to all what is owed to them: taxes to whom taxes are owed, revenue to whom revenue is owed, respect to whom respect is owed, honor to whom honor is owed.
Romans 13:6-7 (ESV)

Rendering to God What Is God’s

Reflection

What does this text teach us? Paul concludes his teaching on submission to governing authorities with a practical command: pay what you owe. But notice how he frames this mundane duty—taxes aren’t merely civic obligations but responses to “ministers of God.” This transforms our understanding of earthly authority entirely.

The word “ministers” (leitourgoi) is the same term used for priests serving in the temple. Paul is saying that even secular authorities, knowingly or not, serve God’s purposes in maintaining order and justice. This doesn’t mean every government action is righteous, but that the institution of government itself reflects God’s design for human flourishing.

Paul’s list—taxes, revenue, respect, honor—moves from material obligations to matters of the heart. It’s relatively easy to write a check; it’s harder to maintain a respectful attitude toward authorities whose decisions we question. Yet both are commanded because both reflect our submission to God’s sovereign ordering of society.

This passage echoes Jesus’ words about rendering to Caesar what is Caesar’s and to God what is God’s. But here’s the deeper truth: when we fulfill our earthly obligations with proper motives, we are rendering to God what is God’s. Our civic duties become acts of worship when done in obedience to His Word.

Notice that Paul doesn’t qualify these commands based on whether authorities are wise, just, or even Christian. He writes to believers living under Roman rule—hardly a paragon of biblical virtue. Yet he calls them to faithful citizenship because their ultimate allegiance to Christ shapes how they engage every earthly relationship.

This transforms how we approach everything from tax season to interactions with government officials. Grumbling while we comply misses the point entirely. God calls us to an attitude that recognizes His hand even in secular authority, trusting His sovereignty over all human institutions.

For believers, there are no merely secular duties. Whether paying taxes or showing respect to elected officials, we serve the King of kings through faithful engagement with earthly kingdoms. This doesn’t mean blind compliance when human law contradicts God’s law, but it does mean our default posture is respectful submission rather than reflexive resistance.

Our citizenship in heaven doesn’t exempt us from earthly responsibilities—it transforms them into opportunities for faithful witness.

Confessional Tie-in

“God, the supreme Lord and King of all the world, has ordained civil magistrates, to be, under Him, over the people, for His own glory, and the public good: and, to this end, has armed them with the power of the sword, for the defense and encouragement of them that are good, and for the punishment of evil doers.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter XXIII, Article 1

Prayer Prompt

Lord, help me see Your sovereign hand even in earthly authorities. Grant me wisdom to fulfill my civic duties with a heart that ultimately serves You, trusting Your perfect governance over all human institutions.

Go Deeper

This week, examine your attitude toward civic responsibilities. Are you fulfilling them as unto the Lord, or merely as burdensome obligations? Consider how your approach to earthly authority reflects your understanding of God’s sovereignty over all areas of life.
