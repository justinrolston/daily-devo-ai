In this the love of God was made manifest among us, that God sent his only Son into the world, so that we might live through him.
1 John 4:9 (ESV)

God’s Love Made Visible

Reflection:

What does this text teach us about the nature of God’s love? John declares that God’s love “was made manifest”—it was revealed, displayed, and demonstrated in a way that could not be missed. But notice how this manifestation occurred: not through abstract declarations or philosophical arguments, but through the concrete, historical act of sending “his only Son into the world.”

The phrase “only Son” carries profound weight. This isn’t merely about Jesus being unique among many sons, but about His singular, irreplaceable relationship with the Father. The Greek word monogenes speaks to His one-of-a-kind nature—there is no other like Him, no substitute, no backup plan. When God chose to reveal His love, He held nothing back. He gave what was most precious to Him.

John emphasizes the purpose behind this divine sending: “so that we might live through him.” This phrase exposes our desperate condition. We need life because we are spiritually dead in our trespasses and sins. We cannot generate this life ourselves, earn it, or manufacture it through religious effort. Life comes only “through him”—through Christ’s perfect obedience, His atoning death, and His victorious resurrection.

The word “through” is crucial. Jesus is not merely an example of how to live or a teacher showing us the way. He is the very means by which spiritual life flows to dead souls. Just as physical life requires oxygen flowing through our lungs, spiritual life requires Christ’s righteousness flowing through our union with Him.

This manifestation of love stands in stark contrast to human love, which is often conditional, self-serving, or limited by circumstances. God’s love moved Him to action when we were still His enemies, unable and unwilling to seek Him. His love doesn’t respond to our worthiness; it creates our worthiness through Christ.

For believers struggling with doubt about God’s care, this verse offers rock-solid assurance. When circumstances make you question whether God truly loves you, look to the cross. When feelings fluctuate or life grows dark, remember that God’s love isn’t proven by your comfort but by His sacrifice. The sending of His Son remains history’s greatest love letter, written in blood and sealed with an empty tomb.

Confessional Tie-in: “God, out of his mere good pleasure, from all eternity, elected some to everlasting life, and entered into a covenant of grace, to deliver them out of the estate of sin and misery, and to bring them into an estate of salvation by a Redeemer.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter VII, Article III

Prayer Prompt: Father, thank You for making Your love visible through the gift of Your Son. When I doubt Your goodness or question Your care, remind me of the cross where Your love was demonstrated beyond all question. Help me live today in the confidence of being loved unconditionally through Christ.

Go Deeper: Consider specific moments when you’ve questioned God’s love for you. How does the concrete reality of Christ’s incarnation and sacrifice speak to those doubts? Spend time today meditating on what it cost the Father to send His only Son, and let that truth reshape how you view your circumstances and struggles.
