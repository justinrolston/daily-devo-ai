The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom, and the knowledge of the Holy One is insight.
Proverbs 9:10 (ESV)

Where True Wisdom Begins

Reflection

What does this text teach us? Solomon declares a fundamental truth that shapes how we understand everything else: genuine wisdom doesn’t begin with human reasoning, academic achievement, or life experience. It starts with “the fear of the LORD”—a reverent awe and humble submission to God as our Creator and sovereign King.

This “fear” isn’t cowering terror, but the proper response of creatures before their holy Creator. It’s recognizing that God is infinitely greater than us in power, wisdom, and moral perfection. When we truly grasp who God is and who we are in relation to Him, everything else falls into proper perspective. This reverent fear becomes the foundation upon which all true understanding is built.

The parallel phrase reinforces this truth: “the knowledge of the Holy One is insight.” To know God—not merely know about Him, but to have genuine, personal knowledge of His character—gives us the insight we need to navigate life. This knowledge comes through His Word, where He has revealed Himself most clearly in the person and work of Jesus Christ.

How different this is from our culture’s approach to wisdom! The world tells us to trust our hearts, follow our dreams, or lean on human expertise. But Scripture reveals that apart from knowing God, we’re spiritually blind, stumbling in darkness even when we think we’re enlightened. Our natural minds are clouded by sin, making us prone to self-deception and foolish choices.

This truth should humble us daily. How often do we make decisions based on our own understanding rather than seeking God’s wisdom through prayer and Scripture? How frequently do we lean on cultural values or personal preferences instead of biblical truth?

Yet this verse also offers tremendous hope. God invites us to know Him! Through Christ, we can approach the throne of grace with confidence. The Holy Spirit illuminates our minds to understand spiritual truth. As we grow in knowing God through His Word and prayer, we gain the insight needed for wise living—in our relationships, work, parenting, and every area of life.

True wisdom isn’t about being clever or well-educated. It’s about rightly relating to God, which then enables us to rightly understand ourselves, others, and the world around us.

Confessional Tie-in

“The whole counsel of God, concerning all things necessary for his own glory, man’s salvation, faith, and life, is either expressly set down in Scripture, or by good and necessary consequence may be deduced from Scripture: unto which nothing at any time is to be added, whether by new revelations of the Spirit, or traditions of men.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter 1.6

Prayer Prompt

Father, I confess that too often I’ve sought wisdom apart from You, trusting in my own understanding rather than fearing You as I ought. Grant me a heart that reveres You as holy and sovereign, and help me grow in the knowledge of who You are through Your Word, that I might live with true insight and wisdom.

Go Deeper

Consider one decision you’re currently facing. How might approaching it with reverent fear of the Lord change your perspective or process? Spend time this week meditating on God’s attributes revealed in Scripture, asking Him to deepen your knowledge of His character and increase your awe of His holiness.
