Since we have these promises, beloved, let us cleanse ourselves from every defilement of body and spirit, bringing holiness to completion in the fear of God.
2 Corinthians 7:1 (ESV)

Promises That Purify

Since we have these promises, beloved, let us cleanse ourselves from every defilement of body and spirit, bringing holiness to completion in the fear of God. (2 Corinthians 7:1)

What does this text teach us? Paul’s urgent call to holiness rests on a profound foundation: God’s promises. The “these promises” refers back to the previous verses, where God declares His people to be His dwelling place—that He will walk among us, be our God, and we will be His people. Such breathtaking promises demand a response.

Notice the beautiful tension in this verse. Paul calls us to “cleanse ourselves,” yet this cleansing brings holiness “to completion”—suggesting God has already begun this work. This isn’t salvation by works, but the Spirit-empowered response of grateful hearts. Because God has promised to dwell with us, we’re motivated to pursue purity that honors His presence.

The cleansing Paul describes is comprehensive—affecting both “body and spirit.” Sin’s defilement touches every aspect of our being. Whether it’s what we consume with our eyes, the words we speak, or the attitudes we harbor, nothing escapes sanctification’s reach. This isn’t merely external moralism but heart-level transformation that flows outward.

Paul’s phrase “bringing holiness to completion” reveals sanctification as an ongoing process. We don’t achieve instant perfection, but we’re progressively conformed to Christ’s image. This completion happens “in the fear of God”—not cowering terror, but reverent awe that recognizes His holiness and our privilege as His children.

The driving force behind this pursuit isn’t shame or duty, but love responding to love. God’s promises anchor our identity: we are His beloved people, His temple, His treasured possession. When we truly grasp these promises, holiness becomes not a burden but a joy—the natural overflow of hearts captured by grace.

This passage speaks to our daily struggles with compromise. Whether facing temptation or wrestling with habitual sin, we’re reminded that our pursuit of holiness isn’t self-improvement but covenant faithfulness. We cleanse ourselves because of who we already are in Christ, not to earn His favor.

Confessional Tie-in: “They who are effectually called do in this life partake of justification, adoption, and sanctification, and the several benefits which in this life do either accompany or flow from them.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Question 32

Prayer Prompt: Father, thank You for Your precious promises that make us Your dwelling place. Create in us clean hearts and renew steadfast spirits within us. Help us pursue holiness not from fear but from grateful love for all You’ve done.

Go Deeper: Examine one area where God might be calling you to greater purity—perhaps in your thought life, relationships, or habits. Meditate on the specific promises of God that most encourage your heart toward holiness today.
