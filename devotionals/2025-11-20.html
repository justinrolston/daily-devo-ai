Daily Devotional

November 20, 2025

[Let Us Sing Songs of Praise] Oh come, let us sing to the LORD; let us make a joyful noise to the rock of our salvation! Let us come into his presence with thanksgiving; let us make a joyful noise to him with songs of praise!
Psalm 95:1-2 (ESV)

Come Before Him with Joy

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Psalm 95 opens with an exuberant call to worship that reveals something profound about our relationship with God. The psalmist doesn’t merely suggest we acknowledge God politely—he calls us to “make a joyful noise,” to sing with abandon, to come bursting into God’s presence with thanksgiving and praise.

Notice the progression here: we’re invited to come to the Lord, then come into His presence. This isn’t casual approach to a distant deity, but intimate access to the “rock of our salvation”—a title that speaks of God’s unshakeable reliability and saving power. The Hebrew word for “rock” suggests not just strength, but a fortress, a place of refuge where we find complete security.

The repeated phrase “let us” reveals worship as both personal and communal. We don’t approach God as isolated individuals, but as part of His people, joining our voices with the chorus of believers across time and space. There’s something beautifully contagious about corporate praise—it lifts our hearts beyond our circumstances and reminds us we’re part of something infinitely greater than ourselves.

But why such joy? Because worship isn’t primarily about our feelings—it’s about God’s character and His saving work. We sing because He is our salvation, because He has done what we could never do for ourselves. Our praise flows not from perfect lives, but from perfect grace.

This challenges our often halfhearted approach to worship. When we truly grasp that the sovereign Creator of the universe welcomes us into His presence—not because we’ve earned it, but because Christ has secured our access—how can we respond with anything less than joyful noise?

Whether you’re facing difficulty or celebrating blessing today, this psalm reminds us that worship reorients our perspective. In God’s presence, our problems don’t disappear, but they’re placed in proper proportion to His greatness and faithfulness. When we remember who He is and what He has done, thanksgiving becomes our natural response.

Confessional Tie-in:

“The duty which God requireth of man is obedience to his revealed will… and thankfulness for his benefits.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Question 39

Prayer Prompt:

Lord, You are indeed our rock and salvation, worthy of all praise. Help me approach You today with the joy and thanksgiving that flows from knowing I am loved, forgiven, and secure in Christ. Let my worship—both private and public—reflect the magnitude of Your grace.

Go Deeper:

Consider what might be hindering joyful worship in your life—is it circumstances, sin, or simply forgetting God’s goodness? Make time today to specifically thank God for His salvation, and let that gratitude overflow into praise. How might you encourage others to join you in making “joyful noise” to the Lord?
