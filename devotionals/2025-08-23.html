When I thought, “My foot slips,” your steadfast love, O LORD, held me up. When the cares of my heart are many, your consolations cheer my soul.
Psalm 94:18-19 (ESV)

When Your Foot Slips

Reflection:

What does this text teach us about the precarious nature of life and God’s response to our struggles? The psalmist uses vivid imagery—a slipping foot—to describe those moments when we feel ourselves losing our grip on stability, hope, or faith itself. This isn’t merely about physical stumbling; it’s about those seasons when everything seems to be giving way beneath us.

The Hebrew word for “slips” suggests movement, instability, being shaken loose from secure footing. We’ve all experienced this—whether through unexpected loss, overwhelming circumstances, or simply the accumulated weight of living in a fallen world. The psalmist’s honesty is refreshing: he acknowledges the reality of feeling unsteady rather than pretending to have perfect spiritual composure.

But notice what happens in that moment of slipping: “your steadfast love, O LORD, held me up.” The Hebrew word “hesed”—steadfast love—speaks of God’s covenant faithfulness, His unchanging commitment to His people. When our circumstances shift and our emotions waver, God’s love remains constant. This isn’t merely God observing our struggle with sympathy; it’s His active intervention, His strong hand catching us before we fall.

The second verse deepens this truth. “Cares of the heart” encompasses all our anxieties, sorrows, and burdens—not just external circumstances but the internal weight they create. The word “many” suggests an overwhelming multiplicity of concerns. Yet God’s “consolations” don’t merely match our troubles; they “cheer” our souls. The Hebrew implies actual delight and joy arising in the midst of our difficulties.

This reveals a profound truth about God’s grace: He doesn’t always remove our trials, but He provides His presence within them. His consolations aren’t platitudes or distractions—they’re substantial comfort that transforms our inner experience even when outer circumstances remain unchanged.

For believers, this passage assures us that our security doesn’t depend on maintaining perfect spiritual footing. Our God specializes in catching those whose feet slip and lifting up those who are bowed down. His steadfast love is not contingent on our steadfast performance.

Confessional Tie-in: “The almighty power, unsearchable wisdom, and infinite goodness of God so far manifest themselves in his providence that it extends itself even to the first fall, and all other sins of angels and men, not by a bare permission, but such as has joined with it a most wise and powerful bounding, and otherwise ordering and governing of them, in a manifold dispensation, to his own holy ends.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter V, Article IV

Prayer Prompt: Father, thank You that when my foot slips, Your steadfast love holds me up. When anxiety multiplies in my heart, fill me with Your consolations that bring true joy. Help me rest in Your faithfulness rather than my own stability.

Go Deeper: Consider what specific “cares of your heart” need God’s consolation today. Practice bringing these to Him throughout the day, remembering that His steadfast love is actively working to hold you up. Reflect on past times when you felt your “foot slip”—how did God’s faithfulness sustain you then?
