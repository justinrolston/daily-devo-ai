And you, child, will be called the prophet of the Most High; for you will go before the Lord to prepare his ways, to give knowledge of salvation to his people in the forgiveness of their sins, because of the tender mercy of our God, whereby the sunrise shall visit us from on high...
Luke 1:76-78 (ESV)

The Dawn of Divine Mercy

Reflection

What does this text teach us? These words from Zechariah’s prophetic song reveal God’s tender mercy breaking into our world like the dawn. Speaking of his infant son John, who would become the Baptist, Zechariah declares that this child will prepare the way for the Lord by giving “knowledge of salvation to his people in the forgiveness of their sins.”

Notice the beautiful progression here: preparation leads to knowledge, knowledge reveals salvation, and salvation comes through forgiveness. John’s ministry would not merely point people toward moral improvement or religious ritual, but toward the fundamental human need—forgiveness of sins. This wasn’t just information about salvation, but experiential knowledge of it, the kind that transforms hearts and lives.

The source of this salvation is breathtaking: “the tender mercy of our God.” The Greek word for “tender mercy” speaks of deep, compassionate love that moves one to action. God’s mercy isn’t distant or clinical—it’s intimately personal, like a parent’s heart moved by their child’s need. This mercy doesn’t wait for us to clean ourselves up first; it comes to us in our sinfulness.

Then comes that stunning image: “the sunrise shall visit us from on high.” Just as dawn breaks the darkness with gentle, irresistible light, so God’s mercy comes to those sitting in spiritual darkness and the shadow of death. We don’t manufacture this light or earn it—it visits us from above, a gift of pure grace.

For believers today, this passage reminds us that our salvation began with God’s initiative, not ours. We were in darkness, unable to find our way to Him. But His tender mercy moved toward us like dawn moves across the earth, not because we deserved it, but because of who He is.

This should humble us deeply—we contribute nothing to our salvation except our need. Yet it should also fill us with wonder and gratitude. The God of the universe bent low to visit us with His mercy, sending first a forerunner to prepare hearts, then His own Son to accomplish what we could never do.

How does recognizing God’s tender mercy as the source of our salvation change how we approach Him today?

Confessional Tie-in

“God, out of his mere good pleasure, from all eternity, elected some to everlasting life, and entered into a covenant of grace, to deliver them out of the estate of sin and misery, and to bring them into an estate of salvation by a Redeemer.” — Westminster Confession of Faith 7.3

Prayer Prompt

Father, thank You that Your tender mercy visited me when I sat in darkness. Help me never to lose the wonder of Your undeserved grace, and make me a faithful witness to others who still need to see the sunrise of Your salvation.

Go Deeper

Reflect on areas of your life where you’re still trying to earn God’s favor rather than resting in His tender mercy. Consider how you might prepare the way for others to encounter Christ, just as John prepared hearts for Jesus’ ministry.
