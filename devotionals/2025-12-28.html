December 28, 2025

Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.
Matthew 11:28 (ESV)

Finding Rest in Christ’s Invitation

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? In Matthew 11:28, Jesus extends one of Scripture’s most tender invitations. The word “come” is not merely a suggestion but an authoritative call from the Son of God. Christ addresses “all who labor and are heavy laden”—a description that encompasses far more than physical exhaustion. The Greek word for “labor” suggests toiling to the point of weariness, while “heavy laden” paints a picture of someone carrying an unbearable burden.

Jesus is speaking to those crushed under the weight of trying to earn God’s favor through religious performance. The Pharisees had multiplied God’s law into countless regulations, creating burdens “hard to bear” (Luke 11:46). But Christ’s invitation extends beyond first-century religious oppression to every person exhausted by the futile attempt to justify themselves before a holy God.

The promise Jesus makes is profound: “I will give you rest.” This isn’t temporary relief but the deep soul-rest that comes from knowing our sins are forgiven and our standing with God is secure. The rest Christ offers is fundamentally different from the world’s solutions to life’s burdens. While the world says “try harder,” Jesus says “come to me.” While religion says “do more,” Jesus says “it is finished.”

This divine rest begins the moment we trust in Christ alone for salvation. Our striving to earn God’s acceptance ceases because Jesus has perfectly fulfilled the law’s demands on our behalf. His righteousness becomes ours through faith, and the burden of our guilt is transferred to Him who bore it on the cross.

Yet this invitation also speaks to believers who find themselves again carrying burdens Christ never intended them to bear. We can return daily to Jesus, laying down our anxieties, our attempts at self-justification, and our weariness from spiritual striving. In Christ, we find not only initial salvation but ongoing refuge for our souls.

The beauty of this invitation is its simplicity and sufficiency. Jesus doesn’t require us to clean ourselves up first or prove our worthiness. He simply says “come”—and in coming, we discover that His rest is not earned but graciously given.

Confessional Tie-in: “All those that are justified God vouchsafeth, in and for his only Son Jesus Christ, to make partakers of the grace of adoption… they are freed from the condemning wrath of God, and delivered from the curse of the law.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter XII

Prayer Prompt: Lord Jesus, I come to You acknowledging my weariness and the burdens I cannot carry alone. Thank You for Your gracious invitation and the perfect rest found in Your finished work. Help me to cease from my own striving and rest fully in Your righteousness.

Go Deeper: Consider what specific burdens you’re carrying that Jesus invites you to lay down today. Meditate on how Christ’s completed work on the cross addresses not just your guilt but also your daily anxieties and spiritual striving.
