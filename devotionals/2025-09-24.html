Therefore do not throw away your confidence, which has a great reward. For you have need of endurance, so that when you have done the will of God you may receive what is promised.
Hebrews 10:35-36 (ESV)

Holding Fast When the Road Grows Long

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? The author of Hebrews addresses believers who were growing weary in their faith journey. These early Christians faced persecution, social ostracism, and the daily grind of living as followers of Christ in a hostile world. The writer urges them not to “throw away” their confidence—a phrase that evokes the image of someone carelessly discarding something valuable.

This confidence is not self-assurance or positive thinking, but rather the bold assurance that comes from knowing Christ and His finished work on our behalf. It’s the settled conviction that God’s promises are true, that Christ’s sacrifice is sufficient, and that our hope is secure. This confidence has “great reward”—not merely earthly benefits, but the eternal inheritance that belongs to all who are in Christ.

The second verse introduces a crucial element: endurance. The Christian life is not a sprint but a marathon. We need staying power to continue doing God’s will when circumstances are difficult, when prayers seem unanswered, and when faithfulness feels costly. This endurance isn’t mustered up from our own willpower—it’s the fruit of the Spirit working within us, sustained by God’s grace.

Notice the beautiful promise embedded here: “when you have done the will of God you may receive what is promised.” This isn’t earning salvation through good works, but rather the natural outworking of genuine faith. Those whom God has justified will persevere in obedience, not to gain God’s favor, but because they already possess it through Christ.

The Christian life requires both confidence and endurance. Confidence without endurance leads to presumption; endurance without confidence becomes mere grit. Together, they form the twin pillars of faithful Christian living. When we’re tempted to give up, we remember that our confidence rests not in our performance but in Christ’s perfect righteousness credited to our account. When we grow overconfident, we remember that the path of discipleship requires daily dependence on God’s sustaining grace.

Confessional Tie-in: “They whom God hath accepted in his Beloved, effectually called and sanctified by his Spirit, can neither totally nor finally fall away from the state of grace; but shall certainly persevere therein to the end, and be eternally saved.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Article XVII.1

Prayer Prompt: Father, when my confidence wavers and my strength fails, remind me that my hope rests securely in Christ’s finished work. Grant me endurance for the journey ahead, that I might faithfully do Your will and receive all that You have promised.

Go Deeper: Consider what specific circumstances tempt you to “throw away” your confidence in God’s promises. Reflect on how both Christ’s completed work and the Spirit’s ongoing presence provide the foundation for lasting endurance in your daily walk with God.
