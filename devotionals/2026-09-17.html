September 17, 2026

[Walk in Love] Therefore be imitators of God, as beloved children.
Ephesians 5:1 (ESV)

Reflecting the Father’s Heart

Title: Beloved Children, Family Likeness

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Paul’s command is startling in its simplicity and its weight: imitate God. Not merely admire Him, obey Him, or serve Him—but imitate Him, the way a child mimics a parent. Notice the foundation Paul lays before the command: “as beloved children.” This is crucial. He does not say, “Imitate God so that you might become His children.” The imitation flows from the relationship, not toward it. You are already beloved. Now walk like it.

This verse sits at a hinge point in Ephesians. Paul has spent four chapters unpacking the riches of grace—chosen, redeemed, sealed, raised with Christ, reconciled to God and to one another. Chapter 5 opens the practical outworking: since you have been remade, live like who you truly are. The context that follows—verse 2 speaks of Christ’s sacrificial love, and the passage continues into holiness, light, and wisdom—reveals that imitating God centers specifically on love, the self-giving love displayed at the cross.

Here is where honest reflection matters: we cannot manufacture this likeness through willpower or moral effort. Children resemble parents by nature, not by strain. Our capacity to imitate God is itself a fruit of grace, the Spirit forming Christ’s character in us over time. This is not instant perfection but patient, ongoing transformation—falling, rising, and being conformed more each day to the family likeness we were given in Christ.

Consider your relationships this week—the coworker who irritates you, the family member who disappoints you, the stranger who is easy to ignore. Does your love reflect the Father’s generous, sacrificial love, or does it mirror the world’s transactional affection? Beloved children don’t imitate God to earn His approval; they imitate Him because His approval, secured in Christ, frees them to love without calculation.

Confessional Tie-in: “Q. 86: Since we are redeemed from our misery by grace through Christ without any merit of ours, why must we still do good works? A. Because Christ, having redeemed us by his blood, also renews us by his Holy Spirit to be his image, so that with our whole life we may show ourselves thankful to God for his blessing.” — Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 86

Prayer Prompt: Father, thank You for calling me Your beloved child before I ever did anything worthy of that name. Forgive me for the ways I resist Your likeness and love conditionally rather than freely. By Your Spirit, form Christ’s character in me today, especially toward the person I find hardest to love.

Go Deeper: Identify one relationship this week where imitating God’s sacrificial love will cost you something—patience, pride, or comfort. Read Ephesians 4:32–5:2 together as one unit, and ask: how does remembering I am forgiven change how I forgive others?
