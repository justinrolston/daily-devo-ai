[Jesus Foretells His Death a Third Time] And as Jesus was going up to Jerusalem, he took the twelve disciples aside, and on the way he said to them, “See, we are going up to Jerusalem. And the Son of Man will be delivered over to the chief priests and scribes, and they will condemn him to death and deliver him over to the Gentiles to be mocked and flogged and crucified, and he will be raised on the third day.”
Matthew 20: 17-19 (ESV)

The Path to Glory Leads Through Suffering

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? As Jesus approached Jerusalem for the final time, He took His disciples aside to prepare them for what was coming. This is the third time Matthew records Jesus predicting His death, and here the details are starkly specific: betrayal, condemnation, mockery, flogging, crucifixion—and resurrection. Jesus knew exactly what awaited Him, yet He continued steadfastly toward the city.

Notice the deliberate language: “we are going up to Jerusalem.” Jesus wasn’t being dragged to His fate; He was purposefully walking toward it. The Son of Man would be “delivered over”—first to religious authorities, then to Gentiles. This wasn’t a failure of His mission but the very heart of it. Every detail of His suffering was foreordained by the Father for our salvation.

The disciples likely focused on the horrific details Jesus described, but we must not miss the final phrase: “he will be raised on the third day.” The cross was never the end of the story. Jesus walked toward suffering because He knew it led to victory—not just His own resurrection, but the redemption of His people.

This passage reveals the heart of the gospel: God’s love demonstrated through substitutionary sacrifice. Jesus endured what we deserved so we might receive what He deserved. The mocking, flogging, and crucifixion that awaited Him were the punishment for our rebellion against God. His resurrection three days later secured our justification and eternal life.

For believers today, this text offers both comfort and challenge. When we face trials, we remember that our Savior chose to endure ultimate suffering for our sake. No pain we experience can compare to His willing sacrifice. Yet we also see that the path of discipleship often involves suffering. Jesus never promised His followers an easy road, but He did promise His presence and ultimate victory.

As we walk through difficulties—whether persecution for our faith, personal struggles, or the ordinary hardships of life in a fallen world—we can fix our eyes on the same hope that sustained Jesus. Our present sufferings are not meaningless; they’re part of God’s good plan to conform us to Christ’s image and display His glory through our lives.

Confessional Tie-in: “The Son of God, the second person in the Trinity, being very and eternal God, of one substance and equal with the Father, did, when the fullness of time was come, take upon him man’s nature, with all the essential properties, and common infirmities thereof, yet without sin; being conceived by the power of the Holy Ghost, in the womb of the virgin Mary, of her substance. So that two whole, perfect, and distinct natures, the Godhead and the manhood, were inseparably joined together in one person, without conversion, composition, or confusion.” — Westminster Confession of Faith 8.2

Prayer Prompt: Lord Jesus, thank You for willingly walking the path to Jerusalem, knowing full well the suffering that awaited You there. Help me to trust Your sovereignty in my own difficulties and to remember that You understand every trial I face because You endured the ultimate trial for my salvation.

Go Deeper: Consider how Jesus’ foreknowledge of His suffering demonstrates both His deity and His love. Spend time meditating on what specific sins of yours were nailed to that cross, and let gratitude for His sacrifice motivate your obedience today.
