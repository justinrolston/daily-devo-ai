I give them eternal life, and they will never perish, and no one will snatch them out of my hand. My Father, who has given them to me, is greater than all, and no one is able to snatch them out of the Father's hand. I and the Father are one.”
John 10:28-30 (ESV)

Held Secure in God’s Hands

Reflection

What does this remarkable passage teach us about our eternal security in Christ? Jesus makes three stunning declarations that should fill every believer’s heart with unshakeable confidence.

First, Jesus declares, “I give them eternal life, and they will never perish.” Notice the certainty—not “might perish” or “could perish,” but “will never perish.” This eternal life isn’t merely endless existence; it’s the very life of God himself, given freely to those who belong to Christ. The Greek word for “never” is emphatic, meaning “absolutely never, not ever, under no circumstances.” When Jesus gives eternal life, it truly is eternal.

Second, Jesus assures us that “no one will snatch them out of my hand.” The image is tender yet powerful—we are held securely in the grip of our Good Shepherd. The word “snatch” suggests violent force, like a wolf trying to tear sheep from the shepherd’s protection. Yet no enemy, no circumstance, no power in heaven or earth can pry us loose from Christ’s grasp. His hands bear the nail prints of our redemption, and those same hands now hold us fast.

Third, Jesus reveals that the Father also holds us: “no one is able to snatch them out of the Father’s hand.” We have double security—held by both the Son and the Father. This isn’t redundant protection; it reveals the perfect unity of the Trinity in our salvation. The Father chose us, the Son redeemed us, and both hold us eternally secure.

Finally, Jesus explains why this security is absolute: “I and the Father are one.” This profound statement of divine unity means our salvation rests not on our grip on God, but on God’s grip on us. We’re held by infinite love, infinite power, and infinite faithfulness.

This truth transforms how we face trials, doubts, and failures. When we stumble, we don’t fall out of God’s hands—we fall into them. When we’re weak, his strength holds us. When we’re faithless, he remains faithful. Our perseverance isn’t ultimately our work but his gracious preservation of us.

This doesn’t lead to carelessness but to confident obedience, knowing that the God who began a good work in us will complete it until the day of Christ Jesus.

Confessional Tie-in

“This perseverance of the saints depends not upon their own free will, but upon the immutability of the decree of election, flowing from the free and unchangeable love of God the Father.” — Canons of Dort, Fifth Head of Doctrine, Article 9

Prayer Prompt

Father, thank you that my security rests not in my ability to hold onto you, but in your unchanging commitment to hold onto me. When doubts arise about my standing before you, remind me that I am gripped by both your hands and Christ’s hands, and nothing can separate me from your love.

Go Deeper

Throughout this day, when you face uncertainty or difficulty, remember that you are held secure in God’s hands. Consider memorizing this passage to anchor your heart in times of doubt. Reflect on this question: How does knowing your eternal security change the way you approach both your failures and your service to God?
