If anyone says, “I love God,” and hates his brother, he is a liar; for he who does not love his brother whom he has seen cannot love God whom he has not seen. And this commandment we have from him: whoever loves God must also love his brother.
1 John 4:20-21 (ESV)

The Inseparable Love: Loving God Through Loving Others

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? John presents us with a stark reality check: authentic love for God cannot exist apart from love for our fellow human beings. The apostle doesn’t mince words—calling someone who claims to love God while harboring hatred for others a “liar.” This isn’t merely harsh rhetoric; it’s a profound theological truth about the nature of genuine faith and love.

The logic John employs is compelling. How can we claim to love an invisible God while failing to love the visible people He has placed in our lives? Our brothers and sisters in humanity serve as the testing ground for our professed devotion to God. This reveals something crucial about the nature of saving faith: it always produces visible fruit in our relationships with others.

This passage connects to Jesus’ teaching that the greatest commandments are loving God and loving our neighbor as ourselves (Matthew 22:37-39). John shows us these aren’t two separate commands but interconnected realities. True love for God flows naturally into love for others because God Himself is love, and His Spirit within us produces this same love.

But let’s be honest about our struggle here. We often find it easier to love an unseen God than to love difficult people. The coworker who undermines us, the family member who disappoints us repeatedly, the neighbor whose lifestyle offends us—these relationships test the genuineness of our faith. John’s words pierce through our comfortable spirituality and expose the places where our love falls short.

Yet this isn’t merely a burden but also a gift. God has given us tangible ways to express our love for Him. When we serve the hurting, forgive those who wound us, and show patience with the difficult, we’re actually loving God Himself. Every act of genuine love toward another person becomes an act of worship.

This commandment assumes we’ve first experienced God’s transforming love. Apart from His grace working within us, such love remains impossible. But as recipients of His mercy, we’re both empowered and obligated to extend that same love to others. The love we show isn’t manufactured by our willpower but flows from the wellspring of God’s love already poured into our hearts.

Confessional Tie-in: “The sum of the ten commandments is, to love the Lord our God with all our heart, with all our soul, with all our strength, and with all our mind; and our neighbor as ourselves.” — Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 4

Prayer Prompt: Father, search my heart and reveal where my love for You lacks authenticity in how I treat others. Grant me Your Spirit’s power to love even those I find difficult, knowing that in loving them, I love You. Transform my relationships into expressions of worship.

Go Deeper: Examine your closest relationships this week—family, coworkers, neighbors. Where is God calling you to demonstrate His love more fully? Consider Jesus’ words in Matthew 25:40 about serving “the least of these” as a way of serving Him directly.
