But seek first the kingdom of God and his righteousness, and all these things will be added to you.
Matthew 6:33 (ESV)

First Things First

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Jesus gives us a profound reordering of life’s priorities in Matthew 6:33. After warning against the anxiety that comes from worrying about food, clothing, and material needs, Christ presents the antidote: “But seek first the kingdom of God and his righteousness.” This isn’t merely good advice—it’s a divine command that strikes at the heart of human nature.

The word “seek” implies an active, ongoing pursuit, not a passive acknowledgment. We are called to pursue God’s kingdom—His sovereign rule and reign—with the same intensity that the world pursues wealth, security, and status. But notice the specific object of our seeking: God’s righteousness, not our own. This points us directly to the gospel truth that we cannot manufacture the righteousness God requires. Instead, we seek the righteousness that comes through faith in Christ, who perfectly fulfilled God’s law on our behalf.

The promise attached—”and all these things will be added to you”—doesn’t guarantee prosperity or ease. Rather, it assures us that when we orient our lives around God’s priorities, He will faithfully provide what we truly need. This requires trust in God’s definition of our needs, which may differ significantly from our own.

This verse exposes our natural tendency to seek God’s blessings while keeping Him at arm’s length from our daily decisions. We compartmentalize our faith, pursuing career advancement, financial security, or relational satisfaction as primary goals, with God as a helpful addition. Jesus calls us to flip this entirely.

When God’s kingdom becomes our primary pursuit, everything else finds its proper place. Work becomes a means of serving God and neighbor rather than self-advancement. Relationships are shaped by Christ’s love rather than personal benefit. Even our anxieties are transformed as we remember that our heavenly Father knows our needs and has promised to meet them according to His wisdom and timing.

This reordering doesn’t happen overnight. It requires daily surrender, constant realignment of our hearts, and the ongoing work of the Holy Spirit to conform our desires to God’s will.

Confessional Tie-in: “What is the chief end of man? Man’s chief end is to glorify God, and to enjoy him forever.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Question 1

Prayer Prompt: Father, forgive me for the times I have sought my own kingdom instead of Yours. Help me to trust that as I pursue Your righteousness and Your rule in my life, You will faithfully provide everything I truly need according to Your perfect wisdom.

Go Deeper: Examine your current priorities and time allocation—what are you actually seeking first? Consider how this week’s decisions about money, relationships, and time could better reflect a kingdom-first mindset. Meditate on how Christ perfectly demonstrated this principle throughout His earthly ministry.
