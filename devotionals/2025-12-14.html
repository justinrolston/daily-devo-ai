[Birth of Jesus Foretold] In the sixth month the angel Gabriel was sent from God to a city of Galilee named Nazareth, to a virgin betrothed to a man whose name was Joseph, of the house of David. And the virgin's name was Mary. And he came to her and said, “Greetings, O favored one, the Lord is with you!”
Luke 1:26-28 (ESV)

The God Who Comes Near

Reflection

What does this familiar passage teach us about God’s character and His approach to humanity? In these opening verses of the Annunciation, we encounter the stunning reality that the almighty God chooses to work through the ordinary and seemingly insignificant.

Consider the scene: Gabriel, God’s messenger, is sent not to Jerusalem’s temple or to a palace, but to Nazareth—a small, unremarkable town in Galilee. The recipient of this world-changing announcement is not a queen or prophetess, but a young virgin named Mary, engaged to a carpenter. This divine encounter reveals God’s sovereign choice to accomplish His greatest work through the humble and lowly.

Gabriel’s greeting, “Greetings, O favored one, the Lord is with you!” speaks to something profound about God’s grace. The word “favored” doesn’t imply Mary earned God’s blessing through her merit, but rather that she was the recipient of undeserved grace. This favor flows entirely from God’s sovereign will, not from anything inherently special about Mary herself. She was chosen to bear the Christ not because she was sinless, but because God, in His perfect wisdom, selected her for this holy purpose.

The angel’s declaration “the Lord is with you” echoes throughout Scripture as God’s promise to His people. From Moses at the burning bush to Joshua entering the Promised Land, God assures His servants of His presence when He calls them to seemingly impossible tasks. This divine presence doesn’t guarantee ease, but it promises sufficiency for whatever God calls us to endure or accomplish.

How does this speak to our daily walk with Christ? Just as God chose Mary—young, unknown, and ordinary—He continues to work through believers who feel inadequate for His calling. We need not wait until we feel worthy or capable. God’s power is displayed most clearly through our weakness, and His grace is sufficient for every task He sets before us.

When we feel overlooked by the world or question whether God could use someone like us, we remember that our God specializes in using the humble things of this world to accomplish His eternal purposes. His favor rests not on our achievements but on His unchanging grace toward all who belong to Him through faith in Christ.

Confessional Tie-in

“It pleased God, in his eternal purpose, to choose and ordain the Lord Jesus, his only begotten Son, to be the Mediator between God and man… to be born of a woman, made under the law, to redeem them that were under the law.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter 8.1

Prayer Prompt

Heavenly Father, thank You for Your sovereign grace that chooses the humble and ordinary to accomplish Your extraordinary purposes. Help me trust in Your presence with me today, knowing that Your power is made perfect in my weakness.

Go Deeper

Consider areas where you feel inadequate or overlooked. How might God be preparing you to serve Him through these very circumstances? Meditate on other biblical examples of God choosing the unlikely—David, Gideon, or the disciples—and ask how their stories might encourage your faith today.
