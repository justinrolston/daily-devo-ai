The Riches of Grace

Ephesians 1:7 (ESV) “In him we have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of our trespasses, according to the riches of his grace…”

Reflection:

What does this magnificent verse teach us about our salvation? Paul declares a profound truth: our redemption is not a mere transaction, but a treasure trove of divine grace. The phrase “in him” anchors everything that follows—our entire spiritual inheritance flows from our union with Christ.

The word “redemption” carries the imagery of a slave market. In the ancient world, to redeem meant to purchase freedom for a captive by paying the required price. We were enslaved to sin, death, and Satan’s dominion, unable to free ourselves regardless of our efforts or good intentions. But Christ paid the ultimate price—His own precious blood—to secure our freedom.

Notice Paul’s emphasis: we have redemption. This is not a future hope we’re striving toward, but a present reality for every believer. The moment we trust in Christ, redemption becomes ours. The forgiveness of our trespasses—every rebellious thought, selfish act, and failure to love God perfectly—is complete and irreversible.

But Paul adds a crucial phrase: “according to the riches of his grace.” God doesn’t forgive us reluctantly or sparingly, like someone counting out coins from a nearly empty purse. Rather, He lavishes forgiveness upon us from His infinite storehouse of grace. The Greek word for “riches” suggests abundant wealth—more than we could ever need or exhaust.

This should transform how we view ourselves and our failures. When guilt whispers that we’ve sinned too greatly or too often, we remember that God’s grace isn’t measured by our small understanding of mercy, but by His infinite riches. When we struggle with forgiving others, we’re reminded of the vast debt that has been cancelled on our behalf.

The blood of Christ doesn’t just cover our past sins—it covers our present failures and future stumbles. This isn’t license to sin carelessly, but freedom to live boldly, knowing our acceptance before God rests not on our performance but on Christ’s perfect sacrifice.

Confessional Tie-in: “All those that are justified, God vouchsafeth, in and for his only Son Jesus Christ, to make partakers of the grace of adoption, by which they are taken into the number, and enjoy the liberties and privileges of the children of God.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter XII, Article 1

Prayer Prompt: Father, I marvel at the riches of Your grace that purchased my freedom through Christ’s blood. Help me live today with the confidence that comes from complete forgiveness, and grant me a heart that extends this same grace to others who have wronged me.

Go Deeper: Consider writing down specific sins that burden your conscience, then physically destroy the paper as a reminder that Christ’s blood has removed them forever. Reflect on this question: If God’s grace toward you is truly this rich and abundant, how should that change the way you treat someone who has hurt you recently?
