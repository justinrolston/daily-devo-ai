September 17, 2025

[Walk in Love] Therefore be imitators of God, as beloved children.
Ephesians 5:1 (ESV)

Beloved Children Who Imitate Their Father

Reflection

What does this text teach us? Paul’s command to “be imitators of God, as beloved children” flows directly from his preceding discussion of forgiveness and love. The word “therefore” connects this imperative to God’s gracious work in Christ—because we have been forgiven and made alive in Him, we are now called to reflect our heavenly Father’s character.

The phrase “beloved children” is foundational to understanding this call. We don’t imitate God to earn His love or secure our place in His family. Rather, because we are already His beloved children through adoption in Christ, imitation becomes the natural overflow of our new identity. Just as children naturally mirror their parents’ behaviors, speech patterns, and values, believers are called to reflect the character of their heavenly Father.

This imitation isn’t about perfection—no child perfectly mimics their parent. Instead, it’s about family resemblance. When Paul speaks of imitating God, he’s primarily thinking of God’s love, forgiveness, and sacrificial giving demonstrated supremely in Christ. The context reveals this as Paul immediately follows with “walk in love, as Christ loved us and gave himself up for us.”

The beauty of this calling lies in its impossibility apart from grace. We cannot manufacture godlike love or forgiveness through willpower alone. Our fallen hearts are naturally bent toward selfishness and revenge. Yet God has not left us as orphans striving to imitate a distant deity. He has placed His Spirit within us, transforming our hearts and enabling what was once impossible.

This truth should both humble and encourage us. When we fail to love as God loves—and we will—we remember that our standing as beloved children isn’t based on our performance but on Christ’s perfect imitation of the Father on our behalf. Simultaneously, we’re motivated to pursue Christlikeness not from fear or obligation, but from gratitude and the joy of bearing our Father’s image.

In practical terms, this means choosing forgiveness over grudges, generosity over selfishness, and patience over irritation. It means asking in difficult relationships: “How has God treated me? How can I reflect His character here?” As beloved children, we have both the motivation and the means to increasingly mirror our Father’s love.

Confessional Tie-in

“We believe that this true faith, being wrought in man by the hearing of the Word of God and the operation of the Holy Spirit, regenerates him and makes him a new man, causing him to live a new life, and freeing him from the bondage of sin.” — Belgic Confession, Article 24

Prayer Prompt

Father, thank You that I am Your beloved child through Christ, not because of my performance but because of Your grace. Help me to imitate Your love and forgiveness today, especially in relationships where I’m tempted toward selfishness or bitterness. Transform my heart to reflect Your character more clearly.

Go Deeper

Consider one relationship where you struggle to show God-like love—how has God’s treatment of you in Christ provided both the model and motivation for loving others? Spend time this week meditating on your identity as God’s beloved child and how this truth should shape your interactions with difficult people.
