[The Righteous Shall Live by Faith] For I am not ashamed of the gospel, for it is the power of God for salvation to everyone who believes, to the Jew first and also to the Greek.
Romans 1:16 (ESV)

Unashamed: The Gospel’s Undiminished Power

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Paul, writing to believers in the imperial capital of Rome—a city saturated with power, philosophy, and pride—declares he is not ashamed of the gospel. This is no small statement. To preach Christ crucified in Rome was to invite mockery; a crucified Messiah seemed foolish to the sophisticated and scandalous to the religious. Yet Paul anchors his confidence not in cultural approval but in what the gospel is: the power of God for salvation.

Notice the phrase carefully. The gospel doesn’t merely describe salvation or point toward it—it is God’s power accomplishing it. Salvation isn’t something we achieve through effort, ritual, or moral improvement. It is God himself acting, breaking into human hearts dead in sin and making them alive through the message of Christ. This is why Paul isn’t ashamed: he has witnessed ordinary words about an executed Jewish teacher transform idolaters, slaves, philosophers, and outcasts into children of God.

Notice too the universal scope—”to everyone who believes, to the Jew first and also to the Greek.” Salvation flows through God’s covenant promises to Israel, yet it extends without partiality to all nations. This reflects God’s sovereign design: one gospel, one Savior, one way of salvation available to all who believe, regardless of background, status, or performance.

For us, this text confronts a quiet temptation—shame. Perhaps not shame of Christ outright, but a subtle reluctance to speak of him plainly, to let our faith interrupt polite conversation, to trust that gospel words carry real power. We often act as though clever arguments or winsome presentation are what save people. But salvation is God’s work, not our rhetorical skill. This frees us from performance anxiety in witness and roots our confidence in the message itself, not our delivery.

Do you believe the gospel still has power today—power to soften hardened hearts, restore broken marriages, free addicts, and comfort the dying? Or has familiarity dulled your wonder at it?

Confessional Tie-in:

“Faith is ordinarily begotten by the ministry of the Word… by which also, and by the administration of the sacraments, and prayer, it is increased and strengthened.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter 14.1

Prayer Prompt:

Lord, forgive me for the times I have shrunk back from your gospel, fearing rejection more than I treasured your truth. Thank you that the same power that raised Christ from the dead works through the simple message of the cross. Give me boldness today to speak of Christ without shame, trusting your Spirit to do what only he can do.

Go Deeper:

This week, identify one relationship where fear has kept you silent about Christ, and pray specifically for boldness there. Consider memorizing Romans 1:16 as a daily reminder that the gospel’s power doesn’t depend on your eloquence. Ask yourself: Do I approach evangelism as persuasion techniques, or as trusting God’s Word to do what it promises?
