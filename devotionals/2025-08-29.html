August 29, 2025

There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither slave nor free, there is no male and female, for you are all one in Christ Jesus.
Galatians 3:28 (ESV)

Unity in Christ: Our True Identity

Reflection

What does this text teach us? Paul’s declaration in Galatians 3:28 reveals one of the most radical truths in all of Scripture: in Christ, the divisions that define and often divide human society are transcended by a greater reality. When Paul writes “there is neither… nor,” he’s not erasing these distinctions entirely, but showing that they no longer determine our ultimate identity or standing before God.

The context is crucial. Paul has been arguing that righteousness comes through faith in Christ, not through works of the law. Now he shows the implications: if salvation is by grace through faith alone, then no human category—ethnic, social, or biological—gives anyone advantage in God’s kingdom. The Jewish-Gentile divide that dominated the ancient world, the slave-free distinction that shaped society, and the male-female difference that God himself established in creation—none of these affect our spiritual standing.

This unity isn’t based on human effort or social engineering. It’s grounded in the phrase “in Christ Jesus.” Our oneness flows from our union with Christ through faith. When we are justified by faith, we are clothed with Christ himself (verse 27), and this new identity supersedes all earthly categories.

Yet Paul isn’t promoting a bland uniformity. He’s revealing something far more beautiful: despite our vast differences, we share the same salvation, the same Spirit, and the same eternal inheritance. The Jewish believer and Gentile convert both receive identical righteousness—Christ’s own righteousness credited to their account. The wealthy landowner and his servant both kneel at the same cross. Men and women both bear God’s image equally and participate fully in the new creation.

This truth transforms how we view ourselves and others. When we see a fellow believer, we’re not first seeing their race, class, or gender—we’re seeing someone clothed with the same Christ who clothes us. This doesn’t eliminate healthy distinctions in roles or relationships, but it does eliminate any sense of spiritual superiority or inferiority.

The practical implications are staggering. Prejudice becomes not just socially harmful but spiritually absurd—how can we look down on those whom God has clothed with his own Son? Division in the church becomes not just unfortunate but contradictory to the gospel itself.

Confessional Tie-in

“All those that are justified God vouchsafeth, in and for his only Son Jesus Christ, to make partakers of the grace of adoption: by which they are taken into the number, and enjoy the liberties and privileges of the children of God.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter 12.1

Prayer Prompt

Father, thank you that our identity in Christ transcends every earthly division. Help us to see ourselves and others first as those clothed with Christ’s righteousness. Guard our hearts from prejudice and pride, and help us live out the beautiful unity we share in your Son.

Go Deeper

This week, examine your heart for subtle prejudices or feelings of superiority based on earthly categories. How does seeing others as “clothed with Christ” change your interactions? Consider memorizing this verse as a reminder of your true identity whenever worldly divisions tempt you toward pride or discouragement.
