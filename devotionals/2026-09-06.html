Jesus answered him, “If anyone loves me, he will keep my word, and my Father will love him, and we will come to him and make our home with him.
John 14:23 (ESV)

Title: A Home Within You

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Jesus speaks these words on the night before His crucifixion, answering a disciple named Judas (not Iscariot) who wondered why Jesus would reveal Himself to His followers but not to the world at large. The answer Jesus gives is profound: love for Christ is shown through obedience, and obedience opens the door to an astonishing reality—God Himself coming to dwell with us.

Notice the order here. It doesn’t say “keep my commandments and then I will love you.” Love for Jesus is the root; obedience is the fruit. This is the pattern of the whole gospel. We do not obey our way into God’s favor—we are drawn into fellowship with Christ by grace, and out of that relationship, genuine love produces a life that treasures His Word.

The promise itself is staggering: “we will come to him and make our home with him.” The eternal God, who once dwelt in a tabernacle and later a temple, now makes His dwelling in the hearts of ordinary believers. This is the fulfillment of covenant promises stretching back through all of Scripture—Immanuel, God with us, is not a distant memory of Christmas morning but a present, abiding reality through the Holy Spirit.

This truth speaks directly into our daily struggles. When we feel spiritually dry, forgotten, or distant from God, this verse reminds us that our standing with the Father does not rise and fall with our feelings. Yet it also calls us forward—do we love His Word enough to let it shape our choices, our speech, our priorities? Obedience flowing from love is not a burden; it is the pathway into deeper communion with the God who has already chosen to make His home in us.

You are not striving to earn a visit from God. If you belong to Christ, He has already moved in.

Confessional Tie-in:

“Q. 1: What is the chief and highest end of man? A. Man’s chief and highest end is to glorify God, and fully to enjoy him forever.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Q&A 1

Prayer Prompt:

Father, thank You that through Christ You have made Your home within me, not because I have earned it, but because of Your grace. Forgive me for the moments when my obedience feels more like duty than love. Teach me today to treasure Your Word so that my life increasingly reflects the nearness of Your presence.

Go Deeper:

Take a few minutes today to read John 14 in its entirety, noticing how Jesus ties love, obedience, and the Spirit’s indwelling together. Ask yourself: Is there an area of disobedience I’ve been excusing, and how might growing love for Christ change my response to it? Consider closing your day by simply resting in the truth that God is not far off—He has made His home with you.
