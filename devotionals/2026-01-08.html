Seeking Good in a Broken World

Amos 5:14-15 (ESV) “Seek good, and not evil, that you may live; and so the LORD, the God of hosts, will be with you, as you have said. Hate evil, and love good, and establish justice in the gate; it may be that the LORD, the God of hosts, will be gracious to the remnant of Joseph.”

Reflection

What does this text teach us? Through the prophet Amos, God calls His people to a fundamental reorientation of their hearts and lives. The command is deceptively simple yet profoundly challenging: seek good, not evil. But this isn’t merely moral advice—it’s a matter of life and death, of God’s presence or absence.

The Israelites had grown comfortable with religious ritual while tolerating injustice in their daily lives. They claimed God’s presence (“as you have said”) but lived as if He were absent. Amos cuts through their self-deception: if you truly want the LORD to be with you, your lives must reflect His character.

Notice the progression in verse 15: hate evil, love good, establish justice. This isn’t passive morality but active transformation. The “gate” was where legal decisions were made, where justice was administered or corrupted. God calls His people to be agents of righteousness in the very places where society’s moral fabric is woven.

The phrase “it may be that the LORD… will be gracious” might seem to suggest uncertainty about God’s mercy. Rather, it reflects the humility appropriate for sinners approaching a holy God. Even our best efforts at righteousness cannot manipulate or guarantee God’s favor—grace remains grace.

For believers today, this passage confronts our tendency to compartmentalize faith. We may worship on Sunday while remaining indifferent to injustice throughout the week. We may speak of God’s presence while our choices suggest we live for ourselves alone.

Yet there’s profound hope here. The God who demands righteousness is the same God who provides it through Christ. Because Jesus perfectly sought good and established justice, we can approach God with confidence. And because His Spirit dwells within us, we can genuinely hate evil and love good—not to earn God’s presence, but because we already have it.

The call to “establish justice in the gate” remains urgent. In our workplaces, communities, and relationships, we’re called to be people who actively pursue what is right and good, reflecting the character of the God who has graciously made His home with us.

Confessional Tie-in: “Good works are only such as God hath commanded in his holy Word, and not such as, without the warrant thereof, are devised by men, out of blind zeal, or upon any pretense of good intention.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Article XVI.1

Prayer Prompt: Lord, search my heart and reveal where I’ve grown comfortable with evil or indifferent to injustice. Give me a genuine love for what is good and the courage to pursue righteousness in every area of my life, knowing that Your presence with me is my greatest treasure.

Go Deeper: Consider one specific area where you need to “establish justice”—perhaps in how you treat others, handle your resources, or respond to injustice around you. Ask yourself: Do my daily choices reflect someone who truly believes God is present with me?
