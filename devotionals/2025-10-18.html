Wait for the LORD; be strong, and let your heart take courage; wait for the LORD!
Psalm 27:14 (ESV)

Finding Strength in Sacred Waiting

Reflection

What does this text teach us? The psalmist David gives us a double command wrapped around a powerful promise. He tells us to “wait for the LORD” - but this isn’t passive sitting around. The Hebrew word qavah means to look eagerly with expectation, like a watchman scanning the horizon for dawn. Between these bookend commands, David provides the fuel for our waiting: “be strong, and let your heart take courage.”

This psalm emerges from a context of real danger. David faces enemies who seek his life, yet he declares the Lord as his “light and salvation” (v.1). His repeated call to wait isn’t born from comfortable circumstances but from tested faith. When God’s promises seem delayed and our prayers appear unanswered, waiting becomes one of the most challenging spiritual disciplines.

But notice what kind of waiting David prescribes. This is muscular waiting - waiting that requires strength and courage. The commands “be strong” and “let your heart take courage” are not suggestions for self-improvement but calls to draw upon resources outside ourselves. True spiritual strength comes not from human willpower but from remembering who God is and what He has promised.

The repetition of “wait for the LORD” emphasizes both the difficulty and necessity of this discipline. Our flesh wants immediate answers, quick fixes, and visible progress. Yet God’s timing operates according to His perfect wisdom, not our impatient desires. The Lord is developing our faith, deepening our dependence, and often preparing us for blessings we’re not yet ready to receive.

This waiting isn’t empty time but pregnant with purpose. While we wait, God works - in us and around us. He strengthens our faith, refines our character, and orchestrates circumstances according to His sovereign plan. Our part is to remain anchored in His promises, continuing in prayer, and refusing to take matters into our own hands.

The beauty of this text lies in its honest acknowledgment that waiting is hard work requiring divine strength, while simultaneously promising that such strength is available. We don’t muster courage through positive thinking but through remembering that our hope rests in the unchanging character of God.

Confessional Tie-in

“This faith is a knowledge grounded upon the Word of God and a confidence of the heart, wrought by the Holy Spirit through the gospel, whereby we rest upon the promises of God.” — Belgic Confession, Article 22

Prayer Prompt

Lord, when everything in me wants to rush ahead or give up entirely, teach me the sacred discipline of waiting upon You. Strengthen my heart with courage that comes from knowing You are faithful to Your promises, and help me trust Your perfect timing even when I cannot see the way forward.

Go Deeper

Practice deliberate waiting today by bringing an ongoing concern to God in prayer, then consciously choosing to rest in His sovereignty rather than anxiously planning your next move. Consider memorizing this verse as an anchor for seasons when God’s timing doesn’t match your expectations. What current situation is calling you to exercise this kind of courageous waiting?
