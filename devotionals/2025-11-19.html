November 19, 2025

[Book One The Way of the Righteous and the Wicked] Blessed is the man who walks not in the counsel of the wicked, nor stands in the way of sinners, nor sits in the seat of scoffers; but his delight is in the law of the LORD, and on his law he meditates day and night.
Psalm 1:1-2 (ESV)

The Path of True Blessing

Reflection:

What does this opening psalm teach us about the blessed life? The psalmist presents two contrasting paths: the way of the righteous and the way of the wicked. Notice the progression in verse 1—walking, standing, sitting—showing how sin gradually entraps us. We begin by merely walking past ungodly counsel, then we pause to stand among sinners, and finally we settle down comfortably in the seat of scoffers. This isn’t describing those who struggle with sin, but those who embrace and celebrate rebellion against God.

The blessed person is defined not primarily by what they avoid, but by what they love. Their delight is in the law of the Lord. The Hebrew word for “delight” suggests pleasure, satisfaction, and joy—not grudging obedience. This person finds genuine happiness in God’s Word because they’ve been transformed by grace to love what God loves.

Day and night meditation reveals the depth of this delight. This isn’t merely daily Bible reading, though that’s important. It’s allowing Scripture to permeate our thoughts, returning to God’s truth throughout our day, chewing on it like a cow chews its cud. When we truly delight in God’s law, meditation becomes natural, not burdensome.

But how do we move from finding God’s commands restrictive to finding them delightful? Only through the gospel. Christ perfectly fulfilled the law we could never keep, bearing its curse on our behalf. Now, united to Him by faith, we’re freed from the law’s condemnation and empowered by the Spirit to find joy in obedience.

This psalm begins the entire Psalter, setting the tone for everything that follows. It reminds us that true blessing isn’t found in worldly success or even in avoiding obvious sins. It’s found in treasuring God’s Word, allowing it to shape our thoughts, affections, and actions. The blessed life flows from a heart captivated by God’s truth.

As we begin our day, we face countless voices offering counsel—media, friends, culture, our own hearts. Will we find our primary counsel in Scripture, or will we subtly drift toward the world’s wisdom? The choice shapes not just our day, but our destiny.

Confessional Tie-in: “The whole counsel of God concerning all things necessary for his own glory, man’s salvation, faith and life, is either expressly set down in Scripture, or by good and necessary consequence may be deduced from Scripture.” — Westminster Confession of Faith 1.6

Prayer Prompt: Father, create in me a heart that truly delights in Your Word. Help me to see Your law not as burden but as gift, revealing Your character and showing me the path of life. Guard me from the subtle drift toward worldly counsel.

Go Deeper: Consider what voices most influence your daily decisions—are they aligned with Scripture? This week, try meditating on one verse throughout each day, returning to it in quiet moments and asking how it applies to your current circumstances.
