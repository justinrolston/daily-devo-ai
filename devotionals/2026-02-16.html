[Love One Another] For this is the message that you have heard from the beginning, that we should love one another.
1 John 3:11 (ESV)

The Command That Defines Us

Reflection

What does this text teach us? John presents love for one another not as a suggestion or ideal, but as the fundamental message of the Christian faith—one that believers have possessed “from the beginning.” This isn’t merely human affection or natural kindness, but the distinctive mark that sets God’s people apart from the world.

The phrase “from the beginning” points us back to Jesus’ own words in the upper room: “A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another: just as I have loved you, you also are to love one another” (John 13:34). This love isn’t new because no one had ever heard of love before, but because it flows from Christ’s sacrificial love for us and becomes the defining characteristic of His people.

But here’s where we must be honest about our hearts. How often do we find loving other believers difficult? When someone disagrees with us, disappoints us, or simply rubs us the wrong way, our natural response reveals the depth of our selfishness. We’re quick to love those who are lovable and slow to extend grace to those who challenge us.

This is precisely why John frames love as something we’ve “heard”—it comes to us from outside ourselves. We don’t generate this love from our own resources. It originates in God’s character, flows through Christ’s redemptive work, and is cultivated in us by the Spirit’s power. The same God who loved us when we were unlovable now calls us to love others with that same transforming grace.

The beauty of this command is that it’s both utterly demanding and completely achievable. Demanding because it calls us to supernatural love that mirrors Christ’s sacrifice. Achievable because the One who commands it also provides the power to obey it. Every time we choose to forgive rather than harbor resentment, to serve rather than demand service, to speak truth in love rather than remain silent—we’re living out this foundational message.

Consider your interactions this week. Have they been marked by the patient, sacrificial love that Christ showed you? When believers fail to love one another, we obscure the gospel before a watching world. But when we love sacrificially, we display the transforming power of the One who first loved us.

Confessional Tie-in

“The communion of saints is a most sweet and comfortable doctrine; teaching us that believers have such communion with Christ their head, that they are partakers of all his graces, death, resurrection, and glory: and being united to one another in love, they have communion in each other’s gifts and graces.” — Westminster Confession of Faith 26.1

Prayer Prompt

Father, forgive me for the times I’ve withheld love from my brothers and sisters in Christ. Fill my heart with Your love so that I might love others as You have loved me, especially those I find difficult to love.

Go Deeper

This week, identify one believer you struggle to love well and take a concrete step toward them—perhaps through encouragement, service, or simply patient listening. Ask yourself: “How has Christ’s love for me been patient and sacrificial, and how can I extend that same love to others?”
