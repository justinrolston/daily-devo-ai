Have this mind among yourselves, which is yours in Christ Jesus, who, though he was in the form of God, did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped, but emptied himself, by taking the form of a servant, being born in the likeness of men. And being found in human form, he humbled himself by becoming obedient to the point of death, even death on a cross.
Philippians 2:5-8 (ESV)

The Mind of Christ: The Greatest Example of Humility

Reflection:

What does this magnificent passage teach us about the mind we should have as believers? Paul calls us to adopt the very mindset of Christ Jesus—a radical call that reaches to the core of our identity and daily living.

The apostle presents Christ’s incarnation not merely as a theological doctrine, but as the supreme example of humility. Though Jesus possessed the very nature of God—sharing fully in divine glory, power, and honor—He did not cling to His rightful position. The phrase “did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped” reveals Christ’s voluntary choice. Unlike Adam, who grasped for equality with God, Jesus released His grip on divine privileges.

But Christ’s humility went far beyond simply setting aside heavenly glory. He “emptied himself” by taking on human nature, becoming a servant. This wasn’t merely a costume change—it was a genuine addition of humanity to His divine nature. The eternal Son became what He had never been while remaining what He had always been.

The climax of Christ’s humiliation was His obedience unto death—and not just any death, but the shameful, cursed death of crucifixion. Here we see perfect obedience to the Father’s will, even when that will led through unimaginable suffering.

What does this mean for us? Having “this mind” among ourselves means embracing the path of humble service rather than self-promotion. In our marriages, workplaces, and churches, we’re called to consider others’ interests above our own. This isn’t natural to us—our fallen hearts instinctively grasp for position, recognition, and comfort.

Yet Christ’s example shows us a different way. True greatness comes through humble service. Real strength is displayed in gentleness. Authentic leadership emerges from sacrificial love. This mindset transforms how we handle conflict, make decisions, and relate to those who seem beneath our notice.

The beauty is that we’re not called to manufacture this humility through gritted teeth. Christ’s mind becomes ours through union with Him. His Spirit works within us, gradually conforming us to His image. As we meditate on His magnificent condescension, our hearts are drawn to follow His example.

Confessional Tie-in: “By his perfect obedience and sacrifice of himself… he has fully satisfied the justice of his Father; and purchased not only reconciliation, but an everlasting inheritance in the kingdom of heaven, for all those whom the Father has given unto him.” — Westminster Confession of Faith 8.5

Prayer Prompt: Lord Jesus, thank You for Your incredible humility in leaving heaven’s glory to serve and save us. Forgive my pride and self-seeking, and grant me Your mind of humble service. Help me to consider others’ interests above my own today.

Go Deeper: Throughout this day, look for specific opportunities to serve rather than be served, to give rather than receive. Consider memorizing this passage to anchor your heart in Christ’s example when pride tempts you to grasp for position or recognition.
