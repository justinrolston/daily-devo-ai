Therefore encourage one another and build one another up, just as you are doing.
1 Thessalonians 5:11 (ESV)

Building Up the Body

1 Thessalonians 5:11 (ESV) “Therefore encourage one another and build one another up, just as you are doing.”

Reflection

What does this text teach us about Christian community? Paul’s simple yet profound command reveals something essential about how believers are meant to live together. The word “therefore” connects this instruction to the preceding verses about Christ’s return and living as children of light. Because we belong to Christ and await His coming, we are called to a particular way of relating to one another.

The Greek word for “encourage” (parakaleo) carries the idea of coming alongside someone to strengthen and comfort them. It’s the same root used for the Holy Spirit as our Comforter. When we encourage fellow believers, we participate in the Spirit’s own work of building up God’s people. This isn’t mere positive thinking or casual compliments—it’s a sacred ministry of speaking truth in love to strengthen faith and foster growth.

Notice Paul’s phrase “build one another up.” The imagery is architectural. Just as a building requires careful construction with each stone supporting the others, the church grows strong when each member contributes to the spiritual edifice. This mutual building happens through words of truth, acts of service, prayers offered, and burdens shared.

What’s striking is Paul’s final phrase: “just as you are doing.” The Thessalonians weren’t perfect, yet Paul recognizes their faithful efforts to encourage one another. This reminds us that encouragement isn’t reserved for spiritual giants—it’s the normal Christian life. Every believer, regardless of maturity level, can speak words that strengthen faith and point others to Christ.

This calling flows from the gospel itself. We who were dead in sin have been made alive in Christ. We who deserved condemnation have received mercy. This transforming grace doesn’t make us inward-focused but compels us outward toward others who need the same hope we’ve received.

In our age of criticism and complaint, this text calls us to a counter-cultural way of living. Rather than tearing down, we build up. Rather than discouraging, we strengthen. This isn’t naive optimism but gospel realism—we see others as God sees them: fallen yet redeemed, struggling yet being sanctified, imperfect yet precious to Christ.

Confessional Tie-in

“All believers, being united to Jesus Christ their head, by his Spirit and by faith, have fellowship with him in his graces, sufferings, death, resurrection, and glory: and being united to one another in love, they have communion in each other’s gifts and graces.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter 26, Article 1

Prayer Prompt

Father, thank You for the gift of Christian community and for brothers and sisters who have encouraged me in faith. Help me to see opportunities today to build others up with words and actions that point them to Christ and strengthen their walk with You.

Go Deeper

Look for one specific way to encourage a fellow believer today—perhaps through a text, phone call, or face-to-face conversation that acknowledges God’s work in their life. Consider: How has someone’s encouragement strengthened your faith, and how might God use you to provide that same gift to another?
