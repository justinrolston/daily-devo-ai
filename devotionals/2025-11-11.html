Daily Devotional

November 11, 2025

[Do Not Love the World] Do not love the world or the things in the world. If anyone loves the world, the love of the Father is not in him. For all that is in the world—the desires of the flesh and the desires of the eyes and pride of life—is not from the Father but is from the world.
1 John 2:15-16 (ESV)

Set Your Heart on Things Above

Reflection

What does this text teach us? John’s stark warning cuts through any comfortable middle ground: we cannot simultaneously love the world and love the Father. This isn’t merely about avoiding obviously sinful behaviors—it’s about the fundamental orientation of our hearts. The “world” here refers not to God’s good creation, but to the fallen system of values, priorities, and desires that operates in rebellion against God.

John identifies three specific manifestations of worldly love: the desires of the flesh (our craving for physical and sensual gratification), the desires of the eyes (our longing for material possessions and visual stimulation), and the pride of life (our hunger for status, recognition, and self-exaltation). These same three temptations that Satan used against Eve in the garden and against Christ in the wilderness continue to assault believers today.

The sobering reality is that these worldly desires aren’t merely external pressures—they find fertile ground in our own hearts. Even as believers, we feel the magnetic pull toward finding our identity in what we own, what we achieve, or how others perceive us. We’re tempted to seek satisfaction in temporary pleasures rather than eternal joys.

But John’s warning comes with an implicit promise: the love of the Father can be in us. When God’s love takes root in our hearts, it gradually displaces our love for the world’s empty promises. We begin to see that the Father’s love offers what the world can never deliver—lasting satisfaction, unshakeable identity, and eternal security.

This transformation doesn’t happen overnight. Sanctification is a lifelong process of having our desires realigned with God’s heart. The Spirit works through God’s Word, prayer, and Christian community to weaken our attachment to worldly things and strengthen our delight in spiritual realities.

The practical implications touch every area of life: how we spend our money, what entertains us, how we measure success, and where we turn for comfort in difficult times. Are we seeking our joy in God himself, or are we using God as a means to obtain worldly happiness? The answer reveals whether the love of the Father truly dwells within us.

Confessional Tie-in

“The chief end of man is to glorify God, and to enjoy him forever.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Question 1

Prayer Prompt

Father, reveal the ways my heart still chases after worldly satisfaction rather than finding my joy in you. Transform my desires so that I might love what you love and treasure what has eternal value rather than temporary pleasure.

Go Deeper

Examine your recent decisions about time, money, and attention—what do they reveal about what you truly love? Consider memorizing this passage and asking the Spirit to bring it to mind when you feel drawn toward finding ultimate satisfaction in worldly things rather than in God himself.
