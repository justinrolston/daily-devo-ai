For our sake he made him to be sin who knew no sin, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God.
2 Corinthians 5:21 (ESV)

The Great Exchange

Reflection

What does this remarkable verse teach us? Paul presents one of Scripture’s most profound truths: the great exchange that lies at the heart of our salvation. Christ, who was absolutely sinless, was treated as if He had committed every sin we have ever committed or ever will commit. In return, we who are thoroughly sinful are treated as if we had lived Christ’s perfect life.

This exchange required nothing less than the Son of God. Only One who “knew no sin” could bear the weight of humanity’s guilt. Jesus didn’t merely take on the appearance of sin or experience its consequences—God “made him to be sin” for us. This means our sin was legally transferred to Christ’s account, and God’s wrath against that sin was poured out on His beloved Son at Calvary.

But the exchange didn’t stop with Christ taking our sin. The verse’s climactic purpose is “so that in him we might become the righteousness of God.” This isn’t merely forgiveness—it’s far greater. We don’t just have our debt canceled; we’re credited with infinite riches. Christ’s perfect righteousness becomes our own, not as something we achieve, but as something we receive by faith alone.

Consider what this means for your daily struggles with guilt and inadequacy. When you fail, remember that Christ has already borne the punishment for that failure. When you feel unworthy to approach God, remember that you stand clothed in Christ’s own righteousness. This isn’t a righteousness that fluctuates with your performance—it’s God’s own righteousness, perfect and unchanging.

This great exchange also transforms how we view our ongoing battle with sin. We fight not to earn God’s favor, but from the security of already possessing it. We pursue holiness not out of fear, but out of gratitude for the One who became sin so that we might become righteous.

The marvel is that this exchange was entirely God’s initiative—”for our sake he made him to be sin.” While we were still enemies, God orchestrated our rescue through the costliest gift imaginable: His own Son.

Confessional Tie-in

“The Lord Jesus, by His perfect obedience, and sacrifice of Himself, which He through the eternal Spirit, once offered up unto God, has fully satisfied the justice of His Father; and purchased, not only reconciliation, but an everlasting inheritance in the kingdom of heaven, for those whom the Father has given unto Him.” — Westminster Confession of Faith 8.5

Prayer Prompt

Father, I marvel that You would make Your sinless Son to be sin for me. Thank You for this great exchange—my sin for His righteousness. Help me live today in the confidence of this truth, neither presuming on Your grace nor forgetting the security I have in Christ alone.

Go Deeper

Meditate on how this exchange affects your identity: you are not defined by your worst moments but by Christ’s perfect record. Consider keeping a journal this week of moments when guilt or inadequacy arise, then remind yourself of your true standing before God in Christ.
