I hasten and do not delay to keep your commandments.
Psalm 119:60 (ESV)

Daily Devotional

Title: No More Delay: The Urgency of a Willing Heart

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Psalm 119, the longest chapter in Scripture, is an extended meditation on the beauty and sufficiency of God’s law. In verse 60, the psalmist makes a striking confession: “I hasten and do not delay to keep your commandments.” This is not the language of grudging obligation but of eager devotion. The Hebrew word translated “hasten” conveys urgency, even haste—the psalmist doesn’t drag his feet toward obedience; he runs toward it.

This matters because our natural condition, apart from grace, is precisely the opposite. Left to ourselves, we delay. We rationalize, procrastinate, and negotiate with God’s clear commands. Adam and Eve hid rather than hastened. We do the same—hesitating to forgive, delaying repentance, postponing obedience until it’s more convenient. The psalmist’s testimony, then, is not a boast of natural virtue but evidence of a transformed heart. Only the Spirit of God can produce this kind of eager responsiveness to His Word (see Psalm 119:32, “I will run in the way of your commandments when you enlarge my heart”).

This connects to the broader biblical theme of the law written on the heart, promised through the prophets (Jeremiah 31:33) and fulfilled in the new covenant. Under grace, obedience flows not from fear of punishment but from love and gratitude—the fruit of union with Christ, who Himself perfectly and eagerly did the Father’s will (Hebrews 10:7, John 4:34).

For daily living, this verse confronts our comfortable delays. Where have you said, “I’ll deal with that sin tomorrow,” or “I’ll forgive when I feel like it”? The gospel doesn’t merely forgive our delays—it reorients our desires, giving us, by grace, a taste for obedience itself. This isn’t about earning favor; it’s the grateful response of those already loved and secured in Christ. Ask the Spirit today to enlarge your heart so that swift obedience becomes not a burden, but a joy.

Confessional Tie-in: “Good works are only such as God hath commanded in His holy Word… Their ability to do good works is not at all of themselves, but wholly from the Spirit of Christ.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter 16, Sections 1 & 3

Prayer Prompt: Lord, I confess how often I delay what You have clearly commanded, choosing comfort over obedience. Thank You for Christ, who never delayed to do Your will, and whose righteousness covers my failures. By Your Spirit, enlarge my heart so I might run toward Your commands with joy, not obligation.

Go Deeper: Identify one area where you’ve been “delaying”—a conversation, a confession, an act of obedience you’ve postponed—and take one concrete step today. For deeper study, read Psalm 119:25-40 and notice how the psalmist connects renewed vitality (“revive me”) with a heart inclined toward God’s Word.
