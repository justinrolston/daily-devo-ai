Do not be deceived: God is not mocked, for whatever one sows, that will he also reap. For the one who sows to his own flesh will from the flesh reap corruption, but the one who sows to the Spirit will from the Spirit reap eternal life.
Galatians 6:7-8 (ESV)

The Unchanging Law of the Harvest

Reflection

What does this text teach us? Paul presents one of life’s most fundamental principles: the spiritual law of sowing and reaping. Just as a farmer cannot plant corn and expect wheat, we cannot live contrary to God’s design and expect blessing. This isn’t merely agricultural wisdom—it’s a divine principle woven into the fabric of creation itself.

The apostle begins with a stark warning: “Do not be deceived: God is not mocked.” The word “mocked” carries the idea of turning up one’s nose in contempt. Paul warns against the dangerous assumption that we can somehow outsmart or bypass God’s moral order. Many live as if their choices exist in a vacuum, as if God either doesn’t see or doesn’t care about the seeds they’re planting daily.

But God has established an unchanging harvest law. “Whatever one sows, that will he also reap.” This principle operates with mathematical precision in both directions. Those who “sow to the flesh”—living for immediate gratification, selfish ambition, and worldly pursuits—will inevitably “reap corruption.” The word suggests decay, futility, and ultimate disappointment. Sin promises satisfaction but delivers emptiness.

Conversely, those who “sow to the Spirit” will “reap eternal life.” This doesn’t mean we earn salvation through good deeds—Scripture clearly teaches we’re justified by faith alone. Rather, Paul describes the fruit that flows from a Spirit-transformed heart. When the Holy Spirit indwells us, He produces desires and actions that align with God’s character. We begin choosing patience over anger, generosity over greed, truth over deception.

The encouragement here is profound: every Spirit-led choice matters. Every act of kindness, every moment spent in prayer, every decision to obey rather than compromise—these are seeds that will produce a harvest of blessing, both in this life and the next.

Yet this passage also calls for honest self-examination. What kind of seeds are we planting with our time, money, words, and affections? Are we investing in relationships that honor God? Are we cultivating habits that draw us closer to Christ? The harvest is coming—not just in eternity, but often in the natural consequences we experience today.

The good news is that God’s grace enables us to sow differently. Through Christ, we’re not condemned to repeat destructive patterns. The Spirit empowers new choices that yield life and peace.

Confessional Tie-in

“The Spirit of God maketh the reading, but especially the preaching, of the word, an effectual means of convincing and converting sinners, and of building them up in holiness and comfort, through faith, unto salvation.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Q. 89

Prayer Prompt

Father, help me see clearly the seeds I’m planting daily through my choices and attitudes. Grant me wisdom to sow to the Spirit rather than the flesh, trusting that You will bring forth a harvest of righteousness in Your perfect timing.

Go Deeper

Examine one area of your life where you’ve been “sowing to the flesh”—perhaps in your thought life, spending habits, or relationships. What would it look like to sow to the Spirit in that area today? Consider how God’s patience allows time between sowing and reaping, creating space for repentance and new patterns of faithfulness.
