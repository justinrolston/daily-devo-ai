August 13, 2026

For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared beforehand, that we should walk in them.
Ephesians 2:10 (ESV)

God’s Masterpiece, Made for Purpose

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Paul has just spent nine verses demolishing any notion that we can save ourselves. We were dead in sin, by nature children of wrath, saved only by grace through faith—”not a result of works, so that no one may boast” (v. 9). Then comes verse 10, and it changes everything about how we see good works. We are not saved by works, but we are saved for them.

The word Paul uses for “workmanship” is poiema—the same root from which we get “poem.” You are God’s masterpiece, his handiwork, crafted with intention and care. Before you ever took a breath, God had already authored the good works you would walk in. This is not self-improvement; it is divine architecture. Salvation is not merely rescue from something—it is re-creation into someone.

Notice the order: created first, then good works follow. This mirrors the whole shape of the gospel. Grace always precedes gratitude. Justification always precedes sanctification. You do not perform good works to become God’s workmanship; you do good works because you already are. The Christian life is not a ladder we climb toward God, but a garden he plants and tends within us.

This truth speaks to two common struggles. To the anxious believer haunted by inadequacy, wondering if their service ever measures up: rest. God prepared your path beforehand; he is not waiting to see if you’ll figure it out on your own. To the comfortable believer coasting on grace as a license for passivity: wake up. Grace that doesn’t produce fruit isn’t biblical grace at all. We were created for good works—not as an afterthought, but as the purpose of our new life.

So walk. Not frantically, not fearfully, but faithfully—one step at a time, in the specific works God has already prepared for your specific life, today.

Confessional Tie-in:

“Good works are only such as God has commanded in his holy Word, and not such as, without the authority thereof, are devised by men… These works, done in obedience to God’s commandments, are the fruits and evidences of a true and lively faith.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter 16, Sections 1 & 2

Prayer Prompt:

Father, thank you for making me your workmanship, not by my effort but by your grace alone. Forgive me for the days I either strive anxiously to earn your favor or grow lazy, forgetting the good works you’ve prepared for me. Open my eyes today to see the specific ways you’ve called me to walk, and give me strength by your Spirit to walk in them faithfully.

Go Deeper:

Ask yourself: What good work has God placed directly in front of me today—in my home, workplace, or church—that I’ve been avoiding or overlooking? Spend a few minutes reading Ephesians 2:1–10 as a whole, tracing the movement from death to life to purpose, and consider journaling how your understanding of “good works” shifts when you see them as prepared rather than invented.
