November 03, 2025

[Submission to the Authorities] Let every person be subject to the governing authorities. For there is no authority except from God, and those that exist have been instituted by God.
Romans 13:1 (ESV)

Divine Authority and Human Government

Reflection

What does this text teach us? Paul’s instruction in Romans 13:1 presents a truth that can challenge our modern sensibilities: we are called to submit to governing authorities because God has established them. This isn’t merely pragmatic advice for keeping peace—it’s a theological statement about how God exercises His sovereign rule through human institutions.

The phrase “there is no authority except from God” reveals something profound about divine providence. God doesn’t merely permit governments to exist; He actively institutes them as part of His ordering of creation. This means that even imperfect rulers serve within God’s sovereign plan. Paul wrote these words under the Roman Empire—hardly a godly government by any measure—yet he recognized God’s hand in establishing even pagan authority.

This doesn’t mean governments are infallible or that their commands always align with God’s will. Scripture provides examples of faithful believers who disobeyed earthly rulers when their commands contradicted God’s law—think of Daniel’s friends refusing to bow to Nebuchadnezzar’s statue, or the apostles declaring “we must obey God rather than men.” The submission Paul calls for is not absolute, but it is the default posture of believers who trust God’s sovereignty.

Why does this matter for our daily walk? Because our attitude toward authority reveals our trust in God’s providence. When we chafe against legitimate governmental authority—whether it’s traffic laws, tax obligations, or civic duties—we’re often revealing a deeper struggle with God’s sovereign ordering of our world. The believer who submits to governing authorities (within biblical bounds) demonstrates faith that God is working through even imperfect systems.

This submission also serves the gospel’s advance. Peter reminds us that our good conduct should “silence the ignorance of foolish people.” When Christians are known as good citizens who respect authority while maintaining ultimate allegiance to Christ, we adorn the gospel and remove stumbling blocks to faith.

The call to submission isn’t burdensome legalism—it’s an expression of faith in our sovereign God who works all things according to His perfect will.

Confessional Tie-in

“It is the duty of people to pray for magistrates, to honor their persons, to pay them tribute and other dues, to obey their lawful commands, and to be subject to their authority for conscience’ sake.” — Westminster Confession of Faith 23.4

Prayer Prompt

Father, help me trust Your sovereignty even when I struggle to understand Your ways. Grant me a heart that submits to the authorities You have established, while keeping my ultimate allegiance to You. Make me a faithful citizen who honors You through respect for legitimate authority.

Go Deeper

This week, examine your attitudes toward authority—governmental, workplace, or church leadership. Are there areas where prideful resistance might be masking a deeper struggle with God’s sovereignty? Consider how your respectful submission to legitimate authority might serve as a witness to God’s order and character.
