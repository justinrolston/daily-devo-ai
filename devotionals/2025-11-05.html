For because of this you also pay taxes, for the authorities are ministers of God, attending to this very thing.
Romans 13:6 (ESV)

God’s Ministers in Unexpected Places

Reflection

What does Romans 13:6 teach us about the nature of earthly authority? Paul makes a remarkable claim: “the authorities are ministers of God.” The Greek word for “ministers” here is leitourgoi - the same term used for priests serving in the temple. This isn’t mere political commentary; it’s a profound theological statement about how God governs His world.

In context, Paul has just explained that governing authorities are established by God to maintain order and punish wrongdoing. Now he addresses the practical question every believer faces: Why should we pay taxes to earthly governments? His answer cuts deeper than civic duty - we pay because these officials, whether they acknowledge it or not, serve as God’s appointed servants in maintaining social order.

This truth confronts our natural tendency to view government as merely human institution. Even when leaders are flawed or policies seem misguided, God sovereignly uses earthly authority as one means of restraining evil and promoting the common good. This doesn’t make every governmental action righteous, but it does mean that the institution itself operates under divine appointment.

Consider how this transforms our attitude toward civic responsibility. Paying taxes isn’t merely legal obligation - it’s participation in God’s providential ordering of society. When we fulfill these duties, we’re acknowledging God’s sovereignty over all spheres of life, not just the explicitly religious ones.

This passage also reminds us that God’s kingdom advances through various means. While the church proclaims the gospel and administers the sacraments, civil authorities maintain the peace necessary for human flourishing. Both serve divine purposes, though in different ways.

For believers living under governments that may be indifferent or even hostile to faith, Paul’s words offer both comfort and challenge. Comfort, because God remains sovereign over all earthly powers. Challenge, because we’re called to honor these authorities as God’s servants, even when it costs us financially or personally.

This doesn’t mean blind obedience to everything government demands, but it does mean approaching civic duties with the recognition that we’re ultimately serving God through these earthly structures. Our tax payments, jury service, and civic engagement become acts of worship when done in acknowledgment of God’s sovereign rule over all creation.

Confessional Tie-in

“God, the supreme Lord and King of all the world, hath ordained civil magistrates, to be, under Him, over the people, for His own glory, and the public good: and, to this end, hath armed them with the power of the sword, for the defence and encouragement of them that are good, and for the punishment of evil doers.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter 23.1

Prayer Prompt

Father, help me to see Your sovereign hand even in earthly authorities. Grant me wisdom to honor civil government as Your appointed means of maintaining order, while keeping my ultimate allegiance to Your kingdom. When civic duties feel burdensome, remind me that I’m serving You through faithful citizenship.

Go Deeper

This week, as you handle civic responsibilities - whether paying bills with taxes included or participating in community life - pause to acknowledge God’s sovereignty over these earthly structures. Consider how your faithful participation in civic duties can be a quiet witness to God’s ordering of all creation.
