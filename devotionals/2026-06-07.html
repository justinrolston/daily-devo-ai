Before the mountains were brought forth, or ever you had formed the earth and the world, from everlasting to everlasting you are God. For a thousand years in your sight are but as yesterday when it is past, or as a watch in the night.
Psalm 90:2,4 (ESV)

The God Who Transcends Time

Reflection

What does this text teach us about the nature of God? Psalm 90:2,4 reveals two profound truths that should anchor our hearts in times of uncertainty and change. First, God exists in eternal permanence—He was God before creation began and will remain God forever. Second, God’s relationship to time is fundamentally different from ours—what seems like an eternity to us is but a moment in His sight.

Moses, the author of this psalm, understood something crucial about human frailty in contrast to divine eternality. While mountains, which seem so permanent and enduring to us, are merely temporary formations that God “brought forth,” the Lord Himself exists “from everlasting to everlasting.” Before anything we consider ancient or stable existed, God was already there in His fullness and glory.

The second verse provides a striking perspective on time itself. When we face trials that seem to drag on endlessly, when we wait for prayers to be answered, or when we wonder if God has forgotten us, we must remember that “a thousand years in your sight are but as yesterday when it is past, or as a watch in the night.” This isn’t meant to minimize our suffering, but to remind us that God’s timing operates on an entirely different scale than ours.

This truth should deeply comfort believers. The God who saves us is not bound by the limitations that constrain us. He doesn’t grow weary with the passage of time, forget His promises as years pass, or lose sight of our needs in the vastness of eternity. The same God who spoke creation into existence is intimately involved in the details of our daily lives.

Yet this passage also calls for humility. Our human perspective is incredibly limited. We see only moments and seasons, while God sees the entire sweep of history as one unified plan. This should cultivate patience in our hearts as we trust His timing for answered prayer, for the resolution of difficulties, and for the ultimate fulfillment of His kingdom purposes.

When anxiety about the future grips us, or when present circumstances seem overwhelming, we can find peace in knowing that our eternal, unchanging God holds all of time in His hands. He who has always been God will continue to be God, working all things together for His glory and our good.

Confessional Tie-in

“God is a Spirit, infinite, eternal, and unchangeable, in his being, wisdom, power, holiness, justice, goodness, and truth.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Question 4

Prayer Prompt

Eternal God, help me to rest in Your perfect timing and unchanging character. When I am anxious about tomorrow or overwhelmed by today, remind me that You hold all of time in Your sovereign hands. Grant me patience to trust Your wisdom when I cannot see Your purposes.

Go Deeper

Consider keeping a journal of how God has worked in your life over time, noting His faithfulness in ways you couldn’t see in the moment. Reflect on this question: How might viewing your current circumstances from God’s eternal perspective change your response to them today?
