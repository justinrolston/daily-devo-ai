And they went with haste and found Mary and Joseph, and the baby lying in a manger. And when they saw it, they made known the saying that had been told them concerning this child. And all who heard it wondered at what the shepherds told them. But Mary treasured up all these things, pondering them in her heart. And the shepherds returned, glorifying and praising God for all they had heard and seen, as it had been told them.
Luke 2:16-20 (ESV)

Treasuring Christ in Our Hearts

Reflection

What does this beautiful nativity scene teach us about how we should respond to the revelation of Christ? In these verses, we witness three distinct responses to the birth of Jesus, each offering instruction for our own walk with God.

The shepherds responded with immediate obedience and haste. When the angel proclaimed the good news, they didn’t hesitate or debate—they went quickly to see what God had revealed. Their response teaches us that divine revelation demands action. When God speaks through His Word, we must not be hearers only, but doers. The shepherds’ urgency reflects the appropriate response to the gospel: Christ’s coming is news too wonderful to delay acting upon.

Notice also how the shepherds became witnesses. After seeing the Christ child, “they made known the saying that had been told them.” They couldn’t keep silent about what they had experienced. This is the natural overflow of true encounter with Christ—we must tell others. The gospel creates witnesses, not merely recipients.

But perhaps most striking is Mary’s response: she “treasured up all these things, pondering them in her heart.” While others wondered and the shepherds proclaimed, Mary quietly meditated. The word “treasured” suggests she carefully stored these events like precious jewels, turning them over in her mind repeatedly. She understood that God’s works deserve deep contemplation, not just momentary amazement.

Mary’s pondering teaches us something crucial about spiritual maturity. In our age of instant everything, we often want immediate understanding of God’s ways. But Mary shows us the discipline of patient reflection. She didn’t demand immediate answers to the mystery unfolding before her. Instead, she trusted God’s timing while carefully considering His works.

This passage reveals that God’s revelation should produce both immediate response and lifelong meditation. Like the shepherds, we must act quickly when God calls. Like Mary, we must cultivate hearts that treasure God’s Word and works, pondering them deeply over time. Both responses flow from faith—active obedience and contemplative worship are not opposites but complementary aspects of mature discipleship.

The shepherds returned “glorifying and praising God,” having found everything exactly as promised. When we respond rightly to God’s revelation, the result is always worship. Our obedience and meditation should culminate in praise for His faithfulness.

Confessional Tie-in

“The whole counsel of God, concerning all things necessary for his own glory, man’s salvation, faith, and life, is either expressly set down in Scripture, or by good and necessary consequence may be deduced from Scripture.” — Westminster Confession of Faith 1.6

Prayer Prompt

Father, help me respond to Your Word with both the shepherds’ urgency and Mary’s contemplative heart. Give me grace to act quickly in obedience while also treasuring Your works through patient meditation, that my life might glorify You.

Go Deeper

Consider keeping a spiritual journal where you can “treasure up” God’s works in your life like Mary did. What recent evidence of God’s faithfulness deserves deeper pondering? How might both immediate obedience and patient reflection strengthen your faith today?
