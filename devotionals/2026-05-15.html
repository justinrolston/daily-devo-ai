As you do not know the way the spirit comes to the bones in the womb of a woman with child, so you do not know the work of God who makes everything.
Ecclesiastes 11:5 (ESV)

The Mystery of God’s Hidden Ways

Reflection

What does this text teach us? Solomon presents us with a profound comparison that should humble every believer. Just as we cannot fully comprehend the miraculous formation of life in the womb—how breath enters bones, how spirit animates flesh—so we cannot fully grasp the complete scope of God’s work in His creation.

The Hebrew word for “spirit” here can mean both breath and the life-giving essence that God imparts. This reminds us of Genesis 2:7, where God breathed into Adam the breath of life. Every child conceived carries this same divine mystery—life that comes only from the Creator’s hand, following patterns and purposes beyond our complete understanding.

But Solomon’s point extends far beyond biology. If we cannot fully understand even the physical processes we can observe, how much more should we acknowledge our limitations when it comes to God’s eternal purposes? His ways are not our ways, and His thoughts are higher than our thoughts (Isaiah 55:8-9).

This truth confronts our modern tendency toward autonomy and control. We live in an age that promises we can master everything through technology and knowledge, yet the fundamental mysteries of life and God’s providence remain beautifully beyond our grasp. This isn’t a failure of human reason—it’s a reminder of our creatureliness and God’s infinite wisdom.

For the believer, this brings both humility and comfort. Humility, because it reminds us that we are not God and cannot see the full picture of His plan. Comfort, because it assures us that our loving Father is orchestrating all things according to His perfect will, even when we cannot understand His methods or timing.

When we face circumstances that seem senseless, relationships that bring pain, or prayers that appear unanswered, Ecclesiastes 11:5 calls us to trust in God’s hidden wisdom. Just as He forms each child in the womb according to His design, He is forming us and our circumstances according to His eternal purposes. Our inability to see the complete picture doesn’t diminish His perfect knowledge or loving care.

This mystery should drive us not to frustration, but to worship. The God who knows how to form spirit and bones in secret places also knows how to work all things together for our good and His glory.

Confessional Tie-in

“God’s works of providence are his most holy, wise, and powerful preserving and governing all his creatures, and all their actions.”
— Westminster Shorter Catechism, Q&A 11

Prayer Prompt

Father, I acknowledge that Your ways are far beyond my understanding. Help me trust in Your perfect wisdom when I cannot see Your purposes clearly. Grant me the humility to rest in Your sovereignty over all things, including the mysteries of my own life.

Go Deeper

Consider areas of your life where you’re struggling to understand God’s purposes. Instead of demanding answers, practice surrendering these mysteries to His wise care. Reflect on Deuteronomy 29:29: “The secret things belong to the LORD our God, but the things that are revealed belong to us and to our children forever.”
