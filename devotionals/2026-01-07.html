[Walk in Love] Therefore be imitators of God, as beloved children. And walk in love, as Christ loved us and gave himself up for us, a fragrant offering and sacrifice to God.
Ephesians 5:1-2 (ESV)

Walking in the Love That Defines Us

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Paul begins with “therefore,” connecting these verses to the preceding passage about putting off the old self and being renewed in Christ. Because we have been transformed by God’s grace, we are now called to be “imitators of God, as beloved children.” This phrase reveals both our identity and our calling—we are God’s beloved children, and from this secure position, we naturally imitate our heavenly Father.

The command to “walk in love” is not merely moral instruction but flows from our union with Christ. Paul immediately defines what this love looks like by pointing to Christ’s sacrificial love for us. He “gave himself up for us, a fragrant offering and sacrifice to God.” This wasn’t just an act of love—it was love itself made manifest, pleasing to the Father as a sweet aroma.

Notice the beautiful progression: we are beloved children who imitate God by walking in the same love Christ demonstrated. This isn’t about mustering up feelings of affection but about sacrificial action that mirrors Christ’s self-giving nature. The Greek word for “walk” (peripateo) suggests our entire way of life, not just occasional acts of kindness.

This love is distinctly Christian because it finds its pattern and power in Christ’s atoning work. When Christ gave himself up for us, he wasn’t just providing an example—he was accomplishing our redemption and securing our adoption as God’s children. Now, empowered by his Spirit and assured of our belovedness, we can love others with the same self-sacrificing love.

The “fragrant offering” language draws from Old Testament worship, where acceptable sacrifices produced a pleasing aroma to God. Our love-motivated actions, rooted in Christ’s perfect sacrifice, become acts of worship that honor our Father. This transforms ordinary acts of service, forgiveness, and kindness into sacred offerings.

Walking in love means daily choices to put others’ needs before our own comfort, to forgive as we have been forgiven, and to serve sacrificially. It’s costly love—love that gives up rights, convenience, and self-interest. Yet we can love this way because we are secure in our Father’s love, knowing that Christ’s sacrifice has already secured our acceptance with God.

Confessional Tie-in: “The principal acts of saving faith are accepting, receiving, and resting upon Christ alone for justification, sanctification, and eternal life, by virtue of the covenant of grace.” — Westminster Confession of Faith 14.2

Prayer Prompt: Father, thank you for adopting me as your beloved child through Christ’s sacrifice. Help me to walk today in the love that Christ showed me, giving myself sacrificially for others as an offering pleasing to you.

Go Deeper: Consider one relationship where you can demonstrate Christ-like sacrificial love today. What would it look like to “give yourself up” for that person’s good? Reflect on how your secure identity as God’s beloved child frees you to love others without seeking anything in return.
