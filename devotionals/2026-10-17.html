The friendship of the LORD is for those who fear him, and he makes known to them his covenant. My eyes are ever toward the LORD, for he will pluck my feet out of the net.
Psalm 25:14-15 (ESV)

Friendship with God

Title: The Secret Place of Friendship

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? David declares that the LORD offers something astonishing: friendship. Not merely the relationship of a sovereign to a subject, but intimate fellowship—the kind where secrets are shared. The Hebrew word translated “friendship” can also mean “counsel” or “intimate conversation.” God draws near to those who fear him, drawing back the curtain on his covenant purposes, letting them in on what he is doing in the world and in their lives.

This friendship is not earned through human merit or cleverness. It belongs to “those who fear him”—those who stand in reverent awe before a holy God, who recognize both his majesty and their own need. This fear is not terror but the posture of a humbled heart that trusts God completely. It is the same posture seen throughout Scripture: Abraham called God’s friend (James 2:23), Moses speaking with God face to face, and ultimately believers today, who through Christ’s finished work, have been brought near to God as sons and daughters rather than strangers (Ephesians 2:13).

Notice what follows this declaration of friendship: “My eyes are ever toward the LORD.” This is not passive sentiment but active dependence. David lives in a world full of snares—enemies, temptations, circumstances threatening to entangle his feet. Yet his eyes remain fixed on the LORD, trusting him to deliver. This is the posture of faith: not the absence of danger, but constant, deliberate attention toward the only One who can free us.

For believers today, the net may look different—anxiety, besetting sin, relational conflict, the slow erosion of hope—but the pattern remains the same. We are not called to free ourselves through willpower or escape through distraction. We are called to fix our eyes on the LORD, trusting that the same covenant friendship extended to David has been secured for us through Christ, who was himself caught in the ultimate net of sin and death on our behalf, and was raised in victory.

Confessional Tie-in:

“Q. What is the chief end of man? A. Man’s chief end is to glorify God, and to enjoy him forever.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Q.1

Prayer Prompt:

Lord, I confess my eyes drift so easily toward my troubles rather than toward you. Thank you for calling me friend through the blood of Christ, not because I have earned it, but because you have drawn me near. Keep my eyes fixed on you today, and free my feet from whatever net now entangles me.

Go Deeper:

Spend a few minutes today naming the “net” currently pressing on you—worry, temptation, discouragement—and consciously turn your eyes toward the LORD in prayer rather than toward the problem itself. Consider reading James 2:14-24 to reflect further on what it means to be called “a friend of God,” and ask: where might reverent fear be missing from my daily walk with Christ?
