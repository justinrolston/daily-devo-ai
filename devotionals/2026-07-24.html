It is well with the man who deals generously and lends; who conducts his affairs with justice.
Psalm 112:5 (ESV)

Well-Being Rooted in Righteousness

Title: The Blessed Life of Open Hands

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Psalm 112 paints a portrait of the righteous person—someone who fears the Lord and delights in His commands (v. 1). Verse 5 zooms in on a specific mark of that righteousness: generosity paired with justice. “It is well with the man who deals generously and lends; who conducts his affairs with justice.” This isn’t a formula for prosperity but a description of the fruit that grows from a heart transformed by God.

Notice the pairing here—generosity and justice aren’t separated. It’s not enough to be generous while being careless or dishonest in other dealings. True righteousness integrates compassion toward others with integrity in all our affairs, whether financial, professional, or relational. This wholeness reflects the character of God Himself, who is both abundantly gracious and perfectly just.

This connects to a larger biblical theme: those who have received grace become channels of grace. Throughout Scripture, God’s people are called to open-handed generosity precisely because God has been generous with them (Deuteronomy 15:10-11; 2 Corinthians 9:6-8). We do not lend and give in order to earn God’s favor—we give because we’ve already received unmerited favor in Christ. Our generosity flows from gratitude, not from a transaction to secure blessing.

The phrase “it is well” doesn’t promise material wealth as a guaranteed outcome. Rather, it speaks to a deeper contentment and stability that comes from living in step with God’s character. There’s a settled peace in the soul of one who isn’t gripping tightly to possessions, who trusts God enough to release resources for others’ good.

For believers today, this raises searching questions: Where do I hold too tightly? Am I generous only when convenient, or does generosity mark my character? Do I conduct my business, my budget, my relationships with the same justice I’d want extended to me? Sanctification means the Spirit is slowly loosening our grip on self-interest, replacing it with Christ-like generosity and integrity—not as a burden, but as increasing freedom.

Confessional Tie-in: “Q. 111: What does the eighth commandment require? A. That I do whatever I can for my neighbor’s good, that I treat others as I would like them to treat me, and that I work faithfully so that I may share with those in need.” — Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 111

Prayer Prompt: Father, thank You for the abundant grace You’ve poured out on me through Christ, though I deserved none of it. Forgive me for the times I’ve clutched my resources out of fear or self-interest rather than trusting Your provision. Teach me to give generously and act justly, reflecting Your character in every corner of my life.

Go Deeper: This week, identify one area—financial, relational, or professional—where you can practice deliberate generosity or greater integrity. Ask yourself: is there someone I’ve been withholding help from, or a business practice I’ve justified rather than aligned with justice? Consider reading 2 Corinthians 8-9 for a fuller picture of gospel-motivated giving.
