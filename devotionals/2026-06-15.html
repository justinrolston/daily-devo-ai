A Father’s Greatest Joy

Proverbs 23:24 (ESV) “The father of the righteous will greatly rejoice; he who fathers a wise son will be glad in him.”

Reflection

What does this text teach us about the nature of true parental joy? Solomon presents us with a profound truth: the greatest satisfaction a father can experience comes not from his child’s worldly success, but from their righteousness and wisdom. This proverb reveals both the heart of godly parenting and the character that brings lasting joy to families.

The Hebrew word for “greatly rejoice” suggests exuberant celebration, the kind of deep gladness that wells up from the soul. Notice that this joy isn’t connected to a child’s achievements, wealth, or social status, but to their moral character. A “righteous” child is one who walks in alignment with God’s ways, while a “wise” child demonstrates the fear of the Lord that Proverbs identifies as wisdom’s beginning.

This principle reflects our heavenly Father’s heart toward us. Just as earthly fathers find their deepest satisfaction in their children’s righteousness, God the Father delights in His children who walk in wisdom and holiness. Through Christ’s perfect righteousness imputed to us, we become sources of the Father’s joy. Yet this text also calls us to pursue practical holiness—growing in wisdom, making choices that honor God, and living lives marked by biblical righteousness.

For parents, this proverb reorders our priorities. While we naturally desire our children’s happiness and success, our primary concern should be their spiritual formation. Are we more excited about report cards or evidence of growing godliness? Do we celebrate achievements in sports and academics with the same enthusiasm we show for acts of kindness, truthfulness, or love for God’s Word?

For adult children, this passage challenges us to consider what kind of joy we bring to our parents. Regardless of our age, we can still be sources of gladness through lives marked by wisdom and righteousness. Even when earthly parents are absent or imperfect, we can live knowing that our heavenly Father rejoices over our growth in holiness.

The gospel transforms both our failures and our progress. When we fall short of righteousness, Christ’s perfect record covers us. When we grow in wisdom, it’s by His Spirit’s power. This removes both despair and pride, leaving us free to pursue the kind of character that brings joy to our Father’s heart.

Confessional Tie-in

“The chief end of man is to glorify God, and to enjoy him forever.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Question 1

Prayer Prompt

Heavenly Father, thank You that through Christ’s righteousness, I am a source of Your joy. Help me to prioritize wisdom and righteousness over worldly success, both in my own life and in how I encourage others. Grant me grace to live in ways that honor You and bring gladness to Your heart.

Go Deeper

Consider what brings you the most joy about the people you love—is it their character or their achievements? Ask the Lord to help you celebrate and encourage righteousness and wisdom in your relationships. Reflect on how your life choices either bring joy to your heavenly Father or grieve His Spirit.
