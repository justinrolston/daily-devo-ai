December 01, 2025

[The Word Became Flesh] In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. He was in the beginning with God. And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we have seen his glory, glory as of the only Son from the Father, full of grace and truth.
John 1:1-2, 14 (ESV)

The Word Who Became Like Us

Reflection

What does this profound passage teach us about the nature of Jesus Christ? John’s opening verses present us with one of the most extraordinary claims in all of Scripture: the eternal Word of God became human flesh and lived among us.

Notice how John begins—”In the beginning was the Word.” This echoes Genesis 1:1, but while Genesis tells us about the beginning of creation, John reveals that the Word existed before any beginning we can imagine. The Word didn’t come into being; He simply was. This Word wasn’t merely with God as a separate entity—He was God, fully divine, sharing in the very essence of the Godhead.

Yet verse 14 presents the stunning turn: this eternal, divine Word “became flesh.” The Greek word for “became” indicates a real change, not just an appearance. The infinite God took on finite humanity. The invisible became visible. The untouchable became touchable. This isn’t mythology or metaphor—this is the historical reality of the Incarnation.

John emphasizes that the Word “dwelt among us.” The Greek word literally means “pitched His tent” or “tabernacled” among us. Just as God’s glory once filled the tabernacle in the wilderness, now His glory dwells in human flesh. Jesus didn’t merely visit earth; He made His home here, experiencing our joys and sorrows, our temptations and struggles.

What does this mean for us today? First, it assures us that God truly understands our human condition. When we face trials, we can know that our Savior has walked in our shoes. When we’re tempted, we can turn to One who faced temptation yet remained sinless. When we feel alone, we can remember that God Himself chose to experience human loneliness.

Second, the Incarnation validates our humanity. God didn’t rescue us from a distance—He entered our world completely. Our physical bodies, our emotions, our daily struggles matter to Him because He took them on Himself.

Finally, this passage calls us to marvel at God’s grace. The eternal Word didn’t become flesh out of necessity, but out of love. He came not to condemn the world, but to save it, displaying His glory “full of grace and truth.”

Confessional Tie-in

“The Son of God, the second person in the Trinity, being very and eternal God, of one substance and equal with the Father, did, when the fullness of time was come, take upon him man’s nature, with all the essential properties, and common infirmities thereof, yet without sin.” — Westminster Confession of Faith 8.2

Prayer Prompt

Lord Jesus, I marvel that You, the eternal Word, would become flesh for my sake. Thank You for entering fully into human experience so that I might know Your grace and truth. Help me to live today with wonder at Your great love.

Go Deeper

Consider throughout your day how Jesus’ full humanity enables Him to sympathize with your specific struggles and joys. Reflect on this question: How does knowing that God became flesh change the way you approach Him in prayer and worship?
