And he sat down and called the twelve. And he said to them, “If anyone would be first, he must be last of all and servant of all.”
Mark 9:35 (ESV)

The Upside-Down Kingdom

Title: The Upside-Down Kingdom

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Jesus has just overheard his disciples arguing about which of them was the greatest. Rather than scold them from a distance, he sits down—the posture of a rabbi preparing to teach something important—and calls the twelve close. His answer overturns everything they assumed about greatness: “If anyone would be first, he must be last of all and servant of all.”

This is not merely good advice for getting along with others. It is a glimpse into the very nature of God’s kingdom, which operates by entirely different values than the world around us. The disciples were thinking in terms of status, position, and recognition—categories the world still prizes today. Jesus redefines greatness as self-giving service, and he does so not as an abstract ideal but as a description of his own mission. He who was first became last; he who deserved all glory took the form of a servant and gave his life as a ransom for many (Mark 10:45).

Here we see both the pattern and the power for Christian living. The pattern is clear: we are called to lay down our desire for recognition and instead look for ways to serve those around us—in our homes, churches, and workplaces. But where does the power come from? Not from gritted teeth and self-improvement, but from union with Christ, who modeled this servanthood perfectly and now works it in us by his Spirit. Our old nature craves the applause of others; the Spirit produces in us a quiet willingness to be overlooked for the sake of love.

This teaching exposes our pride but also comforts us. We are not called to earn greatness by our service—Christ has already secured our standing before God through his finished work. Freed from needing to prove ourselves, we are free to serve without keeping score, trusting that true honor comes from the Father who sees in secret.

Confessional Tie-in:

“Man’s chief end is to glorify God, and to enjoy him forever.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Q.1

Prayer Prompt:

Lord Jesus, you humbled yourself to serve us even unto death—forgive me for craving recognition and comparing myself to others. Thank you that my worth is secure in you, not in human praise. Teach me today, by your Spirit, to find joy in quietly serving those you’ve placed in my path.

Go Deeper:

Consider one person today whose needs you could meet without seeking credit or thanks. As you do, reflect on Philippians 2:5-8—how does Christ’s example reshape your definition of greatness, and where is pride still competing for first place in your heart?
