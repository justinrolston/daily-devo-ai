And Jesus came and said to them, “All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me. Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you. And behold, I am with you always, to the end of the age.”
Matthew 28:18-20 (ESV)

The Great Commission: Authority, Mission, and Promise

Reflection

What does this text teach us? In these final words of Matthew’s Gospel, Jesus makes three profound declarations that transform how we understand our Christian calling. First, He claims absolute authority—not merely regional or temporary power, but complete dominion over all creation, both heavenly and earthly realms. This isn’t a boast but a statement of divine reality following His victory over sin and death.

From this position of supreme authority flows our second truth: the Great Commission itself. The command to “go and make disciples” isn’t optional for followers of Christ. Notice the comprehensive scope—”all nations”—revealing God’s heart for people from every tribe, tongue, and kingdom. This mission involves both baptism (marking entrance into the covenant community) and teaching (ongoing formation in Christ’s ways). The gospel isn’t merely about individual salvation but about creating communities of disciples who live under Christ’s lordship.

Yet Jesus doesn’t send us out alone. His third declaration provides the foundation for courage: “I am with you always, to the end of the age.” This isn’t merely emotional comfort but a promise of His active, powerful presence enabling what seems impossible. The same authority that commands the mission also sustains those who carry it out.

How does this apply to our daily lives? We often compartmentalize evangelism as something for “gifted” Christians or overseas missionaries. But Christ’s authority means His commission extends to every believer in every circumstance. Whether in our workplaces, neighborhoods, or families, we’re called to make disciples through both our words and our transformed lives.

This passage also confronts our fears about sharing faith. We worry about rejection, inadequacy, or saying the wrong thing. But remember—we serve under Christ’s absolute authority and with His guaranteed presence. The results belong to Him; faithfulness belongs to us.

Finally, discipleship isn’t merely about conversion but about “teaching them to observe all that I have commanded.” Our calling includes helping new believers grow in understanding and obedience. This happens through patient relationship, biblical instruction, and modeling what it means to follow Jesus.

Christ’s authority gives weight to His commands and certainty to His promises. In a world that feels chaotic and resistant to the gospel, we can act with confidence because our King reigns supreme.

Confessional Tie-in

“The visible church, which is also catholic or universal under the gospel (not confined to one nation, as before under the law), consists of all those throughout the world that profess the true religion; and of their children: and is the kingdom of the Lord Jesus Christ, the house and family of God.” — Westminster Confession of Faith 25.2

Prayer Prompt

Lord Jesus, You hold all authority in heaven and earth. Help me live today under Your lordship, looking for opportunities to make disciples through both word and deed. Give me courage to share Your love and faithfulness to teach others Your ways, trusting in Your promised presence.

Go Deeper

Consider one specific relationship where God might be calling you to more intentionally make disciples—whether through initial gospel conversations or helping a newer believer grow. What would faithful obedience look like in that relationship this week? Reflect on how Christ’s supreme authority should shape your confidence in His mission.
