September 28, 2026

The natural person does not accept the things of the Spirit of God, for they are folly to him, and he is not able to understand them because they are spiritually discerned.
1 Corinthians 2:14 (ESV)

Eyes That Cannot See

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Paul is addressing why the wisdom of the cross often strikes unbelievers as nonsense. He has just explained that the Spirit reveals to believers “the thoughts of God” (v. 11), truths hidden from ordinary human perception. Now he draws a stark line: the “natural person”—someone operating merely on human faculties, without the Spirit of God—cannot accept spiritual truth. It isn’t simply that they disagree with it or find it unconvincing. Paul says it is folly to them, and they are not able to understand it. This is not a matter of intelligence or effort. It is a matter of spiritual capacity.

This should reshape how we think about unbelief. We often assume that if we just present the gospel clearly enough, logically enough, winsomely enough, people will believe. But Scripture teaches something more sobering and more hopeful: apart from the Spirit’s work, the gospel appears as foolishness (1 Corinthians 1:18). This is why Jesus told Nicodemus that no one can even see the kingdom of God without being born again (John 3:3). Spiritual blindness is real, and it is universal apart from grace.

But here is the hope embedded in this hard truth: if unbelief were merely an intellectual problem, our persuasive words could fix it. Since it is a spiritual problem, only God can fix it—and He does, through the Spirit who opens blind eyes and softens hard hearts. This means your salvation was never about your superior discernment. It was a gift, given by the same Spirit who now “discerns all things” (v. 15) in you.

This truth should produce humility rather than pride. If you understand and treasure the gospel today, it is not because you are wiser than others—it is because grace opened your eyes. This should also fuel patient, prayerful evangelism. When someone mocks or dismisses the gospel, do not despair; pray, knowing only the Spirit can illumine what your words cannot.

Confessional Tie-in:

“Man, by his fall, having made himself incapable of life by that covenant, the Lord was pleased to make a second, commonly called the covenant of grace; wherein he freely offereth unto sinners life and salvation by Jesus Christ; requiring of them faith in him, that they may be saved, and promising to give unto all those that are ordained unto life his Holy Spirit, to make them willing and able to believe.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter 7.3

Prayer Prompt: Lord, I praise You for opening my blind eyes to see the beauty of Christ crucified. Forgive me for the pride that forgets grace alone made me able to believe. Give me patience and hope as I pray for those who still call the gospel foolishness—open their eyes as You opened mine.

Go Deeper: This week, identify one person in your life who dismisses or mocks the gospel, and commit to praying specifically for the Spirit to grant them spiritual sight. Reflect on 1 Corinthians 1:26–31—how does remembering your own calling guard against pride in evangelism? Consider journaling about a time before your conversion when spiritual truth seemed foolish to you, and thank God for the change.
