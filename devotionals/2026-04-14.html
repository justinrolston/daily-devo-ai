[The Resurrection of Christ] Now I would remind you, brothers, of the gospel I preached to you, which you received, in which you stand, For I delivered to you as of first importance what I also received: that Christ died for our sins in accordance with the Scriptures, that he was buried, that he was raised on the third day in accordance with the Scriptures...
1 Corinthians 15:1, 3-4 (ESV)

The Gospel of First Importance

Reflection

What does this text teach us? Paul reminds the Corinthians—and us—of the gospel’s essential elements, describing it as “of first importance.” Notice how Paul frames this: the gospel is something preached, received, and in which we stand. It’s not merely historical information but a living reality that upholds us daily.

The apostle identifies the gospel’s core components with remarkable precision: Christ died for our sins according to Scripture, was buried, and rose on the third day according to Scripture. Each phrase carries weight. Christ’s death wasn’t merely tragic—it was substitutionary, bearing the penalty our sins deserved. His burial confirms the reality of His death; there was no swoon or deception. His resurrection on the third day declares victory over sin, death, and Satan himself.

Paul emphasizes twice that these events occurred “in accordance with the Scriptures.” God’s redemptive plan wasn’t an emergency response to humanity’s fall but His eternal purpose woven throughout the Old Testament. From Genesis 3:15’s promise to Isaiah 53’s suffering servant, Scripture anticipated Christ’s atoning work.

Yet Paul doesn’t present this as mere doctrine to affirm but as gospel “in which you stand.” The same gospel that saved us continues to sustain us. When doubt assails, when circumstances overwhelm, when our hearts condemn us, we return to these foundational truths. Christ’s finished work remains our firm footing.

This passage also reveals the gospel’s communal nature. Paul received it, preached it, and the Corinthians received it. The gospel spreads through faithful proclamation from one believer to another. We’re both recipients and stewards of this treasure.

In our daily lives, we need this reminder of first importance. We easily become distracted by secondary matters—legitimate concerns about family, work, health, or ministry—yet Paul calls us back to what matters most. Christ died for our sins and rose victorious. This gospel truth transforms how we face each day’s challenges, knowing we stand secure in Christ’s completed work, not our fluctuating performance or circumstances.

Confessional Tie-in

“The covenant of grace was differently administered in the time of the law, and in the time of the gospel: under the law it was administered by promises, prophecies, sacrifices, circumcision, the paschal lamb, and other types and ordinances delivered to the people of the Jews, all foresignifying Christ to come; which were, for that time, sufficient and efficacious, through the operation of the Spirit, to instruct and build up the elect in faith in the promised Messiah, by whom they had full remission of sins, and eternal salvation; and is called the Old Testament.” — Westminster Confession of Faith 7.5

Prayer Prompt

Father, thank You for the gospel of first importance—that Christ died for my sins and rose victorious. Help me stand firm in this truth today, finding my security not in circumstances but in Your finished work. Grant me boldness to share this precious gospel with others.

Go Deeper

Consider how the gospel shapes your response to today’s specific challenges. When anxiety or discouragement arise, return to these foundational truths about Christ’s death and resurrection. Ask yourself: How does knowing Christ died for my sins and rose again change how I approach this situation?
