August 19, 2026

Whoever has the Son has life; whoever does not have the Son of God does not have life.
1 John 5:12 (ESV)

Life Found in a Person, Not a Performance

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? John writes with startling simplicity: life itself hinges on possessing the Son. Not admiring him. Not agreeing with facts about him. Having him—the way a branch has connection to a vine, the way a bride has union with her husband. John has spent his entire letter helping believers know they have eternal life (5:13), and here he distills the entire matter into one binary statement. There is no middle ground, no third category of people who have “some life” through partial connection to Christ. You either have the Son and therefore have life, or you don’t have him and remain in death.

This is sobering, but it is also remarkably clarifying. In a world—ancient or modern—that offers a thousand paths to meaning, spirituality, and self-improvement, John cuts through the noise. Life is not found in a philosophy, a moral code, or even sincere religious devotion detached from Christ. Life is found in a Person. This flows directly from what Jesus himself declared: “I am the way, and the truth, and the life” (John 14:6). Eternal life was not merely taught by Christ; it is possessed in him, because he himself is that life (John 1:4; 11:25).

Notice too the completeness this brings for the believer. If you belong to Christ by faith, you do not possess a flickering, uncertain life that depends on your daily performance. You possess his life, secured by his finished work, sustained by his ongoing intercession. Sanctification will follow—slowly, sometimes painfully—but it grows out of a life already given, not toward a life yet to be earned.

For those who feel they must do more to deserve God’s favor: rest. You cannot add to what is already yours in Christ. For those coasting on cultural Christianity without real union with the Son: awaken. Knowledge about Jesus is not the same as having Jesus. Examine your heart today—not to manufacture anxiety, but to seek the settled assurance that comes from genuine faith resting in a living Savior.

Confessional Tie-in:

“Q. What is your only comfort in life and death? A. That I am not my own, but belong—body and soul, in life and in death—to my faithful Savior, Jesus Christ.” — Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 1

Prayer Prompt:

Father, thank you for giving your Son so that dead sinners might have life. Forgive me for the times I have sought life in lesser things or trusted in my own efforts rather than resting fully in Christ. Draw me deeper into union with Jesus today, and give me assurance that comes only from him.

Go Deeper:

Take a few minutes to examine where your sense of spiritual security comes from—your performance, your feelings, or Christ’s finished work? Read John 15:1-11 and meditate on what it means to “abide” in the vine, then ask: Is my life visibly connected to his?
