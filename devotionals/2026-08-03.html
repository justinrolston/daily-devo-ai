But the LORD said to Samuel, “Do not look on his appearance or on the height of his stature, because I have rejected him. For the LORD sees not as man sees: man looks on the outward appearance, but the LORD looks on the heart.”
1 Samuel 16:7 (ESV)

Not by Sight: How God Truly Sees

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Samuel stands before Jesse’s impressive son Eliab, convinced this must be God’s chosen king. He looks like a king—tall, strong, commanding. But God stops him short: “Do not look on his appearance or on the height of his stature, because I have rejected him.” The Lord had already rejected one king who fit the world’s mold of leadership—Saul, who stood head and shoulders above everyone (1 Samuel 9:2). Now God teaches Samuel, and us, a different way of seeing.

This moment reveals something profound about how God operates in contrast to human judgment. We are creatures of the eye—we assess competence, worth, and potential based on what we can measure: appearance, charisma, credentials, strength. But God’s evaluation cuts beneath all of this to the heart—the seat of our desires, motives, and true allegiance.

This truth should humble us deeply. If God judged by outward appearance, none of us would stand. Our polished exteriors often mask hearts still being transformed by grace. Yet this same truth should comfort us profoundly. The God who sees every hidden flaw and secret sin is the same God who, in Christ, looks upon believers and sees the perfect righteousness of His Son credited to their account. He doesn’t merely tolerate our outward performance; He has renewed our hearts through the Spirit and continues that work daily.

This has practical weight for ordinary life. We are tempted constantly to evaluate ourselves and others by worldly metrics—success, appearance, social standing, even spiritual accomplishments visible to others. But God calls us to examine our hearts: What do I actually love? What am I trusting? Are my private thoughts and motives being conformed to Christ, not just my public actions?

David, the unimpressive youngest son tending sheep, was chosen not because his heart was perfect, but because God was preparing him—and through his line, preparing the perfect King whose heart would be perfectly pleasing to the Father.

Confessional Tie-in:

“God knoweth perfectly and infallibly the hearts and all the thoughts and intents thereof, and is intimately acquainted with the whole nature and all the actions of men.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter 2.2 (paraphrased summary of God’s omniscience)

Prayer Prompt:

Lord, You know my heart far better than I know it myself—search me and reveal what I cannot see. Thank You that in Christ, You look upon me with favor, not because of my performance, but because of His perfect righteousness given to me. Teach me today to walk humbly, examining my heart rather than merely managing my image before others.

Go Deeper:

Spend a few minutes today asking God to reveal one area where you’ve been more concerned with outward appearance than inward reality—perhaps in how you pray, serve, or relate to others. Read Psalm 139:23-24 as a model prayer for heart-level honesty before God. Consider: is there someone you’ve judged by outward measures whom God may be calling you to see differently?
