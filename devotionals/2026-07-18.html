I will praise you with an upright heart, when I learn your righteous rules.
Psalm 119:7 (ESV)

Learning That Leads to Praise

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Psalm 119:7 comes from the mouth of a worshiper who links two things we often keep separate: learning and praising. “I will praise you with an upright heart, when I learn your righteous rules.” The psalmist doesn’t say he will praise God instead of studying, or that praise is merely an emotional experience unrelated to truth. He says that as he learns God’s righteous rules—God’s law, His ways, His revealed will—praise rises naturally from an upright heart.

This connects to a theme running throughout Psalm 119, the longest chapter in Scripture, entirely devoted to the beauty and sufficiency of God’s Word. The psalmist doesn’t view God’s commands as burdensome restrictions but as revelations of God’s own righteous character. To know the law is to know something true and good about the Lawgiver Himself. This is why learning leads to praise—not because the law saves us, but because it shows us who God is: holy, just, wise, and good.

Notice too the phrase “upright heart.” Scripture is honest that none of us come to God’s law with a naturally upright heart. Left to ourselves, we suppress the truth, twist God’s commands, and resist His authority. An upright heart capable of genuine praise is itself a gift of grace, worked in us by the Spirit who writes God’s law on hearts of stone (Ezekiel 36:26). The psalmist’s praise, then, is not self-generated moral achievement but the fruit of God’s transforming work already underway in him.

For believers today, this verse offers a corrective to two common errors. Some treat Bible study as merely academic—information without adoration. Others chase worship experiences disconnected from truth. But biblical faith holds these together: the more we understand God’s Word, the more reason we have to worship Him. Sanctification isn’t gritting your teeth to obey; it’s beholding God’s righteousness more clearly and finding your heart drawn to praise Him for it, even as He conforms you to it.

When was the last time studying Scripture moved you to genuine praise, not just information gathering?

Confessional Tie-in:

“The Word of God, which is contained in the Scriptures of the Old and New Testament, is the only rule of faith and obedience… Man’s chief end is to glorify God, and to enjoy him forever.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Q&A 1

Prayer Prompt:

Lord, thank You for giving us Your Word not as burden but as gift, revealing Your righteous character. Forgive me for the times I’ve studied Scripture without letting it move my heart to worship. Give me an upright heart today, that as I learn Your ways, praise would rise naturally within me.

Go Deeper:

This week, as you read Scripture, pause after each passage and ask, “What does this show me about who God is?” before asking, “What should I do?” Consider memorizing Psalm 119:7 as a prayer to pray before opening God’s Word each morning.
