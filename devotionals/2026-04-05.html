For the love of Christ controls us, because we have concluded this: that one has died for all, therefore all have died; and he died for all, that those who live might no longer live for themselves but for him who for their sake died and was raised.
2 Corinthians 5:14-15 (ESV)

Controlled by Love

Reflection

What does it mean to be “controlled” by Christ’s love? Paul uses a powerful Greek word here—synechō—which can mean to constrain, compel, or hold together. It’s not the control of manipulation or force, but the overwhelming influence of divine love that transforms everything about how we live.

Paul’s logic is striking: Christ died for all, therefore all have died. This isn’t merely saying that Christ died in our place (though He certainly did), but that His death was so comprehensive that it legally and spiritually represents the death of everyone He came to save. When Christ died, we died with Him—to sin, to self-rule, to living for our own glory and comfort.

But death leads to new life. Those who have died with Christ now live, but not for themselves. Here lies the heart of Christian transformation: we no longer exist primarily for our own happiness, success, or fulfillment. We live for Him who died and was raised for our sake.

This radically reorients everything. The career decisions we make, the way we spend our money, how we treat our families, our response to suffering—all of it flows from this new reality. We’ve been bought with a price, and that price was nothing less than the life of God’s own Son.

Yet Paul doesn’t present this as burdensome duty. Notice he begins with love. Christ’s love for us is so magnificent, so complete, so undeserved, that it naturally controls us. When we truly grasp that the eternal Son of God willingly laid down His life for rebels like us, how can we continue living as if we belong to ourselves?

This doesn’t mean we become joyless or mechanical in our obedience. Rather, we discover that living for Christ—instead of for ourselves—is where true freedom and satisfaction are found. The self-centered life that once seemed so appealing reveals itself to be a prison. Living for the One who loved us enough to die for us becomes our greatest joy and deepest purpose.

Every morning presents us with this choice: Will I live today for myself, or for Him who died and rose for me? Christ’s love compels us toward the latter, not through guilt or pressure, but through the overwhelming beauty of His sacrificial love.

Confessional Tie-in

“The end of God’s appointing this mediator, and of his giving of his Son to be mediator, was to restore us to his favor, from which by sin we had fallen; so as by this most perfect satisfaction made to his justice by his Son, he might be righteous, and the justifier of him that believes in Jesus.” — Westminster Confession of Faith 8.5

Prayer Prompt

Father, thank You that Your Son’s love controls and compels me, not through force but through the overwhelming beauty of His sacrifice. Help me live today not for myself but for Him who died and rose for me, finding my joy and purpose in bringing glory to His name.

Go Deeper

Consider one area of your life where you’re still living primarily for yourself rather than for Christ. Ask God to help His love increasingly control that area. Meditate on the phrase “he died for all, that those who live might no longer live for themselves”—what would this look like practically in your relationships and daily choices?
