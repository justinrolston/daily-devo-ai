Now to him who is able to do far more abundantly than all that we ask or think, according to the power at work within us, to him be glory in the church and in Christ Jesus throughout all generations, forever and ever. Amen.
Ephesians 3:20-21 (ESV)

More Than We Can Imagine

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Paul closes his prayer for the Ephesians with an explosion of praise, describing God as one “able to do far more abundantly than all that we ask or think.” Notice the escalating language: not merely able to do what we ask, not even able to do more than we ask, but able to do far more abundantly beyond our largest requests and wildest imaginations. This is no small comfort tucked into a letter—it is the hinge on which the entire book turns, moving from doctrine to doxology.

Consider the context. Paul has just prayed that believers would be strengthened, rooted in love, and filled with the fullness of God—requests so vast they stagger the mind. Yet even here, he insists our prayers fall short of God’s capacity to act. Our imaginations, shaped by fear, past disappointments, or limited faith, cannot contain the scope of divine power at work in us.

That phrase matters: “according to the power at work within us.” This is not abstract omnipotence observed from a distance—it is the same resurrection power that raised Christ from the dead, now active in ordinary believers through the Holy Spirit. Your weakness, your sin, your seemingly stalled prayers do not diminish this power. God’s ability to work is not contingent on your ability to ask rightly or believe strongly enough.

For daily living, this reorients both our anxiety and our ambition. When circumstances seem impossible—a broken relationship, a besetting sin, a wayward child, a weary ministry—we are not left to human resourcefulness. And when God does work, the glory returns to Him, “in the church and in Christ Jesus,” not to our cleverness or effort. This frees us from both despair and self-congratulation. The Christian life is not measured by our strength but by trust in a God whose power exceeds our comprehension, displayed through the ordinary means of Word, prayer, and fellowship within His church—forever and ever.

Confessional Tie-in:

“God’s works of providence are, his most holy, wise, and powerful preserving and governing all his creatures and all their actions.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Q. 11

Prayer Prompt:

Father, I confess I often pray small prayers shaped by small faith, forgetting the power that raised Christ from the grave dwells in me. Thank You for working beyond my requests and imagination, for reasons rooted in Your glory rather than my worthiness. Strengthen my trust today in situations where I’ve stopped believing You can act.

Go Deeper:

Take a specific situation you’ve stopped praying about because it seems hopeless, and bring it before God again, remembering His power exceeds your imagination. Reflect: Where have I limited God to the size of my own expectations, and how might corporate worship this week remind me of His greater glory?
