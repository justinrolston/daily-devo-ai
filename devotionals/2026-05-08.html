Walk in wisdom toward outsiders, making the best use of the time. Let your speech always be gracious, seasoned with salt, so that you may know how you ought to answer each person.
Colossians 4:5-6 (ESV)

Wisdom in Word and Deed

Reflection

What does this text teach us? Paul’s instruction to the Colossians reveals two essential dimensions of Christian witness: our conduct and our conversation. The apostle calls believers to “walk in wisdom toward outsiders,” emphasizing that our entire manner of life should reflect the wisdom we have received in Christ. This wisdom is not mere human cleverness, but the divine wisdom that comes from knowing God and His ways.

The phrase “making the best use of the time” carries urgency. Paul uses a Greek word that suggests “buying up” or “redeeming” opportunities. Time is a precious commodity, and believers are called to be spiritually alert, recognizing divine appointments in everyday encounters. Every interaction with unbelievers is a potential moment of gospel witness, not through manipulation or pressure, but through authentic Christian living.

Paul then turns to our speech, commanding that it “always be gracious, seasoned with salt.” Gracious speech flows from hearts transformed by grace. It reflects kindness, thoughtfulness, and genuine care for others. The salt metaphor suggests speech that preserves, flavors, and prevents corruption – words that bring life rather than death, hope rather than despair.

Notice Paul’s realistic expectation: “so that you may know how you ought to answer each person.” He doesn’t assume we’ll always have perfect responses, but calls us to be prepared. This preparation comes through growth in God’s Word, prayer, and walking closely with Christ. Different people require different approaches – the questioning skeptic needs different responses than the hurting neighbor.

This passage reminds us that evangelism isn’t primarily about having the right technique or memorizing arguments. It’s about being the right kind of people – those whose lives have been transformed by the gospel and who naturally overflow with wisdom and grace. Our witness begins with our character, extends through our conduct, and finds expression in our conversation.

The beauty of Paul’s instruction is that it assumes the gospel has already done its transforming work in us. We don’t manufacture wisdom or force grace in our speech. Rather, as those united to Christ, we live out the reality of what God has made us to be.

Confessional Tie-in

“Good works are only such as God hath commanded in his holy Word, and not such as, without the warrant thereof, are devised by men, out of blind zeal, or upon any pretense of good intention.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, 16.1

Prayer Prompt

Lord, grant me wisdom to represent You well before unbelievers, that my life would reflect Your character. Help my words be seasoned with grace, speaking truth in love to each person You place in my path today.

Go Deeper

Consider your recent interactions with non-Christians. How might God be calling you to “redeem the time” in your workplace, neighborhood, or family relationships? Reflect on whether your speech typically builds up or tears down, and ask the Spirit to cultivate gracious words that point others toward Christ.
