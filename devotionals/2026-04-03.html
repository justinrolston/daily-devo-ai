...looking to Jesus, the founder and perfecter of our faith, who for the joy that was set before him endured the cross, despising the shame, and is seated at the right hand of the throne of God.
Hebrews 12:2 (ESV)

Running the Race with Eyes Fixed on Jesus

Reflection

What does this powerful verse from Hebrews teach us about the Christian life? The author paints a vivid picture using the metaphor of a race, urging believers to keep “looking to Jesus” as we run. This isn’t a casual glance, but an intense, sustained focus—like a runner whose eyes never leave the finish line.

Jesus is described as both the “founder and perfecter of our faith.” He is the one who begins faith in us and the one who will complete it. This reminds us that our salvation is entirely God’s work from start to finish. We don’t muster up faith from within ourselves; Christ is both its source and its completion. What comfort this brings to struggling believers who wonder if their faith is strong enough!

The verse then reveals the motivation that sustained Jesus through His suffering: “for the joy that was set before him.” What was this joy? It was the joy of accomplishing our redemption, of bringing many sons and daughters to glory, of fulfilling the Father’s will. Jesus endured the cross not as a victim, but as a victor who saw past the temporary shame to eternal glory.

Notice that Jesus “despised the shame” of the cross. In a culture obsessed with honor and reputation, crucifixion was the ultimate humiliation. Yet Jesus counted this shame as nothing compared to the glory that would follow and the salvation He would accomplish. He didn’t minimize the pain, but He refused to let shame derail His mission.

The result? Jesus “is seated at the right hand of the throne of God”—the position of ultimate authority and honor. His suffering led to glory, His humiliation to exaltation.

For believers today, this means we can endure our own trials by keeping our eyes fixed on Jesus. When we’re tempted to quit, discouraged by setbacks, or overwhelmed by shame from our failures, we look to Him. We remember that our faith doesn’t depend on our strength but on Christ’s finished work. We find motivation not in our circumstances but in the joy set before us—eternal life with God and the privilege of bringing Him glory through our perseverance.

Confessional Tie-in

“This faith is a singular gift of God, and is not in the power of man to procure for himself; and is also not given by God to all men, but only to those whom he has from eternity chosen unto salvation.” — Belgic Confession, Article 24

Prayer Prompt

Father, help me keep my eyes fixed on Jesus today, especially when trials tempt me to look elsewhere for hope. Thank You that my faith rests not on my strength but on Christ’s finished work and His promise to complete what He began in me.

Go Deeper

When facing difficulties this week, practice redirecting your focus from your circumstances to Christ’s example and promises. Consider journaling about specific ways Jesus’ endurance of shame for joy encourages you in your current struggles. What “joy set before you” can motivate faithful perseverance?
