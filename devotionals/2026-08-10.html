[God Is Our Fortress] To the choirmaster. Of the Sons of Korah. According to Alamoth. A Song. God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble.
Psalm 46:1 (ESV)

When the Earth Gives Way

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Psalm 46 opens with a declaration, not a wish: God is our refuge and strength. Not “might be” or “could become”—is. This psalm was likely composed during a season of national upheaval, perhaps when Jerusalem faced siege or catastrophe. The sons of Korah, temple musicians, wrote not from theoretical safety but from lived uncertainty. Verses 2-3 go on to describe mountains falling into the sea and waters roaring—apocalyptic language depicting a world unraveling at its foundations. Yet even there, the psalmist insists: we will not fear.

This is remarkable. The security described here isn’t the absence of trouble but God’s presence in trouble. The Hebrew word for “refuge” carries the sense of a shelter to which one flees for protection, and “very present help” suggests immediacy—God is not far off, sending help from a distance, but near, actively engaged in our distress.

This truth flows from who God is. Because he is sovereign over creation, the same God who set the mountains in place can steady them when they shake. Because he is unchanging, his character remains our fixed point when everything else moves. This isn’t self-help optimism; it’s rooted in the covenant faithfulness of a God who has proven himself trustworthy across generations, and ultimately in Christ, who entered our chaos, bore our sin, and secured our eternal refuge through his death and resurrection.

For the believer today, this means we can name our fears honestly—financial strain, illness, relational fracture, the world’s disorder—without pretending they don’t exist. But we bring them to a God who has not abandoned his post. Our stability was never meant to come from circumstances remaining unchanged, but from the unchanging one who holds us through the change. Where is your ground shaking today? Bring it to the One who cannot be shaken.

Confessional Tie-in:

“What is your only comfort in life and death? That I am not my own, but belong—body and soul, in life and in death—to my faithful Savior, Jesus Christ, who at the cost of his own blood has fully paid for all my sins… He also watches over me in such a way that all things must work together for my salvation.” — Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 1

Prayer Prompt:

Lord, you alone are my refuge when the ground beneath me shakes. Forgive me for seeking security in things that cannot hold—my plans, my strength, my control. Thank you for being present, not distant, in my trouble; help me run to you first, not last.

Go Deeper:

Today, when anxiety rises, pause and speak this verse aloud as a declaration rather than a plea. Consider journaling one specific “shaking” in your life right now and writing beside it what it means that God is a “very present help”—not eventually, but now. For further study, read Psalm 46 in full and note how the refrain “the LORD of hosts is with us” (vv. 7, 11) frames the entire psalm.
