O you who love the LORD, hate evil! He preserves the lives of his saints; he delivers them from the hand of the wicked.
Psalm 97:10 (ESV)

The Call to Holy Opposition

Psalm 97:10 presents believers with a profound truth: “O you who love the LORD, hate evil! He preserves the lives of his saints; he delivers them from the hand of the wicked.”

What does this text teach us? The psalmist establishes a direct connection between loving God and hating evil—these aren’t separate Christian duties but two sides of the same spiritual reality. True love for God naturally produces a corresponding hatred of everything that opposes His holy character. This isn’t mere human emotion but a transformed heart response that flows from knowing God’s perfect nature.

The Hebrew word for “hate” here carries the sense of rejecting, turning away from, and actively opposing. This hatred of evil isn’t optional for believers; it’s the inevitable fruit of genuine love for God. We cannot compartmentalize our affections—claiming to love the light while remaining comfortable with darkness. The more we grow in our knowledge and love of God’s holiness, the more we’ll find ourselves grieved by sin’s presence in our lives and world.

Yet this verse doesn’t leave us to fight evil in our own strength. God Himself “preserves the lives of his saints” and “delivers them from the hand of the wicked.” The word “saints” refers not to super-spiritual individuals but to all who belong to God—those set apart by His grace. Our hatred of evil is matched by God’s protective love for His people.

This divine preservation doesn’t guarantee a trouble-free life, but it assures us that God sovereignly guards what matters most—our eternal souls and our relationship with Him. Even when evil seems to triumph temporarily, God’s people remain secure in His hands. The same God who calls us to oppose evil also equips and protects us in that opposition.

This creates a beautiful balance in Christian living. We’re called to be uncompromising toward sin while resting confidently in God’s care. We don’t hate evil from a position of fear or weakness, but from the security of knowing we belong to the One who ultimately conquers all wickedness.

Confessional Tie-in: “God did from all eternity decree to justify all the elect, and Christ did in the fullness of time die for their sins and rise again for their justification; nevertheless they are not justified until the Holy Spirit does in due time actually apply Christ to them.” — Westminster Confession of Faith 11.4

Prayer Prompt: Father, grow my love for You so deeply that I naturally turn from everything that grieves Your heart. Thank You that my security rests not in my own strength to fight evil, but in Your faithful protection of those who belong to You.

Go Deeper: Examine your entertainment choices, relationships, and daily habits—what does your comfort level with certain things reveal about your love for God? Consider memorizing this verse as a daily reminder of both your calling to holiness and your security in God’s preserving grace.
