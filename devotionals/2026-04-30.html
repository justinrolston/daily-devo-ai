Rather, speaking the truth in love, we are to grow up in every way into him who is the head, into Christ...
Ephesians 4:15 (ESV)

Growing Up in Truth and Love

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Paul commands believers to speak “the truth in love” as the pathway to spiritual maturity. This isn’t merely about being honest in our conversations, though that’s certainly included. The Greek word for “truth” here encompasses the entire reality of God’s character and His gospel. We are called to embody and communicate God’s truth—His Word, His ways, His very nature—but always through the lens of love.

Notice Paul’s connection between truthful communication and spiritual growth. We “grow up in every way into him who is the head, into Christ.” This reveals that maturity in Christ isn’t a private, individual pursuit. It happens in community, through relationships where truth and love intersect. When we speak God’s truth with genuine love for others, we participate in the body’s growth toward Christlikeness.

The phrase “grow up” reminds us that spiritual immaturity is a serious concern. Children are naturally self-centered, easily deceived, and unstable in their convictions. Paul has been warning against doctrinal immaturity that leaves believers “tossed to and fro by every wind of doctrine.” The antidote isn’t simply learning more facts about God, but learning to communicate His truth in love—both receiving it and giving it.

This growth has a specific direction: “into Christ.” He is not merely our example but our head—the source of life, direction, and authority for the entire body. Every word we speak in truth and love should draw ourselves and others closer to Him. When we criticize without love, we tear down rather than build up. When we show love without truth, we enable spiritual immaturity.

Consider how Christ Himself perfectly embodied this principle. He spoke hard truths about sin, judgment, and human nature, yet people felt His love so deeply that they left everything to follow Him. His truth-telling was always for restoration, never mere condemnation. His love never compromised truth but rather provided the safe context in which truth could be received and acted upon.

This has profound implications for our marriages, friendships, and church relationships. Are we known for speaking truth? Are we equally known for our love? The gospel requires both—truth about our sin and God’s love demonstrated in Christ’s sacrifice.

Confessional Tie-in: “The whole counsel of God, concerning all things necessary for his own glory, man’s salvation, faith, and life, is either expressly set down in Scripture, or by good and necessary consequence may be deduced from Scripture.” — Westminster Confession of Faith 1.6

Prayer Prompt: Lord Jesus, You are the head of Your body, the church. Give me wisdom to speak Your truth always in love, never compromising either. Help me grow in spiritual maturity through truthful, loving relationships that draw me and others closer to You.

Go Deeper: Examine your recent conversations—do they reflect both truth and love, or do you tend toward one without the other? Consider how Christ’s example of truth-telling in love challenges you to grow in both courage and compassion in your relationships.
