Let each of us please his neighbor for his good, to build him up.
Romans 15:2 (ESV)

Building Others Up Through Selfless Love

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Paul’s instruction in Romans 15:2 calls believers to a radical reorientation of our natural inclinations. Instead of seeking to please ourselves, we are commanded to “please our neighbor for his good, to build him up.” This isn’t mere people-pleasing or seeking approval, but purposeful action aimed at another’s spiritual and practical benefit.

The Greek word for “build up” (oikodomeo) carries the image of constructing a building, adding strength and stability. Paul envisions the Christian community as people actively constructing one another’s faith, character, and spiritual maturity. This building up isn’t optional—it’s a fundamental expression of love that flows from our union with Christ.

Notice the motivation Paul provides: “for his good.” This distinguishes biblical neighbor-love from worldly accommodation. We don’t simply give people what they want, but what will truly benefit them according to God’s design. Sometimes building up requires gentle correction; other times it means encouragement or practical help. Always, it demands we set aside our immediate preferences for another’s genuine welfare.

This command assumes something profound about our new nature in Christ. By grace, we’ve been freed from the tyranny of self-centeredness that marks fallen humanity. The Spirit enables us to genuinely care for others’ flourishing, not as burdensome duty but as joyful expression of God’s love working through us.

Paul places this instruction within the broader context of Christian unity. When believers consistently seek each other’s good, the church becomes a visible demonstration of the gospel’s power. Our self-sacrificial love points to Christ’s ultimate self-sacrifice for our eternal good.

Consider your daily interactions—with family, coworkers, fellow believers. Are you approaching these relationships asking “How can I build this person up?” or are you primarily focused on your own comfort and preferences? The person who has experienced God’s building-up grace cannot help but extend that same constructive love to others.

Confessional Tie-in: “We believe that this true faith, being wrought in man by the hearing of the Word of God and the operation of the Holy Spirit, regenerates him and makes him a new man, causing him to live a new life, and freeing him from the bondage of sin.” — Belgic Confession, Article 24

Prayer Prompt: Father, thank You for building me up through Christ’s perfect sacrifice. Transform my natural self-focus into genuine love for my neighbors. Help me see opportunities today to encourage, strengthen, and bless others for their true good and Your glory.

Go Deeper: Look for one specific opportunity today to build someone up—through encouragement, practical help, or gentle truth-telling. Reflect on how Christ has built you up through His people, and consider how God might use you similarly in others’ lives.
