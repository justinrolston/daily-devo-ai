Love one another with brotherly affection. Outdo one another in showing honor.
Romans 12:10 (ESV)

Competing in Love

Reflection

What does this text teach us? Paul’s instruction in Romans 12:10 reveals something remarkable about Christian community—it’s meant to be a place of loving competition. But this isn’t the self-seeking rivalry we know so well. Instead, Paul calls us to “outdo one another in showing honor,” creating a beautiful race where everyone wins.

The phrase “brotherly affection” translates the Greek word philadelphia—the tender love between family members. This isn’t merely polite civility or dutiful service, but the warm, genuine care we naturally show those closest to us. Paul envisions the church as a family where this affection flows freely among all members.

Yet Paul doesn’t stop with warm feelings. He calls us to compete—literally to “lead the way” or “take the initiative”—in showing honor. Picture believers constantly trying to outdo each other in acts of respect, encouragement, and recognition. Instead of jockeying for position or recognition, we’re called to be first in giving these gifts to others.

This transforms our natural competitiveness into something beautiful. Rather than seeking our own advancement, we compete to advance others. Instead of demanding honor, we race to bestow it. This reflects the very heart of the gospel—Christ, who deserved all honor, took the lowest place to lift us up.

Such love flows from our union with Christ. Because we’ve received immeasurable honor as God’s adopted children, we’re freed from the desperate need to seek honor from others. Instead, we can lavishly give what we’ve abundantly received. Our security in Christ enables us to celebrate others without threat to our own identity.

This passage challenges our natural tendency toward self-promotion and comparison. How often do we withhold encouragement because we fear it diminishes our own standing? How frequently do we fail to honor others because we’re too focused on receiving honor ourselves?

Paul’s vision isn’t merely about individual relationships—it’s about creating communities that reflect God’s character. When believers genuinely compete in showing honor, the watching world sees something that contradicts every earthly power structure. They witness the upside-down kingdom where greatness is measured by service and honor is gained by giving it away.

Confessional Tie-in

“All those that are united to Jesus Christ their Head, by his Spirit, and by faith, have fellowship with him in his graces, sufferings, death, resurrection, and glory: and, being united to one another as members of the same body, they have communion in each other’s gifts and graces.” — Westminster Confession of Faith 26.1

Prayer Prompt

Father, thank you for the honor you’ve given us as your children through Christ. Help us to overflow with the brotherly affection we’ve received from you, and give us grace to lead the way in honoring others rather than seeking honor for ourselves.

Go Deeper

Look for one specific way today to “outdo” someone in showing honor—perhaps through encouragement, recognition, or celebration of their gifts. Consider: What fears or insecurities hold you back from freely honoring others, and how does your identity in Christ address these concerns?
