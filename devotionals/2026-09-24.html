Therefore do not throw away your confidence, which has a great reward. For you have need of endurance, so that when you have done the will of God you may receive what is promised.
Hebrews 10:35-36 (ESV)

Do Not Throw It Away

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? The writer of Hebrews addresses believers who were growing weary—Christians tempted to abandon their profession of faith under the weight of persecution, disappointment, and delay. His command is striking: “do not throw away your confidence.” This confidence is not self-assurance or optimism, but bold trust in Christ’s finished work, the settled assurance that we belong to God through the blood of Jesus. The word “throw away” implies a deliberate act—these believers weren’t at risk of losing their faith accidentally, but of casting it aside under pressure, like sailors jettisoning cargo in a storm.

Notice the promise attached: this confidence “has a great reward.” God is not indifferent to our perseverance; He has bound up glory with faithfulness. Yet verse 36 tells us how that reward comes—not through passivity, but through endurance. This endurance is not merely gritting our teeth, but a Spirit-wrought perseverance that flows from union with Christ, who himself “endured the cross, despising the shame” (Hebrews 12:2).

Here is the tension every believer knows: we are already justified, secure in Christ, yet we are called to press on, to hold fast, to not throw away what has been graciously given. This is not contradiction but the shape of sanctification—God’s sovereign grace working through our wholehearted perseverance. We do not endure to earn salvation; we endure because we have been saved, and the Spirit who began this work will complete it (Philippians 1:6).

Perhaps you feel the weight of waiting today. The promise delayed. The prayer unanswered. The temptation to quietly walk away from costly obedience. Hebrews meets you here—not with empty encouragement, but with the reminder that your confidence rests not on your strength to endure, but on Christ’s finished work and faithful character. He who promised is faithful. Hold fast—not because you are strong, but because He is.

Confessional Tie-in:

“They, whom God hath accepted in his Beloved, effectually called, and sanctified by his Spirit… can never finally fall away from the state of grace, but shall certainly persevere therein to the end, and be eternally saved… This perseverance of the saints depends not upon their own free will, but upon the immutability of the decree of election.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter 17.1-2

Prayer Prompt:

Father, I confess how easily I grow weary and tempted to abandon hope when Your promises seem delayed. Thank You that my confidence rests not on my own strength but on Christ’s finished work and Your unchanging faithfulness. Strengthen me by Your Spirit today to endure, trusting that You who called me will also sustain me to the end.

Go Deeper:

Consider one area where you’ve been tempted to “throw away” confidence—perhaps in prayer, ministry, a relationship, or a long-standing struggle. Reread Hebrews 11 this week and notice how the saints of old endured “not having received the things promised, but having seen them from afar.” Ask: what would it look like to hold fast today, not in your own strength, but resting in Christ’s?
