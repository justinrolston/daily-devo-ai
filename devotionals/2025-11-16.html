November 16, 2025

Open my eyes, that I may behold wondrous things out of your law.
Psalm 119:18 (ESV)

Eyes Wide Open to God’s Wonder

Reflection

What does this text teach us? The psalmist’s prayer in Psalm 119:18 reveals a profound truth about spiritual sight and the nature of God’s Word. He asks God to “open my eyes” - acknowledging that by nature, we are spiritually blind to the treasures contained in Scripture. The Hebrew word for “wondrous things” suggests extraordinary, miraculous truths that would otherwise remain hidden from our understanding.

This prayer assumes that God’s law - His revealed Word - contains depths of beauty and truth that surpass our natural ability to perceive. The psalmist doesn’t simply ask for better reading comprehension or sharper intellect. He recognizes that spiritual illumination comes only from God Himself. Without divine intervention, we read Scripture with veiled hearts, missing the glory that radiates from every page.

Consider how this applies to our daily Bible reading. How often do we approach Scripture mechanically, reading words but missing the wonder? The psalmist understood that God’s Word isn’t merely information to be processed but revelation to be received. It contains “wondrous things” - glimpses of God’s character, promises of His faithfulness, and the unfolding story of redemption that culminates in Christ.

This prayer also reveals our ongoing dependence on God’s Spirit. Even as believers, we need fresh eyes each day to see what God wants to show us. Yesterday’s insight, while precious, cannot substitute for today’s illumination. The Spirit who inspired the Scriptures must also illuminate them to our hearts.

The word “behold” suggests more than casual observation - it implies wonder, contemplation, and awe. When God opens our eyes, we don’t merely read about His attributes; we behold His glory. We don’t simply learn theological facts; we encounter the living God who speaks through His Word.

This should transform our approach to Scripture. Instead of rushing through our daily reading like a task to complete, we can pause and pray this ancient prayer. We can ask God to surprise us with fresh revelation, to show us connections we’ve never seen, and to apply His truth to the specific circumstances we face today.

Confessional Tie-in

“The whole counsel of God, concerning all things necessary for his own glory, man’s salvation, faith, and life, is either expressly set down in Scripture, or by good and necessary consequence may be deduced from Scripture.” — Westminster Confession of Faith 1.6

Prayer Prompt

Father, like the psalmist, I acknowledge my need for spiritual sight. Open my eyes today to behold the wondrous truths You’ve placed in Your Word. Help me approach Scripture not as a duty but as a treasure hunt, expecting to discover fresh revelations of Your glory and grace.

Go Deeper

Before your next Bible reading, pause and pray Psalm 119:18. Ask God specifically what He wants to show you today. Consider keeping a journal of the “wondrous things” God reveals to you through His Word, noting how He applies ancient truths to your current circumstances.
