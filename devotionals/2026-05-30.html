In God, whose word I praise, in God I trust; I shall not be afraid. What can flesh do to me?
Psalm 56:4 (ESV)

Finding Security in God’s Unshakeable Word

Reflection:

What does Psalm 56:4 teach us about where we find true security? David writes these words from a place of genuine danger—fleeing from enemies who sought his life. Yet in the midst of real threats, he makes a profound declaration: his trust rests not in his own strength, political alliances, or military might, but in God and His word.

“In God, whose word I praise”—David begins by anchoring his confidence in the character and promises of God as revealed through His word. The Hebrew word for “praise” here suggests boasting or glorying in something. David finds his boast not in human wisdom or strength, but in the reliability of God’s revealed truth. God’s word is not merely information; it is the unshakeable foundation upon which faith stands.

This connects to the broader biblical theme that God’s people live by every word that proceeds from His mouth. Just as Jesus would later resist Satan’s temptations by wielding Scripture, David finds his refuge in the promises and character of God revealed in His word. When circumstances scream one message, God’s word speaks a more authoritative truth.

“In God I trust; I shall not be afraid”—genuine faith produces a settled confidence that transcends circumstances. This isn’t positive thinking or denial of real dangers. David acknowledges his enemies exist, but he places them in proper perspective. When we truly grasp God’s sovereignty and faithfulness, even legitimate concerns lose their power to overwhelm us.

“What can flesh do to me?”—this rhetorical question reveals the transformed perspective that flows from trusting God’s word. “Flesh” represents human power at its strongest—yet compared to God’s eternal purposes, it is temporary and limited. This doesn’t mean we’ll never face hardship, but that no earthly power can ultimately frustrate God’s good plans for His people.

For believers today, this psalm speaks to our tendency to find security in unstable things—health, finances, relationships, or political outcomes. While God calls us to faithful stewardship in these areas, our ultimate trust must rest in His unchanging character and promises. When anxiety threatens to overwhelm, we return to what God has revealed about Himself in Scripture.

Confessional Tie-in: “The whole counsel of God, concerning all things necessary for his own glory, man’s salvation, faith, and life, is either expressly set down in scripture, or by good and necessary consequence may be deduced from scripture: unto which nothing at any time is to be added…” — Westminster Confession of Faith 1.6

Prayer Prompt: Father, when my heart is anxious and circumstances feel overwhelming, help me remember that Your word stands forever. Teach me to find my security in Your promises rather than in the unstable things of this world, and grant me the peace that comes from trusting in Your sovereign care.

Go Deeper: Consider what current anxieties reveal about where you’re placing your ultimate trust. Spend time meditating on specific biblical promises that speak to your areas of concern, asking how God’s word might reshape your perspective on present challenges.
