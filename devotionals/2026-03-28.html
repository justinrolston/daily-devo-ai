For there is one God, and there is one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus, who gave himself as a ransom for all, which is the testimony given at the proper time.
1 Timothy 2:5-6 (ESV)

The One Mediator Who Changes Everything

Reflection

What does this text teach us? Paul presents us with two foundational truths that should anchor our understanding of salvation: there is one God, and there is one mediator between God and humanity—Jesus Christ. This isn’t merely theological information; it’s the heartbeat of the gospel.

The word “mediator” describes someone who stands between two parties to reconcile them. But why do we need a mediator at all? Because sin has created an infinite chasm between holy God and fallen humanity. We cannot bridge this gap through our efforts, religious rituals, or moral improvements. The gulf is too wide, and we are too spiritually dead to cross it.

Enter Jesus—fully God and fully man. His unique nature makes Him the perfect mediator. As God, He possesses the power and authority to represent the Father. As man, He can truly represent us. No angel could do this work, nor could any mere human being, no matter how righteous. Only the God-man could stand in this crucial position.

Notice how Paul describes Christ’s mediating work: “who gave himself as a ransom for all.” This wasn’t a reluctant sacrifice extracted from an unwilling victim. Jesus voluntarily offered Himself as the price to free us from sin’s bondage. The word “ransom” indicates both the costliness of our redemption and the completeness of our liberation. Christ didn’t merely show us the way to God; He became the way by paying what we could never pay.

This truth transforms how we approach God daily. We don’t come based on our performance, mood, or spiritual temperature. We come confidently through our Mediator, knowing that His perfect righteousness covers our imperfections, His finished work secures our acceptance, and His ongoing intercession sustains our relationship with the Father.

In a world filled with competing voices promising alternative paths to peace with God—whether through religious works, philosophical systems, or spiritual experiences—Paul’s words cut through the confusion. There is one God and one way to Him. This isn’t narrow-mindedness; it’s the gracious provision of a loving Father who sent His Son to accomplish what we could never achieve.

When you feel distant from God today, remember: you have a Mediator. When guilt whispers accusations, remember: your ransom has been paid. When you’re tempted to earn God’s favor through your efforts, remember: Christ’s work is complete and sufficient.

Confessional Tie-in

“Christ, by his obedience and death, did fully discharge the debt of all those that are thus justified, and did make a proper, real and full satisfaction to his Father’s justice in their behalf.” — Westminster Confession of Faith 11.3

Prayer Prompt

Father, thank You for providing Jesus as our one perfect Mediator. Help me rest fully in His completed work rather than my own efforts, and give me boldness to approach Your throne of grace knowing that Christ’s righteousness is my confidence.

Go Deeper

Throughout today, when you pray, consciously remember that you’re approaching God through Christ your Mediator—let this truth fill you with confidence rather than fear. Consider this question: How does knowing there is only one way to God actually demonstrate His incredible love rather than His limitation?
