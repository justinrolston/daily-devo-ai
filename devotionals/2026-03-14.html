March 14, 2026

For this very reason, make every effort to supplement your faith with virtue, and virtue with knowledge, and knowledge with self-control, and self-control with steadfastness, and steadfastness with godliness, and godliness with brotherly affection, and brotherly affection with love. For if these qualities are yours and are increasing, they keep you from being ineffective or unfruitful in the knowledge of our Lord Jesus Christ.
2 Peter 1:5-8 (ESV)

Building Blocks of Spiritual Growth

Reflection

What does this remarkable passage teach us about the Christian life? Peter presents us with a beautiful chain of spiritual qualities, each link building upon the previous one. This isn’t a checklist to complete but rather a portrait of what God produces in believers as they “make every effort” to grow in grace.

Notice that this progression begins with faith—not our faith in God, but the faith that God has already given us. This divine gift becomes the foundation upon which everything else is built. Peter then calls us to “supplement” this faith, using a word that originally described funding a choir or athletic team. Just as a generous patron would spare no expense to ensure excellence, we’re called to invest wholeheartedly in our spiritual development.

The sequence Peter outlines reveals how spiritual maturity unfolds. Virtue (moral excellence) flows from faith, leading to knowledge—not mere intellectual understanding, but the deep, personal knowledge of God that transforms how we live. This knowledge produces self-control, the ability to govern our desires and responses according to God’s will rather than our impulses.

Self-control develops into steadfastness—that rock-solid perseverance that holds fast through trials and temptations. Steadfastness then blossoms into godliness, a life that reflects God’s own character. From godliness springs brotherly affection, genuine care for fellow believers. Finally, this love for the family of God expands into love itself—that Christ-like love that extends even to enemies.

Here’s the encouraging truth: Peter isn’t describing a ladder we must climb through human effort. Rather, he’s showing us what God’s grace looks like as it works itself out in our daily lives. The phrase “make every effort” doesn’t suggest we earn these qualities, but that we actively cooperate with the Holy Spirit who is already at work within us.

The promise is wonderful: when these qualities are “increasing” in our lives, we won’t be “ineffective or unfruitful.” God doesn’t call us to spiritual productivity and then leave us to figure it out alone. He provides both the pattern and the power for growth that honors Christ and blesses others.

Confessional Tie-in

“This sanctification is throughout the whole man, yet imperfect in this life: there abide still some remnants of corruption in every part, whence arises a continual and irreconcilable war between the flesh and the spirit.”

— Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter XIII, Article 2

Prayer Prompt

Father, thank You for the faith You have given me as the foundation for spiritual growth. Help me to actively cooperate with Your Spirit’s work, investing wholeheartedly in developing these Christ-like qualities. Make me increasingly fruitful for Your kingdom’s sake.

Go Deeper

Examine your life for evidence of this spiritual progression—not to condemn yourself, but to see where God is already at work and where you might more intentionally cooperate with His grace. Consider which quality in this chain most needs attention in your current season, and ask God to strengthen that area through His Word and Spirit.
