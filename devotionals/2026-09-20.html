“Be glad, O children of Zion, and rejoice in the LORD your God, for he has given the early rain for your vindication; he has poured down for you abundant rain, the early and the latter rain, as before.
Joel 2:23 (ESV)

Rain After the Locusts

Title: Rain After the Locusts

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Joel writes to a people who had watched locusts strip their fields bare, a nation staring at famine and ruin as a consequence of covenant unfaithfulness. Yet here, in the middle of judgment, God speaks tenderness. He commands his people to rejoice—not because their circumstances have changed yet, but because he promises to send rain. The early rain would soften the soil for planting; the latter rain would mature the harvest. Both were necessary, and both were gifts of God’s hand, “as before”—a restoration to the fruitfulness they once knew.

Notice what grounds their joy: not the rain itself, but the Lord who gives it “for your vindication.” The Hebrew idea here carries the sense of righteousness restored, of God himself acting to set things right. This is a picture of grace meeting genuine repentance (see Joel 2:12-13). God does not simply repair what sin destroyed; he vindicates his people, showing that their hope was not misplaced.

This pattern echoes throughout Scripture. God is the one who waters what is dead and brings life from barren ground—ultimately fulfilled in Christ, who pours out his Spirit as latter-day rain upon his church (Acts 2), and who alone vindicates sinners not through their own effort but through his finished work on the cross. The locust years of our own lives—seasons of loss, discipline, or the slow erosion of joy—are never the final word for those united to Christ. He restores what sin and sorrow consume.

Are you in a season that feels stripped bare? This text calls you to remember that God’s rain is not random; it is purposeful, covenantal, and timed for your good. Your vindication rests not in circumstances reversing, but in Christ’s righteousness credited to you.

Confessional Tie-in:

“What is faith in Jesus Christ? Faith in Jesus Christ is a saving grace, whereby we receive and rest upon him alone for salvation, as he is offered to us in the gospel.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Q. 86

Prayer Prompt:

Lord, thank you for being the God who sends rain in due season, even after the locusts have done their worst. Forgive me for doubting your faithfulness in dry seasons, and give me patience to wait for your restoring hand. Pour out your Spirit afresh on me today, that I may rejoice in you regardless of my circumstances.

Go Deeper:

Read Acts 2:17-21 and consider how Joel’s promise of the Spirit finds its fullest meaning in Pentecost. This week, identify one “locust season” in your life—past or present—and journal how God has been, or might be, bringing restoration through Christ rather than through changed circumstances alone.
