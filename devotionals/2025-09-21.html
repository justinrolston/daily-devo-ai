September 21, 2025

The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ and the love of God and the fellowship of the Holy Spirit be with you all.
2 Corinthians 13:14 (ESV)

Three-in-One Blessing

Reflection

What does this text teach us? Paul concludes his second letter to the Corinthians with what may be the clearest Trinitarian blessing in all of Scripture. Here we see the apostle invoking all three persons of the Godhead—not as separate deities, but as the one true God working in perfect unity for our good. This isn’t merely a polite farewell; it’s a profound theological statement about how God relates to His people.

Notice the beautiful order: grace from Christ, love from the Father, and fellowship through the Spirit. This sequence reflects the economy of salvation itself. We begin with Christ’s grace—His unmerited favor that bridges the chasm between holy God and sinful humanity. Through His perfect life, substitutionary death, and glorious resurrection, Jesus secures for us what we could never earn. This grace isn’t simply God’s attitude toward us; it’s His active work on our behalf.

The love of God the Father serves as the fountain from which all blessing flows. This isn’t sentimental affection, but the eternal, unchanging love that moved Him to send His Son. Before we ever existed, before we could do good or evil, God set His love upon His people. This divine love doesn’t fluctuate with our performance—it remains constant because it’s rooted in God’s character, not our worthiness.

The fellowship of the Holy Spirit makes this blessing personal and present. The Spirit doesn’t merely inform us about God’s love and Christ’s grace; He brings us into intimate communion with the triune God. Through the Spirit, we experience what theologians call “union with Christ”—we’re not just forgiven observers, but beloved children brought into God’s very family.

Paul’s benediction reminds us that Christian life isn’t about mustering up enough faith or moral effort. It’s about receiving and resting in what the triune God has already accomplished. When we feel spiritually dry, we can anchor our hearts in Christ’s grace. When we doubt God’s care, we can rest in the Father’s unchanging love. When we feel distant from God, we can rely on the Spirit’s faithful presence.

This three-fold blessing shapes how we approach each day. We don’t wake up wondering if God loves us—His love is established. We don’t strive to earn His favor—Christ’s grace is sufficient. We don’t journey alone—the Spirit ensures our fellowship with God remains unbroken.

Confessional Tie-in

“There are three persons in the Godhead: the Father, the Son, and the Holy Ghost; and these three are one God, the same in substance, equal in power and glory.”
— Westminster Shorter Catechism, Question 6

Prayer Prompt

Triune God, I praise You for Your perfect unity and distinct persons working for my salvation. Thank You, Jesus, for Your grace that covers my sin; Father, for Your unchanging love that chose me before time began; Holy Spirit, for Your fellowship that keeps me close to God’s heart each day.

Go Deeper

Consider how this Trinitarian blessing addresses your specific needs today—where do you need Christ’s grace, the Father’s love, or the Spirit’s fellowship most? Memorize this verse as a daily reminder that all of God works for your spiritual good, and let it shape how you bless others in conversation and prayer.
