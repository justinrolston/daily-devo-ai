‘Ah, Lord GOD! It is you who have made the heavens and the earth by your great power and by your outstretched arm! Nothing is too hard for you.
Jeremiah 32:17 (ESV)

Nothing Is Too Hard for You

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Jeremiah cries out these words in the middle of a national catastrophe. Babylon’s armies are besieging Jerusalem, the city is about to fall, and God has just commanded Jeremiah to do something that seems absurd: buy a field. Why purchase land in a country about to be conquered? Yet Jeremiah’s prayer reveals the foundation beneath this strange obedience. Before he asks anything of God, he first remembers who God is—the Creator who made heaven and earth by sheer power, whose outstretched arm accomplishes whatever he wills. From this bedrock truth flows the confession: nothing is too hard for him.

Notice the order here. Jeremiah doesn’t begin with his circumstances; he begins with God’s character. This is the pattern of biblical faith. Our prayers, our hopes, and our obedience are only as strong as our vision of God. If God is small in our minds, our faith will shrink to match. But if we see him as he truly is—sovereign over creation, history, and every human heart—then even the darkest circumstances become occasions for trust rather than despair.

This truth confronts us practically. Perhaps you’re facing a diagnosis that feels like a siege, a relationship that seems as broken as Jerusalem’s walls, or a besetting sin you’ve fought for years without visible progress. The same God who spoke galaxies into existence and stretched out his arm to part seas and raise his Son from death is not diminished by your situation. He is not wringing his hands over your problems.

This doesn’t mean God’s power always looks like immediate rescue. Jerusalem did fall. Jeremiah watched it happen. Yet the field he purchased became a prophetic sign of future restoration—proof that God’s power operates according to his purposes and timing, not always our preferences. Our task is not to dictate how God’s power should show itself, but to trust that it will accomplish his good and perfect will, even through suffering, even through waiting.

Confessional Tie-in:

“God’s works of providence are, his most holy, wise, and powerful preserving and governing all his creatures, and all their actions.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Q. 11

Prayer Prompt:

Lord, you alone stretched out the heavens and hold all things by your power—forgive me for the times I have treated my circumstances as bigger than you. Thank you that nothing in my life today falls outside your sovereign care. Give me Jeremiah’s faith to obey you even when your ways seem strange to me.

Go Deeper:

Take one situation you’ve labeled “impossible” and speak Jeremiah’s words over it: “Nothing is too hard for you.” Consider reading Genesis 1 or Job 38-41 this week to refresh your vision of God’s creative power, then ask: does my prayer life reflect genuine confidence in this God?
