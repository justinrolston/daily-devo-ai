For if while we were enemies we were reconciled to God by the death of his Son, much more, now that we are reconciled, shall we be saved by his life.
Romans 5:10 (ESV)

From Enemy to Beloved Child

Reflection:

What does this remarkable text teach us? Paul presents one of the most stunning truths in all of Scripture: while we were actively hostile toward God—not merely indifferent, but actual enemies—He chose to reconcile us to Himself through Christ’s death. The Greek word for “enemies” here carries the sense of those who are opposed and antagonistic. This was our natural state before God’s grace intervened.

The logic Paul employs is breathtaking. If God accomplished the greater work of reconciliation when we were His enemies, how much more certain is our ongoing salvation now that we are His reconciled children? The phrase “much more” appears throughout Romans 5, emphasizing the abundant certainty of God’s saving work. Our reconciliation was achieved through Christ’s death—the payment for sin that removed the barrier between us and God. But our continued salvation flows from Christ’s resurrection life, His ongoing intercession, and His eternal priesthood.

This passage destroys any notion that we contributed to our salvation. We were enemies, not seekers. We were hostile, not hungry for God. The initiative, the work, and the completion all belong to Him. Yet how easy it becomes to slip back into thinking we must maintain our salvation through our efforts, as if God’s love fluctuates with our performance.

The security this text provides should transform our daily experience. If God loved us enough to save us when we were His enemies, will He not continue to love us now that we are His children? When we stumble, when we struggle with sin, when we feel distant from God, this truth anchors us: our relationship with Him does not depend on our consistency but on Christ’s finished work.

This reconciliation also calls us to live differently. Those who have been transformed from enemies to children should reflect this new identity. We who were once hostile toward God should now extend His reconciling love to others, even those who oppose us. The love that reached us in our rebellion now flows through us to a watching world.

Confessional Tie-in: “All those that are justified God vouchsafeth, in and for his only Son Jesus Christ, to make partakers of the grace of adoption: by which they are taken into the number, and enjoy the liberties and privileges of the children of God.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter 12.1

Prayer Prompt: Father, thank You for pursuing me when I was Your enemy and making me Your beloved child through Christ’s death. Help me rest securely in Your unchanging love and extend Your reconciling grace to others who need to know You.

Go Deeper: Consider someone in your life who has wronged you. How might God’s reconciling love toward you as His former enemy shape how you respond to them? Meditate on other “much more” passages in Romans 5 to further grasp the abundance of God’s grace in your life.
