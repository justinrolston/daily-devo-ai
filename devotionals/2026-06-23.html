...but they who wait for the LORD shall renew their strength; they shall mount up with wings like eagles; they shall run and not be weary; they shall walk and not faint.
Isaiah 40:31 (ESV)

Strength for the Waiting

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Isaiah wrote these words to a weary people—Israelites facing exile, feeling forgotten by God, wondering if their strength would ever return. Just before this verse, Isaiah reminds us that God “does not faint or grow weary” and that “his understanding is unsearchable” (Isaiah 40:28). The promise of renewed strength isn’t a self-help formula; it flows directly from the character of an infinite, tireless God who freely gives His strength to the weak.

Notice the word “wait.” This isn’t passive waiting, but an active, trusting expectation—the same word used elsewhere for hoping in the Lord. It’s the posture of a soul that has stopped relying on its own resources and has turned wholly toward God’s sufficiency. This is the gospel pattern woven throughout Scripture: we are not strengthened because we are strong, but because we are desperately weak and cling to the One who is not.

This truth confronts our self-reliance. We live in a culture that prizes hustle, self-improvement, and grinding through exhaustion. But Isaiah points us elsewhere—not to more effort, but to renewed dependence. The Christian life is not a marathon we run in our own power; it’s a race sustained by grace, moment by moment, as we fix our eyes on Christ, the author and perfecter of our faith (Hebrews 12:2).

Notice too the progression: mounting up like eagles, running without weariness, walking without fainting. Sometimes God gives us soaring, mountaintop seasons. More often, He calls us to the ordinary faithfulness of walking—the daily, unglamorous perseverance of trusting Him in routine obedience. Both are gifts of grace, and both testify that our strength is borrowed, not earned.

If you’re weary today—in ministry, parenting, work, or simply enduring—this promise is not a rebuke for lacking faith, but an invitation to rest in the One who sustains you. Waiting on the Lord is not weakness; it’s the wisest posture of a creature before its Creator.

Confessional Tie-in:

“Man’s chief end is to glorify God, and to enjoy him forever.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Q.1

Prayer Prompt:

Lord, I confess how quickly I lean on my own strength and grow weary trying to carry burdens you never intended for me to bear alone. Thank you that you never grow tired and that your grace is sufficient for every season of my life. Renew my strength today, Father, and teach me to wait on you with patient, expectant faith.

Go Deeper:

Where in your life are you relying on personal strength rather than waiting on the Lord? Consider setting aside five minutes today simply to sit in silence before God, releasing your anxieties and inviting Him to renew your strength. For further reflection, read Isaiah 40 in its entirety and notice how God’s greatness undergirds this promise of rest.
