Colossians 3:15 (ESV)

Let Christ’s Peace Be the Final Judge

Colossians 3:15 (ESV) “And let the peace of Christ rule in your hearts, to which indeed you were called in one body. And be thankful.”

Reflection

What does this text teach us about the Christian life? Paul uses a fascinating word here for “rule” - brabeuo, which refers to an umpire making the final decision in an athletic contest. He’s telling us to let Christ’s peace serve as the ultimate arbiter in our hearts, the final judge that settles disputes and makes decisions.

But what is this “peace of Christ”? This isn’t merely the absence of conflict or a feeling of calm. The peace of Christ is the profound reconciliation we have with God through Jesus’ atoning work. It’s the settled assurance that our sins are forgiven, that we stand righteous before God, and that nothing can separate us from His love. This peace flows from our justification - we who were once enemies of God have been declared righteous and brought into His family.

Notice that this peace doesn’t operate in isolation. We were called to this peace “in one body” - the church. Our individual experience of Christ’s peace is inseparably connected to our unity with other believers. When we’re tempted toward bitterness, division, or selfish ambition, Christ’s peace should step in like an umpire and make the call: “This doesn’t align with who you are in Me.”

The practical implications are profound. When facing difficult decisions, we can ask: “Which choice aligns with the peace I have in Christ?” When conflict arises in relationships, Christ’s peace reminds us that we’re part of one body, called to unity rather than division. When anxiety threatens to overwhelm, His peace speaks the truth of our secure standing before God.

Paul concludes with a simple command: “be thankful.” Gratitude isn’t an afterthought but the natural response to recognizing Christ’s peace ruling in our hearts. Thankfulness acknowledges that this peace isn’t something we’ve earned or manufactured, but a gift of grace flowing from Christ’s finished work.

This is the Christian’s daily reality - not perfect circumstances, but Christ’s perfect peace serving as the governing principle of our hearts, shaping our decisions, relationships, and perspective on life’s challenges.

Confessional Tie-in

“The peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus… This peace of conscience being founded upon the free remission of sin, and their acceptance with God through Christ.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter XVIII, Article 1

Prayer Prompt

Lord Jesus, thank You for the peace You’ve secured for me through Your sacrifice. Help Your peace rule as judge in my heart today, guiding my decisions and responses. When conflict or anxiety arise, remind me of my secure standing in You and my calling to unity with fellow believers.

Go Deeper

Throughout your day, pause and ask: “Is Christ’s peace ruling in this situation, or am I allowing other voices to make the call?” Consider specific relationships or decisions where you need to invite His peace to serve as the final arbiter. Reflect on how your gratitude might grow as you recognize this peace as God’s gracious gift rather than your achievement.
