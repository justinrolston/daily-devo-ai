To make an apt answer is a joy to a man, and a word in season, how good it is!
Proverbs 15:23 (ESV)

The Gift of Timely Words

Reflection

What does this text teach us? The wise man observes something profound about human communication: there is deep satisfaction in giving an appropriate response, and immeasurable good flows from words spoken at just the right moment. The Hebrew word translated “apt” carries the idea of something perfectly fitted to its purpose—like a key made precisely for its lock.

This verse reveals God’s design for human speech as a tool for blessing rather than harm. In a world where words are often carelessly thrown about, causing division and pain, Solomon highlights the beauty of thoughtful, well-timed communication. The “joy” mentioned here isn’t mere personal satisfaction, but the deep contentment that comes from fulfilling God’s purposes through our speech.

Scripture consistently teaches that our words matter enormously to God. James reminds us that the tongue, though small, can set the course of our entire life (James 3:5). Jesus himself warned that we will give account for every careless word (Matthew 12:36). Yet here in Proverbs, we see the positive potential: our words can be instruments of grace, healing, and encouragement.

The phrase “a word in season” points to divine timing and wisdom. Like a farmer who knows when to plant and when to harvest, the wise person learns when to speak and when to remain silent. This requires more than human insight—it demands dependence on God’s Spirit to guide our conversations.

For believers, this takes on even deeper meaning. We who have been transformed by the gospel should reflect Christ in our speech. Our words should be seasoned with salt (Colossians 4:6), building up rather than tearing down. When we speak truth in love at precisely the right moment, we participate in God’s work of grace in others’ lives.

Consider how Christ himself perfectly embodied this principle. His words to the woman at the well, to Nicodemus, to the thief on the cross—each perfectly timed, perfectly suited to the hearer’s need. As his followers, we’re called to this same careful, loving use of speech.

The challenge for us is learning to pause before we speak, asking God for wisdom about whether our words will truly help. In our age of instant communication, this ancient wisdom calls us to something countercultural: thoughtful, purposeful speech that serves others rather than merely expressing ourselves.

Confessional Tie-in

“Good works are only such as God hath commanded in his holy Word, and not such as, without the warrant thereof, are devised by men, out of blind zeal, or upon any pretence of good intention.” — Westminster Confession of Faith XVI.1

Prayer Prompt

Lord, help me guard my tongue and speak only words that honor you and bless others. Give me wisdom to know when to speak and when to listen, and may my words be instruments of your grace in a hurting world.

Go Deeper

Practice the pause today—before responding in conversations, ask yourself whether your words will truly help the hearer. Consider memorizing verses about godly speech (Ephesians 4:29, Proverbs 16:24) to renew your mind about the power and purpose of your words.
