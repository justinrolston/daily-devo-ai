For Christ also suffered once for sins, the righteous for the unrighteous, that he might bring us to God, being put to death in the flesh but made alive in the spirit...
1 Peter 3:18 (ESV)

The Great Exchange: Christ’s Suffering for Our Salvation

Reflection

What does this text teach us? Peter presents one of Scripture’s most profound declarations about the heart of the gospel: Christ’s substitutionary suffering. The phrase “Christ also suffered once for sins” immediately draws our attention to the finality and completeness of Jesus’ work. This was not a repeated sacrifice or an ongoing process, but a singular, decisive act that accomplished everything necessary for our salvation.

The contrast Peter highlights is staggering: “the righteous for the unrighteous.” Here we see the great exchange that defines Christianity. Christ, who knew no sin, took upon himself the full weight of our rebellion against God. This wasn’t merely an example of noble sacrifice—it was a legal substitution where our guilt was transferred to him and his righteousness was credited to us. The sinless one bore the penalty that the sinful deserved.

But Peter doesn’t stop at the cross. The phrase “put to death in the flesh but made alive in the spirit” points us to the resurrection as the vindication of Christ’s atoning work. His physical death was real and necessary, but death could not hold him. His resurrection by the Spirit’s power demonstrates that the Father accepted his sacrifice and that death itself has been conquered.

The purpose of this great exchange is beautifully simple yet infinitely profound: “that he might bring us to God.” Before Christ’s work, we were separated from God by our sin, unable to approach him or find acceptance. But through Christ’s suffering, the barrier has been removed. We who were far off have been brought near. We who were enemies have been reconciled. We who were dead in trespasses have been made alive.

This truth should transform how we view our daily struggles and joys. When guilt weighs heavy on our hearts, we remember that Christ suffered for our sins—not some of them, but all of them. When we feel distant from God, we recall that Christ has opened the way to the Father. When we face our own mortality, we find hope in the one who conquered death itself.

The Christian life flows from this foundational reality. We don’t suffer to earn God’s favor—Christ has already secured it. We don’t work to bridge the gap between us and God—Christ has already brought us near. Instead, we live in grateful response to what has already been accomplished on our behalf.

Confessional Tie-in

“The Lord Jesus, by His perfect obedience, and sacrifice of Himself, which He through the eternal Spirit, once offered up unto God, has fully satisfied the justice of His Father; and purchased, not only reconciliation, but an everlasting inheritance in the kingdom of heaven, for those whom the Father has given unto Him.”
— Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter 8.5

Prayer Prompt

Father, I worship you for the perfect plan of salvation accomplished through Christ’s once-for-all sacrifice. Forgive me for the times I try to add to his finished work or doubt its sufficiency. Thank you that through Christ’s suffering, I have been brought near to you and made your child.

Go Deeper

Consider throughout your day how the truth “the righteous for the unrighteous” should impact your interactions with others who have wronged you. Meditate on what it means that you have been “brought to God”—what access and intimacy does this provide? Spend time in worship, reflecting on the costliness of your salvation and the completeness of Christ’s work.
