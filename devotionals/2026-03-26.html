For it has been granted to you that for the sake of Christ you should not only believe in him but also suffer for his sake...
Philippians 1:29 (ESV)

A Grant of Grace: Believing and Suffering

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Paul presents suffering for Christ not as an unfortunate byproduct of faith, but as a divine gift—something that “has been granted” to believers. The Greek word here, echaristhe, comes from the same root as grace (charis). Both believing in Christ and suffering for His sake are expressions of God’s grace toward us.

This challenges our natural thinking. We readily accept that faith is a gift from God, but suffering? Paul places both on equal footing. Just as we cannot generate saving faith through human effort, we cannot avoid the sufferings that accompany genuine discipleship. Both are appointed by God’s sovereign grace for our spiritual good and His glory.

The phrase “for the sake of Christ” anchors both our believing and our suffering. We don’t believe in a generic deity or suffer for abstract principles. Our faith is directed toward Christ—His person, His work, His gospel. Likewise, our sufferings are specifically “for His sake”—because we bear His name, follow His ways, and proclaim His truth in a world that rejected Him first.

This doesn’t mean all suffering is spiritual suffering, or that we should seek hardship. Rather, Paul acknowledges that authentic Christian living will inevitably bring opposition. When we live according to kingdom values in a fallen world, friction is unavoidable. The early Philippian believers faced persecution, imprisonment, and social ostracism for their faith.

Yet notice the comfort embedded in this challenging truth: our sufferings aren’t random or meaningless. They’re “granted”—purposefully given by a loving Father who wastes nothing in the lives of His children. Just as He uses our faith to draw us closer to Himself, He uses our sufferings to conform us more fully to the image of His Son.

This perspective transforms how we face trials. Instead of asking, “Why is this happening to me?” we can ask, “How is God using this to deepen my faith and display His glory?” Our sufferings become not obstacles to spiritual growth, but instruments of grace in the hands of our sovereign God.

Confessional Tie-in:

“God’s providence is His completely holy, wise, and powerful preserving and governing of all His creatures and all their actions.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Question 11

Prayer Prompt:

Father, help me embrace both faith and suffering as gifts from Your gracious hand. When trials come for Christ’s sake, strengthen me to see them as opportunities to know Him more deeply and display His sufficiency to a watching world.

Go Deeper:

Consider current difficulties in your life: how might God be using them to strengthen your faith and witness? Reflect on how Christ’s own sufferings give meaning and hope to ours, knowing that our light momentary afflictions are achieving for us an eternal weight of glory.
