For in him the whole fullness of deity dwells bodily, and you have been filled in him, who is the head of all rule and authority.
Colossians 2:9-10 (ESV)

Complete in Christ

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Paul was writing to believers in Colossae who were being tempted by teachers claiming that Christ alone wasn’t enough—that true spirituality required additional rituals, secret knowledge, or the appeasement of lesser spiritual powers. Into that confusion, Paul makes a staggering claim: the entire fullness of God dwells in Jesus Christ, in bodily form. Not a fragment of divinity. Not one emanation among many. The whole of who God is took on flesh and walked among us.

And here is the good news that follows: because Christ is fully God, and because you are united to him by faith, you have been filled in him. Not partially. Not provisionally, pending further achievement. You lack nothing essential for your standing before God, because you are joined to the One who lacks nothing.

This truth confronts two common temptations. The first is the temptation to add to Christ—to think that faith in him is merely the starting point, and that real spiritual maturity comes through some additional practice, experience, or knowledge others claim to possess. Paul says no: he is the head of all rule and authority. Every power, every principle, every spiritual force is subject to him. There is nothing beyond him to reach for.

The second temptation is despair—the sense that we are perpetually incomplete, forever falling short, never quite enough. But your completeness was never about your performance. It rests in him, in his fullness, not your own. This is the heart of grace: God does not fill you based on what you bring, but based on who Christ is and what he has accomplished.

Live today from fullness, not toward it. When anxiety whispers that you need something more—more certainty, more spiritual experience, more moral achievement—remember that you are already filled in the One who holds all authority. Rest is not laziness here; it is faith taking God at his word.

Confessional Tie-in:

“Q. What is true faith? A. True faith is not only a knowledge and conviction that everything God reveals in his Word is true; it is also a deep-rooted assurance, created in me by the Holy Spirit through the gospel, that, out of sheer grace earned for us by Christ, not only others, but I too, have had my sins forgiven, have been made forever right with God, and have been granted salvation.” — Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 21

Prayer Prompt:

Father, thank you for revealing yourself fully in your Son, in whom I lack nothing. Forgive me for the times I’ve sought completeness in things other than Christ, or lived as though I must earn what you have already given. Fill my heart today with quiet confidence in his sufficiency alone.

Go Deeper:

Take a moment to name one area where you’ve been striving to feel “enough”—spiritually, morally, or relationally. Bring that specific struggle to God, asking him to help you rest in your completeness in Christ rather than your own effort. For further study, read Colossians 1:15-20 to see how this fullness connects to Christ’s supremacy over all creation.
