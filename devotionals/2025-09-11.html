[My Help Comes from the LORD] A Song of Ascents. I lift up my eyes to the hills. From where does my help come? My help comes from the LORD, who made heaven and earth.
Psalm 121:1-2 (ESV)

Looking Up: Where True Help Is Found

Reflection

What does this psalm teach us? The psalmist begins with a declaration of faith: “I lift up my eyes to the hills.” In ancient Israel, hills often represented places of worship, refuge, or strategic advantage. Yet the psalmist immediately poses a searching question: “From where does my help come?” This isn’t doubt—it’s deliberate reflection that leads to confident proclamation.

The answer comes swiftly and decisively: “My help comes from the LORD, who made heaven and earth.” Notice the progression here. The psalmist doesn’t find ultimate help in the hills themselves, but in the One who made them. This is crucial—we’re prone to look for security in created things rather than the Creator. Whether it’s our bank accounts, relationships, health, or human institutions, we naturally turn to finite resources for infinite needs.

The psalmist’s confidence rests not merely in God’s willingness to help, but in His infinite capacity to do so. He is “the LORD, who made heaven and earth”—the sovereign Creator who spoke all things into existence and sustains them by His powerful word. This isn’t a distant deity, but Yahweh, the covenant-keeping God who has bound Himself to His people in faithful love.

As pilgrims traveling to Jerusalem for worship, the Israelites would sing this “Song of Ascents” together. They understood that their journey—both physical and spiritual—required divine assistance. The same truth applies to our Christian pilgrimage today. We face circumstances beyond our control, challenges that exceed our wisdom, and spiritual battles that demand supernatural strength.

The beauty of this psalm lies in its simplicity and certainty. When anxiety overwhelms us, when problems seem insurmountable, when we’re tempted to trust in lesser things, we can lift our eyes beyond the immediate and fix them on our faithful God. He who created the vastness of the universe is intimately concerned with the details of our lives.

This isn’t passive resignation but active faith. We lift our eyes deliberately, choosing to focus on God’s character and promises rather than our circumstances. Our help comes from the LORD—not partially, not occasionally, but completely and continually.

Confessional Tie-in

“What is your only comfort in life and in death? That I am not my own, but belong—body and soul, in life and in death—to my faithful Savior, Jesus Christ.” — Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 1

Prayer Prompt

Father, when I’m overwhelmed by life’s challenges, help me lift my eyes to You rather than searching for security in temporary things. Thank You that my help comes from You, the Creator of heaven and earth, who is both powerful enough to help and faithful enough to never abandon me.

Go Deeper

Throughout this week, when facing difficulties, practice the psalmist’s pattern: acknowledge the challenge, ask where your help comes from, then declare God’s faithfulness. Consider keeping a journal of ways you’ve seen God’s help in both small daily needs and larger life circumstances.
