[Christ the Wisdom and Power of God] For the word of the cross is folly to those who are perishing, but to us who are being saved it is the power of God.
1 Corinthians 1:18 (ESV)

The Wisdom That Seems Like Folly

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Paul confronts us with a startling truth: the message of Christ crucified appears as absolute foolishness to a watching world, yet for those being saved, it represents nothing less than God’s own power. This contrast isn’t merely academic—it reveals the fundamental divide between two kinds of people with two entirely different ways of understanding reality.

To those who are perishing, the cross makes no sense. Why would an all-powerful God allow His Son to die a criminal’s death? How could weakness defeat strength, suffering overcome sin, and death conquer death? The world’s wisdom seeks power, success, and self-advancement. A crucified Savior appears to contradict everything that seems logical about how God should work.

But Paul uses the present tense deliberately: “to us who are being saved.” This isn’t a one-time event but an ongoing work of God’s grace. As the Spirit opens our eyes, we begin to see what the world cannot: that the cross demonstrates both perfect justice and perfect mercy. God doesn’t simply overlook our sin—He bears its full weight in Christ. The apparent weakness of crucifixion becomes the ultimate display of divine power, conquering sin, death, and Satan himself.

This passage challenges us to examine our own hearts. Do we sometimes find ourselves embarrassed by the gospel’s simplicity? Are we tempted to make Christianity more palatable by emphasizing its practical benefits while downplaying the scandal of the cross? Paul reminds us that the gospel’s power lies precisely in its apparent foolishness.

Living in light of this truth means embracing a fundamentally different set of values. We find strength in admitting weakness, wisdom in confessing our foolishness, and life through dying to self. When the world mocks our faith as outdated or illogical, we need not be surprised or defensive. The very fact that the gospel seems foolish to natural human reasoning actually confirms its divine origin—no human would invent a religion centered on a crucified God.

Confessional Tie-in: “By this righteousness we are freed from all our sins and from all condemnation, and Christ has accomplished for us all that we must accomplish but cannot accomplish ourselves.” — Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 60

Prayer Prompt: Father, thank You that Your wisdom surpasses all human understanding and that in my weakness, Your power is made perfect. Help me never to be ashamed of the cross, but to glory in its apparent foolishness, knowing it is Your power unto salvation.

Go Deeper: Consider where you might be tempted to water down the gospel’s offense in order to make it more acceptable to others. Reflect on how the cross demonstrates both God’s justice and mercy simultaneously, and ask yourself: does this truth fill you with wonder and gratitude?
