Teach me to do your will, for you are my God! Let your good Spirit lead me on level ground!
Psalm 143:10 (ESV)

Led on Level Ground

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? David wrote Psalm 143 from a place of deep distress—enemies pursuing him, his spirit fainting within him, his heart appalled. Yet in the middle of this crisis, he doesn’t merely ask God to rescue him from trouble; he asks God to teach him obedience. “Teach me to do your will, for you are my God!” This is a remarkable request. David doesn’t claim to already know God’s will and simply need strength to do it. He asks to be taught—acknowledging that even a man after God’s own heart needs ongoing instruction in righteousness.

Notice the foundation of his request: “for you are my God.” David’s plea rests not on his own merit but on the covenant relationship God has established. Because God belongs to him and he belongs to God, David has confidence to ask for guidance. This mirrors the whole shape of the gospel—we do not obey in order to become God’s people; we obey because we already are.

The second half of the verse deepens this dependence. David doesn’t ask merely for information about God’s will; he asks for the Spirit’s leading. He knows that knowledge without power leaves us stranded. He needs to be led, actively, by a Spirit who is described as “good.” This reveals something essential about sanctification: it is not self-improvement but Spirit-empowered transformation. Left to ourselves, our hearts wander into pits and snares. We need someone to walk ahead of us onto level ground—stable, safe footing amid a treacherous world.

For the believer today, this verse offers both comfort and direction. When you feel uncertain about decisions, weary in obedience, or aware of your own tendency to stumble, this is your prayer too. You are not left to figure out holiness by sheer willpower. The same Spirit who indwells you is willing to teach and lead. Sanctification is not a solo project—it is a Spirit-led journey with a Father who delights to instruct his children.

Confessional Tie-in:

“The Spirit of Christ…doth continually furnish them with strength to believe and to do… whatsoever he requires of them.” — Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter 13.1 (adapted reference to sanctification)

Prayer Prompt:

Father, I confess that I often try to walk in my own strength and stumble into uneven, treacherous ground. Thank you for being my God and for sending your Spirit to teach and lead me. Guide my steps today onto the level ground of your will, and grant me a willing heart to follow.

Go Deeper:

Today, before making decisions—large or small—pause and ask the Spirit to lead you, rather than assuming you already know the way. Consider journaling one area of your life that feels like uneven ground right now, and specifically pray Psalm 143:10 over it. Ask yourself: Am I depending on the Spirit’s leading, or am I relying on my own understanding?
