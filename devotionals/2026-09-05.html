And Jesus came and said to them, “All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me. Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you. And behold, I am with you always, to the end of the age.”
Matthew 28:18-20 (ESV)

The King’s Commission

Title: All Authority, All Nations, All Days

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? These closing words of Matthew’s Gospel are spoken by a resurrected King, not a defeated teacher offering final wishes. The disciples had watched Jesus crucified; now they stood before him alive, and some, Matthew tells us honestly, still doubted. Into that mixture of worship and uncertainty, Jesus does not scold—he commissions.

Notice the foundation: “All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me.” Everything that follows rests on this claim. The Great Commission is not first a task believers perform but a declaration of who Christ is. He has been enthroned. The Father has placed all things under the Son’s rule, fulfilling what was promised long before—dominion given to one like a son of man, an everlasting kingdom that will not pass away.

Because Christ reigns, his people go. The command to make disciples is not an optional add-on for the especially zealous; it flows necessarily from Christ’s universal authority. If he rules all nations, then all nations must hear of him. Baptism marks entrance into this kingdom, uniting believers to the Father, Son, and Spirit. Teaching obedience to “all that I have commanded” reminds us that discipleship is lifelong—not a decision made once, but a life continually shaped by Christ’s word.

Then comes the promise that sustains everything: “I am with you always.” The disciples were being sent to a hostile world with no army, no political power, only a message and a promised presence. That presence remains the church’s only true strength today. We do not carry out this commission by our cleverness or courage, but by resting in the nearness of the risen King who has already secured the outcome.

This calls us to examine our own lives. Are we living as citizens of a kingdom whose King already reigns, or are we paralyzed by fear as though authority still hung in the balance? Whether at home, at work, or across the world, the mission remains: make disciples, trusting not in our own strength, but in his abiding presence.

Confessional Tie-in:

“Q. What is the chief end of man? A. Man’s chief end is to glorify God, and to enjoy him forever.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Q.1

Prayer Prompt:

Lord Jesus, I praise you as the King who reigns over heaven and earth, worthy of all authority and glory. Forgive me for the times I have shrunk back from your mission in fear or apathy, forgetting that you go with me. Thank you for your promised presence—strengthen me today to live and speak as one who belongs to your eternal kingdom.

Go Deeper:

Consider one relationship where you could intentionally speak of Christ this week—a neighbor, coworker, or family member. Reflect on this question: Do you approach evangelism and discipleship as a burden you carry alone, or as participation in the work of a King who has already won the victory? Spend time meditating on Christ’s promise, “I am with you always,” letting it steady any fear you carry into obedience.
