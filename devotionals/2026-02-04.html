[The LORD Is My Rock and My Fortress] To the choirmaster. A Psalm of David, the servant of the LORD, who addressed the words of this song to the LORD on the day when the LORD delivered him from the hand of all his enemies, and from the hand of Saul. He said: I love you, O LORD, my strength. The LORD is my rock and my fortress and my deliverer, my God, my rock, in whom I take refuge, my shield, and the horn of my salvation, my stronghold.
Psalm 18:1-2 (ESV)

The Unshakeable Foundation

Reflection

What does this psalm teach us about God’s character and our relationship with Him? David opens his heart with a declaration that echoes through the ages: “I love you, O LORD, my strength.” These words weren’t spoken in a moment of ease, but in the aftermath of deliverance from his enemies, including King Saul who had relentlessly pursued him.

Notice how David describes God through a cascade of metaphors—rock, fortress, deliverer, shield, horn of salvation, stronghold. Each image builds upon the previous one, painting a picture of absolute security and protection. A rock provides an unshakeable foundation; a fortress offers comprehensive defense; a deliverer acts with power to rescue. These aren’t merely poetic flourishes but theological truths about God’s unchanging nature.

The repetition of “rock” is particularly striking. In David’s world, rocks and cliffs provided refuge from enemies and storms. They were permanent fixtures in a landscape of shifting sands and changing circumstances. When David calls God his rock twice in these verses, he’s declaring that God alone provides the stability and security that human hearts desperately crave.

This psalm reveals a profound truth about genuine faith: it grows strongest not in comfort, but through trials. David’s love for God wasn’t theoretical—it was forged in the crucible of real danger, real fear, and real deliverance. His enemies had been formidable, but God had proven more powerful. His circumstances had been threatening, but God had been his refuge.

For believers today, these words offer both comfort and challenge. When anxiety threatens to overwhelm us, when relationships fracture, when health fails, or when the future seems uncertain, we’re reminded that our foundation isn’t built on shifting circumstances but on the unchanging character of God. Yet this passage also challenges us to examine where we truly place our trust. Do we run first to our own resources, to other people, or to our Rock?

The beauty of this psalm is that it doesn’t promise the absence of enemies or trials. Instead, it promises the presence of an unshakeable God who serves as our strength, protection, and ultimate security. In a world of constant change, He remains our rock.

Confessional Tie-in

“God is a Spirit, infinite, eternal, and unchangeable, in his being, wisdom, power, holiness, justice, goodness, and truth.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Question 4

Prayer Prompt

Lord, You are my rock and fortress when everything around me seems uncertain. Help me to trust in Your unchanging character rather than my changing circumstances. Thank You for being my refuge and strength, and teach me to run to You first in every trial.

Go Deeper

Consider the areas of your life where you tend to seek security apart from God. How might viewing Him as your rock and fortress change your response to current challenges? Spend time meditating on each metaphor David uses, asking how that particular aspect of God’s character speaks to your present needs.
