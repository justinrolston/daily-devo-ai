[I Love the LORD] I love the LORD, because he has heard my voice and my pleas for mercy. Because he inclined his ear to me, therefore I will call on him as long as I live.
Psalm 116:1-2 (ESV)

A Love Born of Being Heard

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? The psalmist begins not with a theological statement but with a personal confession: “I love the LORD.” This love is not abstract sentiment—it flows from a specific experience. God heard his voice. God heard his pleas for mercy. The Hebrew word for “inclined” pictures God bending down, leaning close, as a parent lowers their ear to catch the whispered words of a child. This is stunning intimacy from the sovereign Lord of heaven and earth.

Psalm 116 emerges from real anguish—later verses describe the psalmist facing death itself, entangled in sorrow (v. 3). Yet his response to affliction is not merely relief but love. Why? Because in his distress, he discovered that God actually listens to sinners who cry for mercy.

This is the gospel in miniature. We do not love God first and then earn His attention. Rather, we are helpless, crying out from the depths of our need, undeserving of any response—and God bends down anyway. This is grace: the initiative always belongs to Him. Our love for God is always a response to His prior mercy toward us (1 John 4:19).

For believers today, this truth transforms our prayer life. Perhaps you feel your prayers vanish into silence, unheard and unanswered. This psalm insists otherwise—the God who inclined His ear to an ancient sufferer still bends low to hear His children. Through Christ, our great High Priest, we have even greater confidence that we are heard (Hebrews 4:14-16).

Notice too the resolution born from this experience: “therefore I will call on him as long as I live.” Genuine encounters with God’s mercy do not lead to occasional gratitude but to a lifelong pattern of dependent prayer. The Christian life is not learning to need God less, but discovering fresh reasons to call on Him more.

Confessional Tie-in:

“What is prayer? Prayer is an offering up of our desires unto God, for things agreeable to his will, in the name of Christ, with confession of our sins, and thankful acknowledgment of his mercies.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Q. 98

Prayer Prompt:

Lord, I confess I often doubt whether You truly hear me when I pray. Thank You for bending down to listen to sinners, and for hearing me fully through the merits of Christ. Teach me to call on You not just in crisis, but as the daily pattern of my life.

Go Deeper:

Read all of Psalm 116 this week, noting each specific mercy the psalmist recalls. Consider keeping a prayer journal for seven days, recording not just your requests but the ways you sense God has “inclined His ear” to you. Ask yourself: Does my love for God flow from remembering His mercies, or have I forgotten what He has already done?
