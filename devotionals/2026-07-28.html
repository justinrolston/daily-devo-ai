July 28, 2026

I press on toward the goal for the prize of the upward call of God in Christ Jesus.
Philippians 3:14 (ESV)

Running Toward Home

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Paul writes these words from a place of remarkable spiritual maturity, yet he does not describe himself as having arrived. Just verses earlier, he counts his impressive religious credentials as rubbish compared to knowing Christ (3:8). Now he pictures himself as a runner in a race, straining forward, eyes fixed on the finish line. The Greek word for “press on” carries the sense of vigorous, determined pursuit—this is not passive waiting but active, wholehearted effort.

Yet notice what Paul is pursuing: not self-improvement or personal achievement, but “the prize of the upward call of God in Christ Jesus.” The goal is not something Paul earns by his running—it is something God has already called him toward. This is the beautiful tension of the Christian life: we are fully secure in Christ’s finished work, and yet we are called to strain forward in ongoing pursuit of him. Our justification is complete and unshakeable; our sanctification is a lifelong race.

This matters for believers who feel the weight of their remaining sin. Perhaps you feel like you should be further along by now—more patient, more faithful in prayer, less prone to the same old temptations. Paul, writing near the end of his ministry, still describes himself as pressing on, not as one who has already obtained perfection (3:12). The Christian life is not measured by arrival but by direction. Are you facing forward? Are you still running, even if you stumble?

The call is “upward”—it originates from God and pulls us toward him. We do not run toward a vague goal but toward a Person, the same Christ who took hold of us (3:12). Our effort in sanctification is never independent of grace; it is grace working in us, enabling us to will and to work for his good pleasure (Philippians 2:13). We run because we have already been caught by Christ, not in order to be caught by him.

Confessional Tie-in:

“Sanctification is the work of God’s free grace, whereby we are renewed in the whole man after the image of God, and are enabled more and more to die unto sin, and live unto righteousness.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Q. 35

Prayer Prompt:

Lord, thank you for calling me upward to yourself through Christ, and for holding me secure even when my progress feels slow. Forgive me for the seasons I have grown weary or complacent in this race. By your Spirit, renew my strength today and fix my eyes on Jesus, the goal and prize of my pursuit.

Go Deeper:

Consider one area where you have grown discouraged in your walk with Christ. Rather than measuring yourself against perfection, ask: am I still facing forward, still pressing on? Read Hebrews 12:1-2 alongside this passage and reflect on how the “cloud of witnesses” and Christ’s own endurance encourage your race today.
