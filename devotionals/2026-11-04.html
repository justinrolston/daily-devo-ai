...who has made us sufficient to be ministers of a new covenant, not of the letter but of the Spirit. For the letter kills, but the Spirit gives life.
2 Corinthians 3:6 (ESV)

When Rules Run Out of Power

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Paul is defending his ministry to a Corinthian church that had been seduced by teachers demanding strict adherence to the law as the path to spiritual legitimacy. Paul draws a sharp contrast: the old covenant, given through Moses and written on tablets of stone, could command but could not transform. It exposed sin and pronounced judgment—it “kills.” The new covenant, however, operates by the Spirit, who writes God’s law not on stone but on human hearts, producing real, living obedience from the inside out.

This is not a rejection of Scripture’s authority—Paul himself calls it inspired and useful. Rather, he’s addressing what the law, by itself, can accomplish in a fallen heart. The law reveals God’s holiness and our failure to meet it. It diagnoses the disease but offers no cure. Left to ourselves, we are spiritually dead, unable to produce the righteousness God requires. This is the sobering reality of our condition apart from grace.

But here is the gospel: God does not leave us with mere instruction. He sends His Spirit to accomplish what the law could never do—to give life to those who were dead in sin. This is the miracle at the center of the new covenant. Our sufficiency, Paul says, is not in ourselves but comes from God himself.

Consider your own spiritual life. Do you find yourself relying on checklists, performance, or willpower to feel close to God? The letter alone will always condemn you, because none of us keeps it perfectly. But if you are in Christ, the same Spirit who raised Jesus from the dead dwells in you, writing God’s truth on your heart and producing fruit that mere rule-keeping never could. Sanctification is not gritting your teeth harder—it is walking daily in dependence on the Spirit who empowers obedience that flows from love, not fear.

Confessional Tie-in:

“What does God require of us that we may escape this misery? … True faith is not only a knowledge and conviction… but also a deep-rooted assurance, created in me by the Holy Spirit through the gospel.” — Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 21

Prayer Prompt:

Lord, I confess how often I try to earn what you have already freely given through your Spirit. Thank you for not leaving me to the condemnation of the law, but for sending your Spirit to give me new life and write your truth upon my heart. Teach me today to walk in dependence on your Spirit rather than my own strength.

Go Deeper:

Notice one area today where you’ve been relying on self-effort rather than the Spirit’s power—perhaps in resisting a particular sin or loving a difficult person. Ask the Spirit specifically for help in that moment, trusting he is at work in you. For further reflection, read Ezekiel 36:26-27, where God promises the very heart-transformation Paul describes here.
