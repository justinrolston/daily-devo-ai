The Wisdom of Counting Days

Psalm 90:12 (ESV)
“So teach us to number our days that we may get a heart of wisdom.”

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Moses, writing this ancient psalm, confronts us with a profound prayer request: teach us to count our days wisely. But notice—he doesn’t ask us to teach ourselves. He pleads with God to be our instructor, recognizing that only divine teaching can transform our perspective on time and mortality.

The Hebrew word for “number” suggests more than mere counting. It means to assign significance, to weigh carefully. Moses isn’t asking for mathematical skills but for spiritual insight into the true value of our brief earthly sojourn. In the broader context of Psalm 90, he has just reminded us that our days are “like grass” and that “we are brought to an end by your anger.” Human life, apart from God’s grace, is both fleeting and fallen.

This sobering reality should drive us not to despair but to wisdom. The “heart of wisdom” Moses seeks is not intellectual prowess but the deep, soul-level understanding that reorients our entire lives around eternal realities. When we truly grasp the brevity of our days and the certainty of standing before our Creator, everything changes. Our priorities shift, our relationships deepen, and our pursuit of temporal pleasures gives way to storing treasures in heaven.

For believers, numbering our days means recognizing each one as a gracious gift from our sovereign God. We don’t approach our mortality with the fear of those without hope, but neither do we live carelessly as if this life is all there is. Instead, we live with holy urgency—making the most of every opportunity to glorify God, serve others, and grow in Christlikeness.

This wisdom transforms how we spend our time, energy, and resources. It helps us distinguish between what is merely urgent and what is truly important. It compels us to forgive quickly, love deeply, and speak truthfully because we understand that our days are numbered by a loving Father who works all things according to His perfect will.

The path to this wisdom begins with acknowledging our desperate need for God’s teaching. Left to ourselves, we live as if we have unlimited tomorrows. But when the Spirit opens our eyes to see our days as God sees them, we discover the profound freedom that comes from living in light of eternity.

Confessional Tie-in:

“Man’s chief end is to glorify God, and to enjoy him forever.”
— Westminster Shorter Catechism, Question 1

Prayer Prompt:

Father, teach me to see my days through Your eyes. Help me live with both urgency and peace, knowing that my times are in Your hands. Grant me wisdom to invest my brief life in what truly matters for eternity.

Go Deeper:

Consider keeping a simple daily journal, asking yourself each evening: “How did I use this gift of another day for God’s glory?” Reflect on Jesus’ own words about the shortness of the day and the need to work while there is still time (John 9:4).
