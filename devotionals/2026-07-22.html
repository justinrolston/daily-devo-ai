He said to them, “But who do you say that I am?” Simon Peter replied, “You are the Christ, the Son of the living God.”
Matthew 16:15-16 (ESV)

The Question That Demands an Answer

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Jesus stands with His disciples near Caesarea Philippi, a region saturated with pagan temples and idol worship, and asks a question that echoes through every generation: “Who do you say that I am?” Notice the shift Jesus makes. He first asks what others are saying—the popular opinions, the crowd’s theories. Then He turns the question inward, personal and unavoidable: “But who do you say that I am?”

Simon Peter’s answer is remarkable not because of his own insight, but because of what it reveals. He confesses, “You are the Christ, the Son of the living God.” This is no small title. Peter identifies Jesus as the long-promised Messiah and as God Himself, sharing the very nature of the living God in contrast to the lifeless idols surrounding them. Jesus later affirms that this truth was not discovered through human reasoning but revealed by the Father (v. 17)—a truth that anticipates the reality that no one comes to genuine faith apart from God’s gracious work in the heart.

This question refuses to remain in the first century. Every person who hears the name of Jesus must eventually answer it. Culture offers its own theories—Jesus as a moral teacher, a wise prophet, a historical curiosity. But these answers, however respectful, fall short of the truth Peter confessed. Our salvation, our worship, and our daily walk hinge entirely on rightly answering this question.

For the believer, this isn’t a one-time confession but a daily reorientation. Do we live as though Jesus is merely a helpful example, or do we approach Him as the sovereign Christ and divine Son before whom every knee will bow? Our anxieties, ambitions, and affections reveal what we truly believe about who He is. When trials come, when temptation whispers, when comfort seduces us toward lesser things—our functional answer to this question shapes everything.

Peter’s confession invites us to examine our own hearts today. Not merely what we say Jesus is, but what our lives declare Him to be.

Confessional Tie-in:

“Q: What is your only comfort in life and death? A: That I am not my own, but belong—body and soul, in life and in death—to my faithful Savior, Jesus Christ, who at the cost of his own blood has fully paid for all my sins and has completely freed me from the dominion of the devil.” — Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 1

Prayer Prompt:

Father, I confess that I often answer the question of who Jesus is with my lips more readily than with my life. Thank You for revealing the truth of Christ to me by Your grace alone, not by my own wisdom. Help me today to live in light of who Jesus truly is—my Savior, my Lord, and my God.

Go Deeper:

Spend a few moments today writing your own answer to Jesus’s question in your own words—not a memorized creed, but a personal confession born from Scripture and experience. Consider: does your response to stress, decision-making, or temptation this week reflect a belief in Jesus as sovereign Lord and divine Son? Read Colossians 1:15-20 for a fuller picture of who this Christ truly is.
