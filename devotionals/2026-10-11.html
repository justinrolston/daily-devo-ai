[My Soul Waits for God Alone] To the choirmaster: according to Jeduthun. A Psalm of David. For God alone my soul waits in silence; from him comes my salvation.
Psalm 62:1 (ESV)

Silence That Speaks

Title: Silence That Speaks

Reflection

What does this text teach us? David opens this psalm with a striking posture: his soul waits for God alone, and it waits in silence. This is not the silence of emptiness or despair, but the quiet confidence of a heart that has stopped striving. The Hebrew word for “waits” carries the idea of resting or being still, like a weaned child leaning against its mother. David wasn’t naturally silent—elsewhere in Scripture he cries out, laments, and pleads with raw emotion. But here, surrounded by enemies who sought to topple him “like a leaning wall” (v. 3), he has arrived at a settled trust: salvation comes from God alone, so striving alone is futile.

This truth cuts against the grain of our instincts. We are wired to fix, to control, to find some leverage over our circumstances. Yet Scripture consistently teaches that salvation—whether from physical danger, spiritual bondage, or sin itself—is the Lord’s work from start to finish. We did not initiate our rescue from sin, and we cannot sustain it by our own effort. Just as David could not silence his enemies through human strength, we cannot manufacture peace, righteousness, or security through our own striving. It comes from God alone.

This is the heartbeat of the gospel: salvation is not a cooperative project where we contribute some percentage and God supplies the rest. It flows entirely from His grace, accomplished fully in Christ, who alone bore our sin and alone secured our redemption. Our role is not to add to this but to rest in it—to wait, trust, and be still.

For the anxious soul, this is profound comfort: your security does not depend on your performance. For the restless soul, constantly scheming for control, this is a gentle rebuke: stop grasping and start waiting. Daily life offers endless opportunities to practice this silence—in financial pressure, relational conflict, health struggles, or vocational uncertainty. The invitation is the same: still your soul before the God who alone saves.

Confessional Tie-in

“What is the chief end of man? Man’s chief end is to glorify God, and to enjoy him forever.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Q&A 1

Prayer Prompt

Lord, forgive me for the ways I strive to save myself rather than resting in Your finished work. Thank You that my salvation depends on Your faithfulness, not my performance. Quiet my anxious heart today, and teach me to wait on You alone.

Go Deeper

Today, identify one area where you’ve been “leaning on a wall” of your own making—a source of security that isn’t God. Spend five minutes in silence, not asking for anything, but simply resting in the truth that your salvation is secure in Christ. Consider reading Psalm 62 in its entirety to see how David works out this trust amid real adversity.
