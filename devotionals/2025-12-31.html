December 31, 2025

Thus says the LORD, who makes a way in the sea, a path in the mighty waters, “Remember not the former things, nor consider the things of old. Behold, I am doing a new thing; now it springs forth, do you not perceive it? I will make a way in the wilderness and rivers in the desert.
Isaiah 43:16, 18-19 (ESV)

God Makes a Way When There Seems to Be No Way

Reflection

What does this magnificent passage from Isaiah teach us? God declares Himself as the one “who makes a way in the sea, a path in the mighty waters.” This isn’t mere poetry—it’s a declaration of divine power rooted in historical reality. The Israelites knew firsthand what it meant for God to make a way through impossible circumstances. He had literally parted the Red Sea, creating dry ground where there should have been only crushing waves and certain death.

But notice how God’s promise extends beyond past miracles. He commands His people to “remember not the former things, nor consider the things of old.” This isn’t a call to forget God’s faithfulness, but rather to lift our eyes from past deliverances to see His present and future work. God says, “Behold, I am doing a new thing; now it springs forth, do you not perceive it?”

The Hebrew word for “springs forth” suggests something bursting into bloom, like a plant pushing through hard ground. God’s new work isn’t merely replacing the old—it’s surpassing it. Where He once made a path through water, now He promises to “make a way in the wilderness and rivers in the desert.” These are places of barrenness and death, yet God transforms them into highways of hope and sources of life.

For believers today, this passage speaks directly to our seasons of impossibility. Perhaps you’re facing a wilderness of broken relationships, a desert of financial hardship, or the mighty waters of illness or loss. The same God who delivered Israel is actively working in your circumstances right now. His power isn’t limited by what seems impossible to us.

This promise finds its ultimate fulfillment in Christ, who made the ultimate way through the wilderness of God’s wrath and the desert of our sin. Through His death and resurrection, Jesus opened a new and living way into the presence of God. The gospel itself is God’s supreme demonstration that He makes ways where there are no ways.

When we feel trapped by our circumstances, we can trust that our sovereign God is already preparing paths we cannot yet see. His timing is perfect, His methods are wise, and His purposes will not be thwarted. The question isn’t whether God can make a way—it’s whether we have eyes to perceive the new thing He’s doing.

Confessional Tie-in

“God’s works of providence are his most holy, wise, and powerful preserving and governing all his creatures, and all their actions.” — Westminster Confession of Faith 5.1

Prayer Prompt

Father, when I can only see obstacles and dead ends, open my eyes to perceive the new thing You are doing in my life. Help me trust in Your power to make ways in my wilderness and rivers in my desert, knowing that Your purposes for me are good.

Go Deeper

Identify one “impossible” situation in your life and specifically pray for God to make a way, trusting His wisdom in how and when He acts. Reflect on how Jesus is the ultimate “way” God made for your salvation, and let that fuel your confidence in His present work in your circumstances.
