[The Whole Armor of God] Finally, be strong in the Lord and in the strength of his might. Put on the whole armor of God, that you may be able to stand against the schemes of the devil.
Ephesians 6:10-11 (ESV)

Standing Strong: Our Need for Divine Armor

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Paul’s concluding charge to the Ephesians reveals a sobering reality: the Christian life is warfare. After five chapters of glorious gospel truth about our identity in Christ, Paul shifts to the battlefield. His command “be strong in the Lord” uses the passive voice in Greek, indicating this is not self-generated strength but power received from God. We cannot manufacture the strength needed for spiritual battle—it must come from “the strength of his might.”

The phrase “whole armor of God” emphasizes completeness. Partial protection leaves us vulnerable to Satan’s “schemes”—a word describing methodical, strategic attacks rather than random temptations. The devil is not merely an abstract force of evil but a personal adversary who studies our weaknesses and plans his assault accordingly. This reality should humble us and drive us to dependence upon God’s provision.

Paul’s military imagery would have resonated with first-century readers familiar with Roman soldiers. Yet this armor is not physical but spiritual, provided by God himself. The armor’s purpose is clear: “that you may be able to stand.” Standing implies both defensive readiness and victorious endurance. We’re not called to defeat the devil—Christ has already accomplished that victory—but to maintain our ground through His power.

This passage beautifully illustrates our complete dependence on God’s grace. We cannot fight spiritual battles with fleshly weapons. Our own wisdom, willpower, or moral effort proves insufficient against supernatural opposition. Just as we depend entirely on Christ for justification, we must rely completely on His strength for sanctification and spiritual warfare.

The timing of this command matters. Paul places it after establishing our secure position in Christ. We fight from victory, not for victory. Our standing in the heavenly places (Ephesians 2:6) provides the foundation for standing against the enemy. When we understand who we are in Christ—blessed, chosen, adopted, sealed—we can confidently face spiritual opposition knowing God has already equipped us for triumph.

Every believer faces this warfare. There are no exemptions or furloughs in the Christian life. Yet God’s faithfulness ensures that His armor never fails when properly worn through faith, prayer, and dependence on His Word.

Confessional Tie-in: “We acknowledge, then, that God is perfectly merciful and also very just; and this requires us to flee for refuge to Jesus Christ and to seek in him all that is lacking in us.” — Belgic Confession, Article 24

Prayer Prompt: Lord, I confess my weakness in the face of spiritual battle. Clothe me today with Your armor and strengthen me with Your might. Help me to stand firm in the victory You have already won through Christ.

Go Deeper: Examine your daily spiritual habits—are you regularly “putting on” God’s armor through prayer, Scripture reading, and fellowship? Consider what specific “schemes” you face most often and how God’s strength, not your willpower, provides the answer.
