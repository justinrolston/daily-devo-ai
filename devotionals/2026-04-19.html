1 Corinthians 15:20-22 (ESV)

The First and the Promise

1 Corinthians 15:20-22 (ESV) “But in fact Christ has been raised from the dead, the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep. For as by a man came death, by a man has come also the resurrection of the dead. For as in Adam all die, so also in Christ shall all be made alive.”

Reflection:

What does this text teach us? Paul presents Christ’s resurrection not as an isolated miracle, but as the guarantee of our own future resurrection. The term “firstfruits” comes from Israel’s harvest festivals, where the first portion of the crop was offered to God as a promise that the full harvest would follow. Christ’s resurrection is our firstfruits—the certain pledge that all who belong to Him will likewise be raised.

Paul draws a profound parallel between two representative men: Adam and Christ. Through Adam’s disobedience, death entered the human race. Every person born bears the consequences of Adam’s fall—we inherit both his guilt and his corrupted nature. Death reigns over all humanity because we are “in Adam.” This isn’t merely about physical death, but spiritual separation from God that affects every aspect of our being.

But God has provided a second Adam. Just as Adam’s disobedience brought condemnation to all his descendants, Christ’s perfect obedience and sacrificial death brings life to all who are united to Him by faith. The phrase “in Christ” describes our new spiritual identity. When we trust in Christ, we are transferred from Adam’s dying family into Christ’s living family.

This truth transforms how we face death and suffering. When believers die, Paul says they have “fallen asleep”—a temporary rest before the morning of resurrection. Death has lost its sting because Christ has conquered it. The grave cannot hold those who belong to the risen Savior.

Yet this hope isn’t merely about the distant future. Being “in Christ” means His resurrection life is already at work within us. The same power that raised Jesus from the dead is sanctifying us daily, making us more like Him. Every small victory over sin, every moment of spiritual growth, every act of sacrificial love reflects the reality that resurrection life flows through us now.

This passage calls us to live with resurrection hope. Our sufferings are temporary, our struggles are not permanent, and our God specializes in bringing life from death.

Confessional Tie-in: “The bodies of the dead shall be raised up incorruptible, and shall be reunited to their souls forever.” — Belgic Confession, Article 37

Prayer Prompt: Father, thank You that Christ’s resurrection guarantees my own. Help me live today with the confidence that Your resurrection power is already at work in me, transforming my heart and securing my eternal hope.

Go Deeper: Consider areas where you need resurrection hope today—relationships that seem dead, habits that feel unchangeable, or circumstances that appear hopeless. How does knowing you are “in Christ” rather than “in Adam” change your perspective on these challenges?
