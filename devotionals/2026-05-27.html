Finishing the Course That Matters Most

Acts 20:24 (ESV) “But I do not account my life of any value nor as precious to myself, if only I may finish my course and the ministry that I received from the Lord Jesus, to testify to the gospel of the grace of God.”

Reflection:

What does this remarkable statement from the apostle Paul teach us about true priorities? Speaking to the Ephesian elders, Paul reveals a heart completely captured by Christ’s mission. He doesn’t consider his life worthless—rather, he finds its worth entirely in fulfilling God’s calling.

Paul’s words expose our natural tendency to cling to life as if it belonged to us. We instinctively protect our comfort, reputation, and safety. Yet Paul demonstrates the radical reorientation that occurs when we truly understand the gospel. His life had been purchased by Christ, and therefore belonged entirely to his Savior’s purposes.

Notice what drove Paul’s passion: “the gospel of the grace of God.” This wasn’t mere duty or religious obligation. Paul had been overwhelmed by undeserved grace—God’s favor toward rebels like himself. Having received such mercy, how could he withhold this good news from others? The grace that saved him compelled him to spend himself sharing it.

Paul speaks of “my course” and “the ministry that I received from the Lord Jesus.” God doesn’t call every believer to apostolic ministry, but He does give each of us a unique course to run. Whether as parents, employees, neighbors, or church members, we all have received a ministry—opportunities to testify to God’s grace in word and deed.

This perspective transforms how we view hardship. Paul wasn’t naive about suffering; he knew imprisonment and possibly death awaited him. Yet finishing his course mattered more than preserving his life. When we grasp that our true identity and purpose come from Christ, even our trials become opportunities to demonstrate the gospel’s power.

Paul’s example challenges our modern preoccupation with self-preservation and personal fulfillment. The Christian life isn’t primarily about finding ourselves or maximizing our potential—it’s about faithfully completing the mission God has given us. This doesn’t diminish life’s value but reveals its true worth: lives spent for the gospel bear eternal significance.

Confessional Tie-in: “Man’s chief end is to glorify God, and to enjoy him forever.” — Westminster Shorter Catechism, Question 1

Prayer Prompt: Lord, help me hold my life with open hands, finding my worth not in self-preservation but in faithful service to Your gospel. Grant me Paul’s heart to value finishing Your course for my life above my own comfort and safety.

Go Deeper: Consider what specific “course” God has set before you in this season. How might viewing your current circumstances—both joyful and difficult—as opportunities to testify to God’s grace change your perspective today? Reflect on how the gospel that saved you can flow through your ordinary interactions this week.
